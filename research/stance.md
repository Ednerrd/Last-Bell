## Part 1. What the repo already says about how a boxer STANDS (for the 3D boxer)

Read-only dig, Oct 23 2026. Short version: the research is about fight IQ, not anatomy. It never gives a stance
in degrees, inches or weight percentages. What exists is qualitative (bladed / square / crouched / tall / flat /
bouncy), guard descriptions, and a handful of amateur-study numbers on fatigue. Sources are named per bullet.
Tags: [S]=sourced in the file, [B]=blog/forum/low grade, [est]=the note-writer's own guess, [Ed]=Ed's call/photos.

### 0. GAPS FIRST (what the research does NOT cover)
- No foot width in inches or vs shoulders. No front-back foot spacing. No toe angles (front or rear).
- No bladed angle in degrees anywhere. "Bladed" and "square" are used only as opposites; "45 degrees" appears nowhere.
- No knee-bend angle, no weight split % (front/back), no hip/shoulder line angles, no torso-lean degrees.
- No chin-tuck measure, no elbow angles. Hand heights are words only (cheeks, temples, waist, belly).
- No southpaw body notes beyond "mirror, open stance, liver faces the rear hand" (mind_combos.md, mind_southpaw.md are about matchups).
- No hurt-posture description beyond legs/hands/looking down. No "tired posture" beyond hands drop, bounce dies, flat feet.
- Nothing on head height as a fraction of standing height, or crouch depth per style, in numbers.
- Only real numbers: amateur fatigue timings (tells.md), a pull-back of ~4 in, jab reach ~34-37 in, a few reach/height stats (section 6).
- So the 3D build must invent the base pose from general boxing knowledge; the research only supplies the DIFFERENCES between
  styles/guards and the movement behaviour.

### 1. Base stance (generic)
- Feet staggered, chin down, hands up = step 1 of the teaching ladder; fixes the two classic faults, squaring up and crossed feet. (mind_learn.md 1b, snippets: gymdesk, Evolve fundamentals)
- Footwork rule: step-drag, never cross the feet. (mind_learn.md 1b; boxinglife.md l80 Roach)
- Roach (boxinglife.md l80): balance first, never cross feet, stance NOT too narrow (needs a lateral base) and NOT square;
  knees bent so a slip is one move.
- Jab safety (jabsafe.md l32, Expert Boxing [S]): bent knees, weight centred; leaning forward on the jab makes you counterable.
- Bladed = the lead shoulder toward the opponent, chest not facing him; square = chest faces him (bigger target, weaker rear hand,
  easy to walk through; mind_learn.md table, coaching blogs [B]). A squared man's rear hand is closer to the target (skillr.md high guard, Duran).
- Counter-man's centred balance: men who lean over the front foot when punching are easy to counter; men who stay centred can roll back
  (boxinglife.md l28, probably Mayweather talk). Do not lean over the front knee: the uppercut comes from the blind spot (boxinglife l83).
- Crossed/lined-up feet = off balance on any head movement, rear hand loses its base (mind_learn.md table, trainer quote [B]).
- Power starts at the feet: back foot drives, hips twist; same sync keeps posture when slipping and rolling (skillr.md s13, Inoue).
- Inoue (skillr.md s13): wide and STABLE base, adapts (squarer for a high guard, more bladed when backing up in a long/Philly guard).
- Canelo (corners_video.md l249 Bivol fight): tight high guard and FLAT, PLANTED feet for power.
- Punch weight shift (combat_research.md l68): the hip travels ~10-20% of stance width toward the target on a cross, then returns.
  (This is the only "% of stance width" in the repo; it is for 2D but fits 3D.)
- Wide legs: Spence reaches and comes up short because his legs are too wide (coach_c.md l235 BoMac; corners.md l301 "reaching, legs too wide"). So "wide base" = reach flaw, not a stable strength, there.
- Stance NOT to copy: Charlo's squared stance "works for Jermall, may not work for somebody else" (coach_b.md l231, Shields).
- The 3D build already uses (PLAN.md Round 1/2 notes): soft knees, heel down, shorter stance, fists forward after Ed called the first try
  a stiff "crane-kick" pose. Step-drag with width kept; feet crossing under 1% of frames.

### 2. By style (tall/crouched, wide/narrow, bouncy/flat, weight)
- Out-boxer (Ali, Leonard, Pep, Plant): up on the toes, light quick bounce, long range, circles. (combat_research l70; PLAN.md RHY; substyles.md)
  - Tall man's choice (skillr.md s15, [S]): TALL on the toes (pull-backs, bounce, easier on legs, hard to hit a low moving target)
    vs CROUCHED and wide at his level (no punching down, duck under shots, but tiring, he can be circled). Both valid.
  - Tall men lean back / pull the head straight back instead of moving it (size.md l78, l229, Vick MMA study [S]).
  - Long-guard jabbers (Klitschko, Fury, Joshua, Bivol): lead hand posted out, rear hand at chin, BLADED; half-step back after the jab. (skillr.md l73, longguard.md)
  - Hearns (skillr.md l146): 6'2", 78 in reach, NARROW stance, bounces, lead hand at the waist.
  - Leonard (skillr.md l159): NARROW stance, tall, SQUARE-ish, head off the centre line, open guard at SHOULDER height; stands tall, cannot duck; squares up when moving sideways (a flaw).
  - Stevenson (fighters_a l13): southpaw Philly shell, lead hand extended, stands just outside reach.
- Boxer-puncher: any range; Bivol tall high guard gloves at temples, elbows in; Usyk southpaw, long active lead hand, constant SMALL steps, head off line. (fighters_a, fighters_b)
  - Crawford: mid-high guard, stays on the end of reach, switches stance (see 4).
- Counter-puncher: "low, still, almost no bob" (combat_research l70); "still but loose" (PLAN.md Bodies section).
  - Shell/roll men torso shifts forward/back and side to side in the pocket (Mayweather, fighters_b l195).
  - Martinez/Davis/Pep/Roy Jones trap-setters: low open guard keeps shoulders loose; leans on the front foot to LOOK vulnerable; rotates off the centre line (substyles.md).
  - Locche-type hands-down stillness: square, chin high, barely blocks (explainers.md l49).
- Slugger: flat-footed and heavy (PLAN.md Bodies section); "planted feet" (Foreman, Ruiz, GGG; explainers.md l103); "stands in range, trades" (explainers l35).
  - Wilder bounces on the spot rather than moving in/out (coach_c.md l178, Davison; also an easy-flaw tag l280).
  - One-shot puncher plants the feet, few punches (substyles.md).
  - Benavidez: "straight up", high guard, fast hands but slow FLAT feet (fighters_a l100).
  - Canelo: flat-footed steady forward walk, head off centre line on entry, little side to side (fighters_b l101).
  - Wilder/Joshua-type heavies: Joshua tall high guard, crouch in close (fighters_f l28); Wilder UPRIGHT, mid range, little head movement (fighters_f l38).
- Swarmer/pressure: low crouch + high tight guard (Nick Ball: "very low crouched stance, a spring", changes level before every punch, fighters_d l32 + skillr s16);
  Ramirez-type "short squat crouched pressure" (fighters_d l32); heavy "steady shuffle" rhythm (combat_research l70);
  "bobs and weaves" (PLAN.md Bodies); Hagler: solid high guard, chin down, weight a BIT FORWARD (skillr l154);
  Tyson/Frazier peekaboo and cross-guard men are CROUCHED with weight on the FRONT foot (skillr l69).
  Crowding infighter (Marciano, Frazier, LaMotta) crouches (substyles.md).
  Tank/Ortiz-type square stance when he charges: wide and square, walks onto right hands (fighters_c l55-60).
  Weight back is the opposite: loaded stance = weight on the back foot, "like a wolf stalking" (size.md l298; dynamic.md l58 feint to bring him in).
- Awkward / off-balance (Navarrete, Hamed): throws while leaning/reaching/drifting, no clean resets, exaggerated body movement, odd angles, unreadable rhythm (explainers.md l46, substyles.md).
  Fury (fighters_f l17): long range, hands LOW, upper-body feints and pull-back, switches to southpaw.
- Switch-hitter (Crawford, Hagler, Hamed, Ennis): flips stance mid-fight/mid-combo; switching up close costs balance (toprank.md l16, l49 Kambosos "off balance"); half-switches end up square (Hagler, skillr l156).

### 3. By guard
All six game guards: standard, high, peekaboo, philly, cross, handslow (CLAUDE.md). The guard words are in skillr.md s3; PLAN.md has Ed's cross-arm.
- Standard / high guard: gloves at cheeks/temples, elbows in; close gloves for straights, open for hooks; small shifts up/down for head/body.
  Most offense of any guard (squarer, rear hand closer). Costs: bigger target, narrower vision, more static. (skillr.md l70; Bivol fighters_b l51 temples + elbows in; Canelo "high tight")
  Plain standard is "mid-high guard, hands lowish (est.)" for some (Crawford fighters_b l13; Spence-type fighters_c l107).
- Peekaboo (Tyson, Patterson, Frazier, Paz, Bradley): gloves at the CHEEKS, elbows tight, constant side-to-side head movement, bob and weave; crouching, high and tight
  (fighters_b l203, fighters_c l75 "crouching, high and tight", substyles.md). Heavy forward walk, rises into uppercuts/hooks off the duck.
- Philly shell (Mayweather, Toney, Whitaker, Stevenson; Inoue when backing up): BLADED; lead arm lies across the belly/body, lead shoulder HIGH, chin tucked behind the lead shoulder, rear hand at the cheek.
  (skillr l71; fighters_b l195). Body covered, head open; rolls straight punches off the shoulder; torso shifts forward/back/side. Uppercuts mostly don't work vs it because he's bladed.
  Elbows stay down by the ribs, never winged out (Ed, PLAN.md Guard reference).
- Cross-arm (Norton, Moore, Frazier, late Foreman, Holyfield): [Ed, from his photos] BOTH forearms horizontal and STACKED across the body: rear forearm on TOP across the chest just under the chin,
  rear glove resting on the LEAD SHOULDER; lead forearm below across the belly, lead glove out at the REAR side. Chin tucked behind the top arm and lead shoulder, elbows tight to the ribs. Right hand comes straight off the chin, no hammer. (PLAN.md)
  skillr l69: crouched, weight on the FRONT foot (covered head is closer than the open body). Weak to uppercuts; body wide open.
  Note: skillr says "one OR both forearms across the face" (a looser version) vs Ed's fixed stacked picture. Use Ed's.
- Low hands / "handslow" (Hearns, Roy Jones, Mayweather's lead, Martinez, Davis, Fury, Haney's lead, Loma's rhythm):
  hands out of the eyeline, loose whipping shots, head does the defending (skillr l72 "riskiest"). Lead hand at the WAIST (Hearns). Open guard at SHOULDER height (Leonard, Duran).
  Lead hand low "bait" (Mayweather pull counter). Shoulders stay loose.
- Long guard (Fury, Klitschko, Usyk-ish, Stevenson): lead arm extended, rear hand at chin, bladed (skillr l73; longguard.md l8-30). Arm tires; body on that side is open.
- Open guard (Duran, square stance): long arms parry/block from outside; square so the rear hand is close. (skillr l138)
- Loose vs tight (Ed, PLAN.md Bodies): loose hands sit lower and wider and drift; tight hands lock to the chin; tension dial drives the blend.
- Shell used as rest mode ("prevent" shell, boxinglife.md): tight, can see through (hurt.md l68: gloves to forehead, elbows in, weight on the BACK foot).

### 4. Named fighters, one line each (stance only)
- Mayweather: Philly shell, lead arm low across the body, lead shoulder high, chin tucked, torso moves in the pocket; pull counter leaning ~4 in.
- Tyson: peekaboo crouch, gloves at cheeks, constant bob and weave, goes inside, weight forward.
- Canelo: orthodox, tall-ish high tight guard, flat-footed planted, steady walk, leans back to bait; head off centre on entry.
- Bivol: orthodox tall high guard, temples, elbows in; fencer on the back foot.
- Lomachenko: southpaw, hands high-ish and relaxed, constant head/upper-body movement, head lean forward on entry (a flaw), steps off the line.
- Usyk: southpaw, long active lead hand, small steps, outside-foot game, head off the line.
- Stevenson: southpaw Philly shell, extended lead, just outside reach.
- Crawford: orthodox who fights mostly southpaw vs good men; mid-high guard, stays on the end of reach, switches in a combo.
- Ennis: true switch-hitter, slips and rolls, changes stance mid-combo; knees bent on low jabs (ennis.md l10).
- Inoue: orthodox, standard-to-high guard, fade-away step-back, wide stable base, squares or blades by guard.
- Benavidez: straight up, high guard, flat slow feet. Spence: wide legs, reaches, lunges. Fury: hands low, long, leans on men inside.
- Nick Ball: very low crouch, spring. Figueroa-type: short squat crouched, high guard.
- Hearns: tall, narrow, bouncing, lead hand at the waist. Leonard: narrow, tall, square, open shoulder-height guard. Hagler: high guard, chin down, weight a bit forward, switch-hitter. Duran: square, open guard.

- Nakatani: southpaw, tall and upright, long hanging right hand (fighters_b l126). Wilder: bounces on the spot. Foreman: planted.
- Provodnikov/Gatti-type brawlers: walk through shots, hooks to head and body (substyles.md).
- Huni: bounce and split-step footwork (fighters_f l98).

### 5. Movement while standing
- Rhythm by style: pressure = steady heavy shuffle; out-boxer = light quick bounce; counter = low, still, almost no bob (combat_research.md l70).
  Punches start ON the beat with some probability; an off-beat punch is a feint tell. A man who bounces on a beat often punches on that beat (mind_vision l39).
- Ed's loose-but-ready (PLAN.md Bodies, Oct 22): relaxed, light, always moving; head never still, shoulders rolling, hands floating/pumping, breathing; feet never planted long, small steps/resets, planted only to punch; punches snap from the loose hand, then home to guard; torso twists into punches.
  Tension dial 0 loose .. 1 shell; low at range, rising on close/incoming punch/hurt/fear.
- Rest in motion (mind_pace.md l81): elite boxers recover while moving; fatigue shows as clinch time up, guard dropping more often, bouncing on the feet decreasing.
- Tired (several sources): bounce dies, flat-footed, shorter steps, longer recovery (combat_research l28 "fresh bounce on the balls of the feet, tired go flat-footed"; gripes.md l15; coach_a l135 Dundee: tired = flat feet, hands low, head upright);
  hands low (coach_c l270); mouth open, knees straight, eyes drift off the man [B, tells.md l71]. "Head rises / mouth open / flat feet" is only [B] (tells.md l113).
  Bivol vs Beterbiev 2: stood flat-footed, sat down on punches, then went back to moving on his toes (mind_traps l56).
- Tired + gassed in game: PLAN.md says hands sag, breathes through the mouth, bounce dies.
- Hurt: legs tell it: knees dip, wide or crossed feet, stumbling side steps, "drunk" steps (mind_finish.md l16); "wide base, slow reset" (coach_a l273); feet slower and wider (coach_c l266);
  hands drop or hang (mind_finish l19); "heavy feet, very flat-footed" the next round, then up on his toes again after the corner (hurt.md l23, Crawford-Gamboa);
  looking down when hurt gets you hit (hurt.md); "chicken dance / wide base / stutter steps" are NOT in any official source (tells.md l36). Hurt man's hands/head/feet still move: still-moving = conscious (hurt.md l52).
  Hurt cover: tight shell, gloves to forehead, elbows in, weight on the BACK foot, "spread your feet" and relax (hurt.md l62, l68). Stagger = spring impulse + forced step back with guard lower (combat_research l95).
  Clinch: bodies lean on each other (combat_research l95; size.md lean section).
- Close vs range: at range loose and bouncy, hands lower; close/inside tight, head on chest/cheek (dynamic.md Duran/Hatton/GGG), squared up with short hooks off a hip rhythm
  (dynamic.md l50: "square up and dig the body"); the long boxing stance close in gives the other man the edge. Inside the short man drops, goes around, short steps (dynamic l46).
- Weight shifts: Hearns weight to the front foot and side to raise the guard; Leonard's lead hook is loaded by the right hand shifting weight forward; Inoue's liver shot transfers weight to the front foot with full rotation; Feint to bring a man in = weight on the back foot (dynamic l58).
- Pull-back: lean back (rear foot), spring off it (Mayweather, Moore "step and fetch it", Leonard); straight-back pullers run out of room (mind_learn).
- Level changes: Hagler dips; Ball changes level before every punch; Kovalev's hip/crouch body feint; Ennis's jab changes levels (chin, chest, belly).
- Southpaw: mirror image. Open-stance matchups put the liver in front of the rear hand; lead foot outside wins the straight back-hand lane (mind_engage l29, mind_combos s4, ring.md l34). Nothing on how a southpaw body is posed differently. Crawford lived southpaw vs Spence. Usyk/Loma/Stevenson/Nakatani are all southpaws.
- Eye/gaze: experts park their gaze on the opponent's chest/middle, read hands and feet with peripheral vision (mind_vision l11). Counter-men read legs, hips, shoulders, never the eyes (boxinglife l28).
- Tells to show: lean over the front foot, head lean on entry, chin lift when overreaching with the jab (Inoue read), right hand slightly low, squares up when moving sideways (Leonard), pulls straight back with hands out (Hearns).
- Breathing: Ed's "life" layer; tired men breathe harder (nothing numeric).

### 6. Numbers (all of them I found)
- Amateur fatigue (Dunn et al. 2017, PLOS ONE, 26 elite boxers, 3x3 min; tells.md l68 [S]): guard visibly lowered R1 24.1 s, R2 31.0 s, R3 34.1 s (about +29% then +41%);
  clinch time 11.1 / 17.1 / 19.8 s; on-the-spot bounce time FELL; stepping time flat. Guard drop = a third of the round by R3 (that is a gift for the 3D tired layer).
- Recovery/hurt tell: recovery to hands slower than baseline by ~30% = treat as hurt (coach_a l273 [est]). Hands/feet late 15-25% = tired (coach_c l265 [est]).
- Mayweather pull counter: lean back ~4 inches, right hand down the middle (explainers l150 [S/B]).
- Punch weight shift: hip travels ~10-20% of stance width (combat_research l68 [est]).
- Rhythm: hit stop 9/11/13 frames in other games (combat_research l46); Wind-up for power shots 2-3x the jab's startup with a visible shoulder dip and rear-heel lift (combat_research l18).
- Jab reaches ~34-37 in from stance; 1 in wingspan ~0.5 in of jab; ~1.5% per inch (size.md l200).
- Heights/reach given: Hearns 6'2" / 78 in; Ball 5'2"; Nakatani 5'8"; Fury 6'9"-ish (277 lb at his heaviest, Wilder 2: 273 vs 231 lb). Mayweather jab connect 50%, etc. (not stance).
- Stoppage/hurt timing: delayed legs-out about 4 s after a body shot (tells.md l30).
- Game's own punch timing: impact at 60% of the punch; 3D chain: hips ~70% turned before glove 10% out, glove lands 54%, home 88% (PLAN.md Round 2).
- Straight-guard blocks and hook numbers in skillr/jabsafe are about punch outcomes, not posture.

### 7. Contradictions and judgement calls
- Wide vs narrow: Inoue "wide and stable" and Spence "legs too wide (a flaw)" vs Hearns/Leonard "narrow". Roach: "not too narrow, not square". So: wide is fine for planted power men, a flaw for reach/lunging; narrow suits tall movers. Not in conflict if read by style.
- Tall vs crouched out-boxer: skillr says either works for the tall man; PLAN.md says out-boxers bounce on the toes (taller). Pick tall-on-toes as default, crouch as the variant.
- Cross guard: skillr = "one or both forearms across the face" vs Ed's photos = both forearms STACKED lower (chest + belly). Use Ed. Note Norton in particular (Ed's reference).
- Philly vs uppercuts: skillr says uppercuts mostly don't work vs it (bladed), the game's GUARD_HOLES gives uppercut 1.15 (a hole). A render pose must not decide this; it is a balance flag.
- Futch's "stand upright" vs the general rule that hurt/blind men should cover and keep the head moving (coach_a, corners.md): the file frames it as a mistake.
- Weight forward vs back: cross-guard men and Hagler weight forward; hurt shell and loaded counter weight back; Mayweather's low-lead bait weights the front foot to look hittable, then he pulls. So weight is a STATE, not just a style.
- Flat-footed: slugger/Canelo/Benavidez flat is "normal"; tired out-boxer flat is a fatigue sign. Treat as style base + fatigue offset.
- "Mouth open, head up, flat feet" while tired are [B] only; the hands-down and lost bounce are [S].
- Ali hands: not covered at all (only distance and rope-a-dope).

### 1. Feet
- Traditional stance has "that line in between your feet" (feet on a line toward the opponent, lead foot a bit
  forward) and a solid base; Jeffries lists the pros wearing it (GGG, Khan, Joshua, Canelo). [Tony Jeffries, PLU3-tlD3y4]
- Peekaboo (Tyson) is a squarer stance: lead foot forward but set on roughly a 45 degree angle, "a lot more square"
  than the traditional bladed one. [Tony Jeffries, Ai8DVNb2g_U]
- High guard is thrown from a squared stance, body slightly crouched. [Skillr, tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Philly shell / lead-hand-extended guards are bladed to be a smaller target (and maximise reach). [Skillr, tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Feet land back in the "perfect stance" after any pivot; going too far round leaves you off balance, too little leaves
  you square and open. [Tony Jeffries, hNclexRmDsY]
- (general, unverified) width about shoulder width, lead toe ~15-45 deg in, rear foot ~45-90 deg out, heel-toe-ish
  offset; rear heel slightly up, lead foot flat-ish. No clip here gave these numbers.

### 2. Knees
- Peekaboo/high guard: crouched, a little sink. Pivot clip says "sink your weight just a little bit" before a pivot,
  then come back up, don't keep dropping. [Precision Striking, YHF-u7YlEag]
- Boxing on toes = light, always ready to step in/out; flat-footed = planted, more power, better balance.
  [Tony Jeffries, k2HKIN1iuFA]
- (general) knees soft/bent a few degrees, never locked; shown is a "spring" not a deep squat.

### 3. Weight
- Oliver Betts: weight is set by where the belly button/hip centre sits. Don't stand 50/50 when in punching range or
  defending: keep weight over the BACK leg (hips back, hips slightly more bent, lead shoulder dropped a touch) so the head
  is as far from the opponent as possible, and throw all punches from there without the head travelling forward;
  slip off the back leg too. 50/50 is fine when just moving around the ring (outboxer = more 50/50). His pro-style
  advice: back-weighted pressure fighter. [Oliver Betts, v9hd9DpytQw] (no exact % given)
- Tyson-style lead hook: slip, keep upright, ~60% of weight on the FRONT leg, then drop/turn back heel and rotate.
  Slipping side to side loads the weight on the same-side foot to load that hand. [Tony Jeffries, Ai8DVNb2g_U]
- Mayweather: leans back slightly, lead foot stays close, weight shifts to rear foot to create distance, makes the
  opponent reach. [Pro Striking, _PGzvfY2caY]
- Punch power from two sources: moving the body mass into the target (forward step, driven off rear leg) and rotation;
  combine them. A rotation needs front foot holding as the counter-force. [Precision Striking, i5a6mBTx8wA]
- Disagreement: Betts says back-weighted; Jeffries "traditional stance, transfer weight with each punch"; Precision
  Striking says drive forward off the rear leg. Reconcile: rest back-weighted, shift forward during the punch, return.

### 4. Hips / shoulders / chin / lean
- Chin down, hands up, elbows in = the traditional baseline. [Tony Jeffries, PLU3-tlD3y4]
- Bladed (Philly shell) vs square (peekaboo/high guard): square = more power forward, more slips side to side; bladed
  = smaller target, rear hand farther from target. [Skillr, tBJ4IWYzEWI; Jeffries, Ai8DVNb2g_U]
- Dropping the hips back, bending the hips a bit more and dropping the lead shoulder = how to lean away without moving the
  feet. [Oliver Betts, v9hd9DpytQw]
- No degree figures for hips/shoulders in the clips I got. (general) hips ~45-60 deg from the target line, shoulders a
  bit squarer than hips.

### 5. Hands and elbows (basic guard)
- Traditional: hands up, elbows in, chin down. Lead hand must stay up through pivots (hand dropped = clipped).
  [Jeffries, PLU3-tlD3y4; hNclexRmDsY]
- Peekaboo: hands cover the cheeks, forearms block the centre line, elbows guard the body (rear elbow = liver, lead elbow
  = body and middle). Hands high but not covering the eyes. [Jeffries, Ai8DVNb2g_U; Fight Club, 34VfvQcl1II]
- High guard: both arms up, elbows tucked to the sides, slightly crouched. Closes in the middle for straight shots,
  opens at the sides for hooks. [Skillr, tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Philly shell: lead hand rests across the stomach, rear hand diagonal at the face side with elbow tucked in.
  [Skillr, tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Extended-lead-hand guard: lead hand out, rear hand at chin level. [Skillr, tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Cross guard: arms tight like a scarf; leaves big openings. [Modern Martial Artist, dtF0gxP1n1M]
- (general) glove ~1-3 in off the cheek, rear glove touching, lead glove ~4-8 in forward.

### 6. Footwork
- Move the foot closest to the direction first: forward = lead foot first then rear; back = rear first then lead; left
  = left first then right; right = right first then left. Reason: never get feet too close or crossed, which unbalances
  you. Some elite fighters break this, but it is the base rule. [Skillr, E9KsmUO1tjQ]
- Stepping back: press on the lead foot, step back with the rear foot (then lead follows). Most basic defensive footwork,
  takes you out of range without reading the punch. [Skillr, E9KsmUO1tjQ]
- L-step: lead foot back while the rear foot goes to the side, then reset the lead foot. Gets out of range and lateral in
  one move. [Skillr, E9KsmUO1tjQ]
- Pivot: lead foot stays planted, rear foot swings round like a compass. Cue: a nail through the lead foot, "squish the
  bug"/put out a cigarette. About a 90 degree change of angle. Orthodox pivot to the left (counterclockwise) is the
  easy one. Feet land back in the correct stance. [Skillr, E9KsmUO1tjQ; Jeffries, hNclexRmDsY; Precision, YHF-u7YlEag]
- Pivot to the right (clockwise) is harder, leaves you squared, used mostly inside/on the ropes. Jeffries prefers a step
  version where the front foot lifts a tiny bit for a split second, feels more balanced. [Jeffries, hNclexRmDsY]
- Pivot and hips: pivots and punch hips work "in opposites". After a jab the hips are closed, so the natural pivot is
  out the other way; after the right hand the body is turned so the natural pivot is counterclockwise. Sink the weight a
  little first (pressure on the rear leg) so it accelerates. [Precision Striking, YHF-u7YlEag]
- Pivoting around the lead foot ends you closer to the opponent one way and farther the other; step small on the
  1-2 to get off the centre line then pivot. [Precision Striking, YHF-u7YlEag]
- Pivot-hook timing: the hook and the landing foot should land at the same instant for balance and power.
  [Jeffries, hNclexRmDsY]
- Lateral move: the foot opposite to the direction pushes, the other opens to the side; on a squared stance (feet side by
  side) a faster repeated way to go around the ring. [Skillr, E9KsmUO1tjQ]
- Step-shuffle (Tyson-linked): small step forward and slightly sideways, head off line, then rear foot chases lead as you
  hop to a new position at about 90 degrees. Back version crosses the rear leg forward. [Skillr, E9KsmUO1tjQ]
- Shifting stance (orthodox to southpaw) is a pivot-like motion with the rear foot going forward. [Skillr, E9KsmUO1tjQ]
- On toes vs flat: on toes = footwork defense, in and out, faster punches (Pacquiao steps and punches together),
  costs energy; flat = more power, better balance, better inside, less energy, but defense relies on head and hands only,
  always in range. Jeffries: train both, mostly on toes. Disagreement: pros of the era told him to plant his feet.
  [Tony Jeffries, k2HKIN1iuFA]
- Lomachenko style: pivots and shuffles to the opponent's side, leaning on the opponent so he can't turn. [Modern
  Martial Artist, FjHaeloJvS0]

### 7. Punch chain
- Forward step then drive off the rear leg, the front foot lands with the punch. Variants: front-foot step only, push off
  with both feet, rear-foot skip first (telegraphs). [Precision Striking, i5a6mBTx8wA]
- Rotation: full pivot (rear heel turns out, hips and shoulders turn) down to a small shoulder/hip burst. Pros use small
  rotation. Front leg is the hold/brace. Acceleration and timing matter more than size. [Precision, i5a6mBTx8wA]
- Rear heel drops/turns for the lead hook ("drop the back heel and transfer the weight around"). [Jeffries, Ai8DVNb2g_U]
- Return to stance: feet land back in stance after pivots and steps; lead hand stays up. [Jeffries, hNclexRmDsY]
- (general) order: rear foot pushes, hips turn, shoulder, then hand last; punch lands as the lead foot lands.

### 8. Mistakes that look wrong (avoid in animation)
- Crossing the feet while moving laterally. [Skillr, E9KsmUO1tjQ]
- Dropping the lead hand on a pivot or step. [Jeffries, hNclexRmDsY]
- Pivoting too far (all the way round) or not far enough (square, open). [Jeffries, hNclexRmDsY]
- Feet getting close together when stepping because the order was wrong. [Skillr, E9KsmUO1tjQ]
- Standing 50/50 upright in range with the head over the lead foot (easy to hit). [Oliver Betts, v9hd9DpytQw]
- Pulling the head forward when punching. [Oliver Betts, v9hd9DpytQw]
- Landing the punch and the foot at different times (off balance, slow rhythm). [Jeffries, hNclexRmDsY]
- Dropping lower and lower in a pivot instead of sinking once and rising. [Precision, YHF-u7YlEag]
- (general) too wide, too square outside peekaboo, straight legs, high bouncing.

### Summary
1. Moving: nearest foot first, always return to a set stance; never cross; steps small, lead hand up.
2. Pivot = lead foot pinned, rear foot swings ~90 deg; hips open/close opposite the punch; step version lifts the front foot a hair.
3. Weight rests slightly back (Betts) and shifts forward on the punch (~60% front on a hook); feet land with the hand.
4. Guards: bladed for Philly/outboxer, square-ish ~45 deg for peekaboo/high guard; elbows tucked, chin down.
5. Few hard numbers survived the bot wall; get width/toe/knee/degrees from a retry of rU2MWIoVVos, e2S6-gwR0es, xYFja6-AA8g, vg4jPfOap_8.

### GUARDS

#### Peekaboo (Tyson / D'Amato)
- Stance is SQUARE, not the 45-degree traditional one. Lead (left) foot forward but hips and chest face the opponent. [Tony Jeffries, Ai8DVNb2g_U]
- Built to move FORWARD and side to side, not backward. "Can never remember Tyson moving backwards." So no retreating footwork. [Jeffries, Ai8DVNb2g_U]
- Hands up to protect the chin but NOT covering the eyes. Not out in front like a classic stance, they sit close to the face. Elbows in: right elbow guards the liver, left elbow guards the liver on its side plus the solar plexus line through the middle. [Jeffries, Ai8DVNb2g_U]
- Hands cover the cheeks, forearms block the centre line, elbows protect the body. Not passive: a "springboard". Hands drifting low is the named common mistake. [Fight Club, 34VfvQcl1II]
- Head movement: slip side to side with the hands up; the weight is already transferred because the stance is square, so the slip is faster than from a bladed stance. Slip AND step together: slip left, step left foot, weight onto that foot, loading that hand (left hook); mirror right. [Jeffries, Ai8DVNb2g_U]
- Rhythm: "right left right left" constant side-to-side bob so the opponent can't time him. Waits for the last moment to move the head (batter timing). [Jeffries Ai8DVNb2g_U; Fight Club 34VfvQcl1II]
- Dips VERY low against taller men, so they have to punch down, then comes up to finish. Lower centre of gravity (he was 5'10" vs 6'2"-6'3" average heavy). [Jeffries Ai8DVNb2g_U; Fight Club 34VfvQcl1II]
- Footwork: quick shuffle of the lead foot then reset the back foot to change angles, mid-combination. "Constant motion". [Jeffries Ai8DVNb2g_U]
- Numbers: weight on the front foot ~70% on his lead hook vs ~60% for a traditional hook (Jeffries's demo). [Jeffries Ai8DVNb2g_U]
- Silhouette: compact square block, chin tucked behind gloves at cheek height, elbows pinned, deep crouch, head bobbing in a U / figure-8 on a forward-leaning body. Always walking forward. [PK: gloves around cheek / temple, forearms near vertical]

#### High guard / "Winky" / standard high
- Both arms up, elbows tucked to the sides, body slightly crouched, SQUARED stance. [Skillr, tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Guard CLOSES through the middle for straights and OPENS to cover the sides for hooks; small raises/lowers for head vs body. [Skillr, tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Pros: safest, versatile, good inside and mid-range, rear hand is closer to the target so it hits easier and combos flow. Cons: bigger target (squared), blocks vision, less agile / static, hard to counter off it because hands are glued to the face, you still absorb the rattle. [Skillr tBJ4IWYzEWI; Jeffries H0__TbcXd6I]
- "Hands in front of the face all the time" so a 1-2-hook is hard to SEE coming; versus the traditional stance where hands are a little forward so they have less distance to travel. [Jeffries, H0__TbcXd6I]
- Users named: Ronald "Winky" Wright, Arthur Abraham (Jeffries); Canelo takes punches on a high guard and only opens up to counter. [Jeffries H0__TbcXd6I; MMA vhQam_ZJb2Y]
- Used to push the man backwards and cut the ring: "walk-down with the guard up". [Jeffries, H0__TbcXd6I]
- Silhouette: two gloves stacked at the brow/temple, elbows touching the ribs, peeking over the gloves, squarish, little head movement, plodding forward. [PK: glove tops about eyebrow level, Winky even forward-leaning with gloves nearly touching the forehead]

#### Philly shell (Mayweather / Benton / Toney / Whitaker)
- BLADED stance. Lead arm lowered across the stomach, horizontal ("arm in a sling"), forearm covers the midsection, elbow covers the ribs. Rear hand on the side of the face / cheek (or in front of the face) as a "catcher's mitt". Chin tucked behind the lead shoulder, which rolls up to deflect the cross. [Skillr tBJ4IWYzEWI; Pro Striking _PGzvfY2caY]
- Rear hand is far from the opponent (bladed), so little rear-hand offence; the lead hand is the working weapon: jab and hook come from low, outside the centre vision. [Skillr, tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Posture is a TRANSIENT, not a fixed pose: Floyd shows high guard, long guard (lead hand extended), low guard, and shell, moving between them, often with a slip or a bend at the waist. [Pro Striking _PGzvfY2caY; Skillr hq7evFpmVek]
- Slightly bent over / lower posture in the shell; wide visual field because no glove in front of the eyes. [Pro Striking, _PGzvfY2caY]
- Distance trick: lean back with the lead foot close while the weight goes to the rear foot, making the opponent reach. [Pro Striking, _PGzvfY2caY]
- Facing a southpaw he uses the lead ELBOW instead of the shoulder roll. [Skillr hq7evFpmVek]
- He lowers his stance and shifts weight forward before the lead right. [Skillr hq7evFpmVek]
- Weakness: head open, bad against volume and against angles; the shell needs good timing. Hooks/uppercuts less effective against it (bladed). [Skillr tBJ4IWYzEWI; Pro Striking _PGzvfY2caY]
- Names: Michigan defense (Toney), crab style (Benton). Whitaker also fought hands-down. [Pro Striking _PGzvfY2caY]
- Silhouette: side-on, lead shoulder hunched to the chin, lead forearm flat across the belly, rear glove at the cheek, upper body tilted/rolling, feet relaxed, light, little bounce. [PK: rear glove at cheek or temple, small head tilt]

#### Cross-arm / cross-guard (Norton, Moore, Foreman late, Frazier, Fulmer, Holyfield)
- Arms cross the face: one hand across with a horizontal forearm while the other is low guarding the body; or both arms up, forearms close, "posture slightly crouched and front-foot heavy" so the covered head is the closer target. Used from a SQUARED stance. [Skillr tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Tight around the neck "like a scarf" so it shields uppercuts; elbows and shoulder protect one side, glove and opposite shoulder the other. [Modern Martial Artist, dtF0gxP1n1M]
- Variants: Fulmer's REVERSE cross guard (lead arm on top, forearm + elbow block, rear hand free for explosive power shots; lead elbow raised, dips right); Archie Moore stands square with tight arms ("a wall"), then turns and counters; Foreman: crossblock into a cross FRAME to push the target; Cormier/Buakaw "Dracula guard" (cross block + long arm). [Modern Martial Artist dtF0gxP1n1M]
- Footwork/pace: pressure and patience; marches forward; the fist flows from the punch straight into a cross block instead of retracting. [MMA dtF0gxP1n1M]
- Beats outboxers: neutralises the jab at any angle; lean slightly forward and the head is covered while the body is out of reach. [MMA dtF0gxP1n1M]
- Weaknesses: hard to throw out of (arms must reposition so punches are slow, counters and the lead hook / cross are the usual); body wide open; hooks go round the elbows; uppercuts risky; volume punchers beat it (Marciano vs Moore, Holyfield vs Foreman). [Skillr tBJ4IWYzEWI; MMA dtF0gxP1n1M]
- Pair with head movement (Frazier dips left under the block, weaves out to hook). [MMA dtF0gxP1n1M]
- Silhouette: forearms form an X / horizontal bar at the eyes-nose, elbows low and in, gloves near the opposite shoulder, hunched, front-heavy, square, slow plodding walk-in, little head movement. [PK]

#### Hands low / hands down (Roy Jones, Naseem, Toney, Whitaker, Ali early)
- Chin STAYS tucked even with hands down. Rely on head movement plus shoulder roll. [FightMap, NNNnoQHbum0]
- Feet must stay quick and moving all the time (Naseem, Roy Jones, Whitaker); speed (hand, foot, head) is the first priority or the style fails. [FightMap NNNnoQHbum0]
- Bring hands UP in the pocket / on the ropes / when swarmed (even Floyd). [FightMap NNNnoQHbum0]
- Benefits: unorthodox angles, looser snappier whip punches, can throw from weird angles (overhand then uppercut and step out), jab hidden from the centre vision. [FightMap NNNnoQHbum0; Skillr tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Skillr's "low guard": hands at about waist level, head open, body partly open; head hit defended by head movement, body by turning; cons overwhelm pros. [Skillr tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- Silhouette: arms hang by the belt, wrists loose, a small tilt or lean of the head and torso, standing tall, bouncing. [PK: Roy bounces, torso leaned back, Naseem a wide staggering low-hands bob, Jones's lead hand dangles and swings, chin up]

#### Long guard / classic out-boxer (Skillr)
- Lead hand extended to control, rear hand at chin level; bladed stance to maximise reach; body open on the lead-hand side. Tiring for the lead arm. Rear-hand shots parried by the extended lead. [Skillr, tBJ4IWYzEWI]
- "Standard": traditional stance about 45 degrees; hands a bit out in front. [Jeffries, Ai8DVNb2g_U / H0__TbcXd6I]

### STYLES AND FIGHTERS

#### Swarmer / bob-and-weave (Frazier)
- Bends at the WAIST, leans forward like a wrestler, rather than at the knees. Gets lower and leans body weight into the opponent; crashes in with shoulder / head into the sternum. [MMA, 8BHaOqDPeRs]
- Weaves shoulder to shoulder with the punches staying tight; the weave builds the power. [MMA 8BHaOqDPeRs]
- Rhythm: erratic, "dancing to a song only he could hear", broken tempo, hand-milling; syncs with the opponent's rhythm then breaks it with a lead hook. Loses balance now and then. [MMA 8BHaOqDPeRs]
- Does NOT cut the ring with a wide stance or diagonal cross-steps; uses head movement and walks straight in. [MMA 8BHaOqDPeRs]
- Uses a cross block as extra cover while closing; the rear arm hides the lead hand so the hook comes from underneath. Shuffles and jumps into the hook with full body weight. [MMA 8BHaOqDPeRs]
- Silhouette: head low, below the opponent's chin, trunk hinged forward, gloves tucked at the chin / cheeks, constant side-to-side pendulum sway, smiling, always marching in. [PK]
- Tyson fills the same swarmer niche in a square, lower, shorter-step form (see Peekaboo).

#### Canelo (boxer-puncher / counter)
- Narrow, long, BLADED stance combined with a SQUARED-UP upper body and forward high guard. [MMA, vhQam_ZJb2Y]
- Narrow bladed lower body => linear explosions in and out and lean-back. Squared upper => rear hand close for fast counters. Cost: has to drag the rear leg far forward, sometimes leaving his stance. [MMA vhQam_ZJb2Y]
- Mostly takes punches on the high guard; when the counter opens he commits and uses head movement/roll while the punch is in flight. Doubles up on the same hand by stepping / dropping the weight between. [MMA vhQam_ZJb2Y]
- Skillr/Canelo-vs-Munguia: also low-guard stretches (hands down). [Skillr tBJ4IWYzEWI mentions Canelo keeping hands down]

#### Lomachenko (angles / awkward-ish out-boxer)
- Wins with vision control: keeps the feinting back hand in the opponent's eye line "like a matador's cape" while moving off the line; mentions the shuffle pivot ("Matrix shuffle"); leans weight on the opponent's back/shoulder while shuffling. [MMA, FjHaeloJvS0; MMA 0_KhCwYH2WI]
- The look: a southpaw who constantly moves around the opponent's lead foot; stance is not described in captions. [PK: fairly narrow, light on the toes, hands medium-high with a loose lead hand, pivoting on the lead foot, steps in triangles]

#### Southpaw (general)
- Opposite lead; leads with the right hand and foot, power hand (left) is the rear. Fights are decided by foot position: lead foot OUTSIDE the opponent's lead foot lines up the rear hand and keeps head offline; INSIDE lines up your jab but also the opponent's rear hand. Pacquiao: inside / outside angles, turning on a dime; Whitaker: inside foot plus Philly shell; Hagler: switched to orthodox on the spin. [MMA, 0_KhCwYH2WI]
- Names the southpaws: Crawford, Pacquiao, Lomachenko, Whitaker, Tagler, Usyk, Hagler (switch). [MMA 0_KhCwYH2WI]
- Game consequence: the mirrored lead hands clash (jab meets jab); the lead-foot battle is visible on screen, feet nearly stomping. [MMA 0_KhCwYH2WI]

#### Mayweather vs the rest of his system
- The shell is "just the posture", the substance is distance, slip-before-the-punch, smother. Smothers by stepping in mid-combination, ring generalship from centre ring. [Pro Striking, _PGzvfY2caY]

#### Not captured (no captions; [PK] only)
- Naseem: wide staggering stance, hands at the waist, chin up, torso leaning, bounce/shimmy, weird lunging angles, springy.
- Crawford: orthodox/southpaw switch, mid-high hands, upright bladed stance, switch often mid-combo, calm and low bounce.
- Usyk: southpaw, upright and bouncy, small shuffling steps in a triangle, hands about chin height and loose, constant feints, rotating shoulders, bounce on the toes.
- Roy Jones: bounce, torso lean-back, lead hand at the waist / swinging, rear glove at chin, explosive dive-bomb entries.

### DISAGREEMENTS / NOTES
- Tyson square (Jeffries) vs mainstream 45-degree stance; and Canelo "bladed legs, squared shoulders" shows that legs and upper body are separate parameters. Worth making hip-angle and shoulder-angle two sliders.
- High guard: Skillr says safest + most offensive of all guards (rear hand closer); Jeffries says it's mostly negatives (can't counter, blind, takes the rattle). Both: it's static; fine for a pressure style, bad for countering.
- Cross guard: Skillr says front-heavy crouched; MMA says it works for any body type and pairs with head movement and forward lean. Foreman = static march, Moore/Frazier = dynamic.
- Philly shell: Skillr calls it "unsafe" (head open); Pro Striking says it's only a posture and a minor part of the defense; Floyd rotates guards, so on screen it should switch.

### FOR THE 3D BOXER (suggested parameters, mine, not sourced)
- Two angle sliders: hip angle (bladed 80 deg to square 0) and shoulder angle (Canelo: bladed legs, square shoulders).
- Crouch/knee bend and torso hinge: Tyson deep knee bend with upright spine; Frazier hinge at the waist; Foreman/Norton hunch forward; Mayweather/Usyk/Roy tall.
- Weight: forward 70/30 for Tyson style; Philly and out-boxers rear-heavy; Canelo lean-back.
- Hand pairs (lead, rear): peekaboo cheeks-both; high guard brow-both; Philly belt-horizontal / rear cheek; cross-arm both forearms crossing at the eyes with the elbows in; low guard belt-both; long guard lead extended, rear chin.
- Head motion: peekaboo figure-8 side-to-side U bob; Frazier big waist pendulum; Philly small roll/tilt; hands-down head wobble; high/cross guard almost none.
- Feet: peekaboo and swarmer forward-only, short shuffles; out-boxer on the toes bouncing; Philly light and slow; cross-guard flat-footed.

### SUMMARY (5 lines)
1. Stance angle and hand shape are the two big reads: square + cheek hands = peekaboo, bladed + low lead arm across the belly = Philly shell, square + forearms across the face = cross guard.
2. Weight and crouch distinguish pressure fighters: Tyson deep knees/70% front, Frazier hinges at the waist, Foreman/Norton hunch front-heavy; out-boxers stay tall and rear-weighted.
3. Head movement rhythm is a style marker: Tyson figure-8 side-to-side, Frazier pendulum with a broken beat, shell shoulder roll, hands-down head-only defense with a tucked chin.
4. Fighters rotate guards (Floyd, Canelo, Frazier use cross block while closing), so it's better to model guards as poses that blend than fixed identities.
5. Captions give few numbers (only 70% vs 60% weight); Naseem, Crawford, Usyk and Winky are unsourced [PK].
