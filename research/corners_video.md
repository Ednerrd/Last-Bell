# Corners from video: batch 1 (Oct 19)

Source: one compilation, "greatest corner moments" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lh54VURUX4c). Ed pasted the auto-transcript; it is not kept. Notes in our own words, with only a few short phrases quoted.

This adds to `research/corners.md` (26 corners, patterns, "For Last Bell" rules 1-20; on branch `claude/clever-planck-urj1i4` until it merges). Moorer-Holyfield is already there. Everything below is new.

**Caveat:** this is a highlight reel, so it only shows corners whose call worked. Don't read "need a KO, got a KO" as the usual result. The names marked (my ID) are my best match from the transcript's misspelled names. Check them before using a name in the game.

## The corners
1. **Nazim Richardson, Mosley-Margarito (2009).** A plan built to unfold over the fight:
   - Early: go to the body, "this guy ain't used to getting it back at the body." Body work now so "no gas in the car" late.
   - Then: the jab, then fast combos, then "swim without getting wet": hit, then slide out to the angle or behind the jab before he answers.
   - Mosley stopped him. Body early, cash in late.
2. **Nazim Richardson, Mayweather-Mosley (2010), before R11.**
   - Mosley was down on the cards. His hands had slowed and he was waiting for the perfect spot.
   - The order: stop waiting, move the hands, step over and come back.
   - Then he made Mosley **say the plan back to him**. That was a hidden stop check: if Mosley couldn't repeat it, Nazim was going to stop the fight. He could, so it went on.
3. **Virgil Hunter, Brandon Gonzales vs Oosthuizen (2013) (my ID).**
   - An unranked underdog vs a top-5 man. Hunter was calm the whole night and fit his talk to the fighter.
   - He repeated one confidence line ("he's ready to be beaten") plus one fix: don't throw one and let him get away.
   - The fighter piled up rounds and still got a draw. Calm corner, good result, robbed cards.
4. **Enzo Calzaghe, Calzaghe-Hopkins (2008).**
   - Calzaghe was down early. Enzo told him he needed a knockdown, which turned him offensive.
   - Hopkins's corner believed they were ahead and stayed careful.
   - Calzaghe won by about a round. That's a second "ahead, so coast" corner that lost (after Fury-Usyk I in `corners.md`).
5. **Freddie Roach, Toney-Jirov (2003), before R12 (my ID).**
   - An honest score: he told Toney he needed a knockdown, and "don't let him hustle you."
   - Toney dropped him in the last 20 seconds.
6. **Emanuel Steward, Lewis-Tyson (2002).**
   - Lewis was well ahead and Tyson was hurt, but Lewis wasn't finishing.
   - Steward was furious: you have a beaten man in front of you, let your hands go.
   - Lewis KO'd him in R8. The finish call on a hurt man, from a fresh fighter, worked.
7. **Randall Bailey vs Mike Jones (2012) (my ID).**
   - Bailey was behind and doing nothing. The head trainer gave up, and the **cutman** took over and screamed at him to do something big.
   - Two big shots, two knockdowns, title. A one-punch power puncher down big has a real "one shot" path.
8. **Chavez-Taylor (1990), R12.** The corner told him straight: you need a KO. He got it with seconds left.
9. **Whitaker-Hurtado (1997), late.**
   - Down on the cards, with a size disadvantage, and not a puncher. The corner said he needed a KO.
   - He landed one clean shot and finished it in R11.
   - This one goes against `corners.md` rule 6 ("no realistic KO, so say win the round").
10. **Miura-Uchiyama (2013), flavor (my ID).** After a big knockdown, the wrong man walked to the other guy's corner and sat on his stool. Both camps laughed.

## What's new for Last Bell
- **Stop check by repeat-back (Nazim).** This is better than `corners.md`'s "how you feel?" idea:
  - A hurt man is asked to say the plan back. His hurt level decides whether he can.
  - If he fails, the corner stops it.
  - Mostly invisible, but it can give a great line: "Say it back to me."
- **"Waiting for the perfect shot" is a real failure.** Output drops and the hands slow. The fix is volume, not power. This fits a counter man who goes passive. The corner call is "move the hands."
- **A plan across rounds.** Body early to empty the tank, cash in late (Nazim). This matches `mind_combos`/body work. The AI corner could hold a 2-step plan, not just one call per break.
- **Corner personality fits the fighter.** Calm and repetitive (Hunter) vs fire (Atlas, Steward, the Bailey cutman). Both work. A mismatch could cost follow-through. A second voice taking over (Bailey's cutman) is the "two voices" case where it helped.
- **"Need a KO" late: keep a real chance.**
  - `corners.md` says to tell a man who can't KO him to win the round. These four (Toney, Chavez, Whitaker, Bailey) show the KO call landing.
  - Rule: a power puncher, or a man whose opponent is tired or hurt, gets the KO call. Others get "win the round", with a small KO chance left in.
  - Highlight bias: most KO calls fail.
- **Coasting while ahead loses again (Hopkins).** This backs `corners.md` rule 7: a misread "we're up" makes the fighter coast.
- **Finish call (Steward-Lewis).** Ahead, fresh, the other man hurt: an angry finish call works. Matches rule 5's gas gate.

# Batch 2: Corrales-Castillo I (2005), full fight broadcast
Ed pasted the full broadcast transcript; it is not kept. Corales's trainer is Joe Goossen. The TV audio only carries some breaks, and Goossen's famous "get inside" line during the R10 delay isn't on it. Who is speaking is a best guess where marked.

## Fight shape
- Castillo is known for: slow start, takes over in the middle, beats his man up late. Commentary had him trailing after 5, "right on time" in R6.
- Corales was taller and better outside, but chose to fight inside, in Castillo's "house." The commentators kept saying he should go back out.
- Cuts both ways from R3-R4: Castillo's left eye shut from the lid down; Corales's left eye shut from the cheek up. By R8 both were one-eyed.
- R7 end: Corales's left hook nearly dropped Castillo. Both men hurt from there on.
- R9: two low-blow warnings in one round and no point taken. The ref doesn't want to decide a close fight on a point.
- R10: Corales down twice from left hooks, the mouthpiece out both times. A point docked for losing it twice. He gets up, lands a right, then a combination, and the ref stops it at 2:06 of R10.
- Cards going in: two for Corales (by 1 and by 3), one for Castillo. Without the stop it was a 10-6 round the other way, and the cards would have flipped.

## The breaks (Corrales side unless marked)
1. **After R1 (won it).** Five things in a row: praise, keep the chin buried when you exchange, the hook and body shots are working, feint the jab more because he's waiting to counter it with the right, praise again. A "sandwich": what works plus one read of the other man, wrapped in praise.
2. **After R2 (won it).** Short: don't hand him the initiative, both hands, two uppercuts then go low.
3. **After R6 (wobbled at the end).**
   - Goossen: box him, he's about to bring pressure, this is where the fight gets decided.
   - Corales: "he didn't hurt me."
   - Goossen doesn't argue: "I know. But you can't leave your hands spread apart and let him outwork you."
   - R7: he boxed outside for a while, as told, then drifted back inside.
4. **After R7 (dropped Castillo almost).** Someone: "don't let him off, he's almost gone." A second voice about not seeing the right hand, likely about Corales's swollen eye (unclear).
5. **After R8 (both eyes swelling, the doctor at the stool).**
   - Goossen counts the rounds out loud: 9, 10, 11, 12. Four rounds left, don't stop on me, it's you or him.
   - The plan: smother him, head off to the side, keep walking forward.
   - The doctor asks; Goossen answers for him: "yes he can."
   - Then: you've got about 10 minutes of fight left, if you want it.
6. **After R9 (side unclear).** "Use the upper hand, he can't see anything you throw from the outside." A blind-side read: work outside shots to the side where his eye is shut.
7. **R10, between the knockdowns.** Goossen put the mouthpiece back in "smartly, very slowly" and bought Corales extra seconds. It cost a point, and it won the fight.

## What's new for Last Bell
- **A shut eye means a blind side.**
  - Corners in this fight say "he can't see the right" and "throw from the outside, he can't see it."
  - Wide shots (hooks, overhands) to the side of a shut eye should land more, and the corner should call it.
  - This fits the planned swelling zone (Ed, Oct 17).
- **A hurt man says he isn't hurt; the corner fixes the hole anyway.** Don't argue, give one guard fix. This supports `corners.md`'s hurt-fighter rule, with the denial line as flavor.
- **The doctor at the stool, and the corner arguing for the fighter.**
  - When the cut doctor checks between rounds, the corner says "he's fine, he can see."
  - A family or loyal corner pushes the doctor's stop later. This fits `CUT`'s doctor and the "corner stops late" rule.
- **Count the rounds out loud, late.** "9, 10, 11, 12, four rounds, it's you or him." A clock line done as a countdown. Good corner-talk content.
- **Stalling for seconds (rare, dirty, real).**
  - After a knockdown, the corner takes its time with the mouthpiece. That buys recovery time and costs a point.
  - Could be a rare cornerman event for a hurt man with a "crafty" corner. Flag for Ed: the ref docks a point, the crowd boos.
- **The fighter doesn't follow the plan.** Told to box outside, he drifts back into the war. Follow-through drops when the plan fights the man's nature.
- **A hurt man can still end it.** Two knockdowns in one round, then the KO. The other man was hurt too, from R7. Both-hurt late rounds should allow wild swings. Check our KD and stop numbers before adding any.
- **The ref and fouls in a close fight.** Two low-blow warnings in a round, no point, because it's close. Later, a point for the mouthpiece. Fits the clinch/ref work (warnings, then a point).
- **The R1 break shape.** Praise, one defensive fix, what's working, one read, praise. A good template for a fresh, winning fighter. `corners.md` says 1 note is normal; this is a chatty corner (about 4 notes), but each note is short.

# Batch 3: Fury-Wilder III (2021), full fight broadcast
Ed pasted the full broadcast transcript; it is not kept. The corners: Fury's is SugarHill Steward, Wilder's is Malik Scott. TV only aired four breaks. Speakers are inferred from the content.

## Fight shape
- Wilder came in with a real plan for once:
  - The jab, a lot of it to the body: 12 jabs in R1, after 19 total punches in R1 of fight 2.
  - Body first, then the right hand over the top.
  - He bulked up to 238. Commentary: more mass tires you faster.
- Fury: 277, his heaviest. Less head movement than fights 1-2. A slap-left decoy, the real threat is the right. He lands one-two then grabs. He leans his weight on Wilder.
- Knockdowns:
  - R3: Wilder down from a right.
  - R4: Fury down twice. Wilder was hurt and still came back.
  - R10: Wilder down.
  - R11: stopped at 1:10. The ref waved it off with Wilder still falling, no count.
- After 8, Fury outlanded him 98-54 but was down 2 KDs to 1. The unofficial card was even around R7-R8. A 10-7 round in R4.
- The ref (Mora): "let them punch out of it." He warned Fury not to headlock. He called a forced knee not a knockdown. Commentary said his counts were slow.
- Tired-man habits commentary called out, again and again:
  - Wilder puts his head down, so the uppercut comes.
  - Hurt, he backs straight up and walks into the right.
  - His hands drop.
  - His missed power shots drain him.
  - His jab disappeared: only 2 landed over 4 rounds once he was tired.

## The breaks
1. **After R1, Fury corner (likely).**
   - "He's getting tired already, you see it?"
   - "Keep setting the trap, you already caught him once with an easy one."
   - Then a sketch of the counter: when he comes in, boom.
   - A read on the other man plus confirming a planned trap works.
2. **After R3, Wilder corner (he was just dropped).**
   - Tactic first, not calm: "When you jab, your right hand is down here. He's throwing the hook as you jab. Keep the hand here to shut the hook down."
   - Then: deep breath, take a sip.
   - Then a second tactic: going forward, he times you, so be alert, not lazy.
   - Then R4: Wilder dropped Fury twice. A hurt man's corner went tactic-first and it worked. A counter-example to `corners.md`'s "state first."
3. **After R6, Fury corner (SugarHill).**
   - "Look at me. Wake up. You're lazy off the break. Stay alert, you control this guy. He only closes the space when you're not alert."
   - "Back to the body, then one-two upstairs."
   - Deep breath.
   - "You hurt him at the end, then he clinched, and you're making too many **silent agreements**. Work the free hand. Be physical."
4. **After R9, Wilder corner (Malik Scott).**
   - "You were blessed with something that can end this, but it has to come behind the jab. Keep thinking."
   - **"What round are we in?"** Wilder: "Like 10." A brain check.
   - "We're in 10 and you're still here. Hold it together, you'll be so proud of yourself tomorrow."
   - "When he backs you up, pivot out. Jab, go to the body. Keep the left up."
   - R10: dropped again. R11: stopped.
- **Off-air:** the doctor checked Wilder's right hand (he clutched his wrist). The corner said it was fine; commentary said it's a habit. A corner waving off an injury check.

## What's new for Last Bell
- **"Silent agreements" in the clinch.**
  - The fighter who's winning lets the clinch become a rest for both men. The corner wants the free hand working.
  - Game: a clinch should rest both men. A "physical" or well-coached man punches out of it (the ref lets them), a lazy one takes the rest.
  - A great corner line. Fits the clinch/ref plans (Ed, Oct 17).
- **"What round are we in?"** A second brain-check question, after Nazim's say-it-back (batch 1). Two cases now, so the stop check by question looks real. Build it as one mechanic with a few lines.
- **The corner fixes YOUR hole the other guy found.** "Your hand drops when you jab, he's timing it." This is the eyes system turned around: the corner sees what the opponent has read. Ties to `him.look` and the traps.
- **Hurt man, tactic first, and it worked.** Malik led with the fix after a knockdown. `corners.md` rule 2 says hurt = state line first. Keep that as the default, but a sharp corner with a clear read can lead with the fix. Maybe by corner skill.
- **"Proud of yourself tomorrow" vs Atlas's "cry tomorrow."** The same lever (tomorrow), with a carrot or a stick. Good line pair for the corner-talk table.
- **"Keep setting the trap."** The corner confirms a trap is working and says keep showing it. Ties to the built setups and traps (show, bank, draw).
- **Tired habits to model** (commentary, not the corner):
  - Head goes down, so uppercuts land.
  - Hurt and backing straight up, he walks into the right.
  - Missed power shots cost gas.
  - The jab dies when tired.
  - Check what the engine already does for each before adding anything.
- **Body mass and gas.** Wilder bulked and tired early. If weight within a division ever matters, heavier = more power, less gas. Idea only.
- **A hand injury, hidden.** The doctor checks, the corner says fine. Only matters if hand injuries get built. Parked.
- **Highlight-bias warning again.** This fight had 5 KDs. Real heavyweights don't average that. Don't tune KD rates to it.
