# Size: height, reach, leverage (Oct 19, full pages)

Method: opened full pages with curl (PLOS paper, AgentMMA, Fight Matrix, CompuBox write-ups, CBS, Wikipedia, MMASucka, trainer sites). Some sites were blocked (Ring, BoxRec, Quora, SAGE, Bruin Sports): those numbers come from search summaries and are tagged [B].
Tags: [S] read in a full source. [Q] direct quote. [B] lore or snippet only, not confirmed.
All heights and reaches are inches unless marked. Fighter sizes are Wikipedia infobox figures: they differ by source by 1-2 in.

## 1. Does reach or height win fights?

Short answer: yes, a little, more in the big divisions, and the data is muddy (longer men are often just better fighters).

Boxing
- Heavyweight title fights, 1990s-2021 (n = 182 bouts, PLOS ONE). Winners were taller and longer than losers. [S]
  - Height: winners median 196 cm, losers 190.5. Means 194.3 vs 190.9 cm (+3.4 cm = 1.3 in). Effect size d = 0.35.
  - Reach: winners mean 202.1 cm, losers 199.1 (+3.0 cm = 1.2 in). d = 0.23, p = 0.003.
  - All title men: mean height 192.6 cm (75.8 in), reach 200.8 cm (79.1 in). So the average heavyweight has reach minus height of +8.2 cm = +3.2 in.
  - Winners also threw more and landed ~8 points more accurately. Jab share was the same for winners and losers.
  - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8786172/
- Same idea, longer window (237 heavyweight title men, 1889-2019): champions beat challengers by +3.4 cm height, +3.6 cm reach, +3.7 kg. [S abstract]
  - https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/201384 (Han et al. 2020)
- 522 European-ranked bouts: winners were 3.12 cm taller on average (p < .05). Quality (record) mattered more. [S]
  - https://www.redalyc.org/journal/710/71055532008/html/
- Fight Matrix (old blog, all of boxing history, all divisions, raw, no controls): [S]
  - Any reach edge: the longer man won 57.1% (draws out).
  - 2 in or more: 58.4%. Climbs to 64.6% at 8 in or more. Drops at 10 in+ (tiny sample).
  - Quote: "size DOES matter." Confounded by quality and matchmaking, so treat as the ceiling.
  - https://www.fightmatrix.com/2008/01/18/boxing-stats-reach-matters/
- I found no peer-reviewed win-per-inch study by weight class in boxing. [S, searched]

MMA (cleaner, big sample, same idea)
- AgentMMA, 6,685 UFC bouts, 2005-2026. Longer-reach man won 52.0% overall. [S]
  - Gap 1-2 in: 49.9% (n 3,398). 3-4 in: 53.1%. 5-6 in: 54.3%. 7 in+: 59.6% (n 376).
  - Edge of 3 in+ by weight: men 125-155 lb 51.6%; 170-185 lb 54.8%; 205-265 lb 61.7%; women 50.6%.
  - Plain read: under 3 in it is a coin flip. Big gaps and big men are where it shows.
  - https://agentmma.com/mma-lab/ufc-reach-advantage
- Same page cites (second-hand): a 2,229-bout study found no armspan effect except heavyweight (small); another found armspan explains under 1% of career win rate, ~0.2 points per cm. [B]
- A UFC blog on 1993-2021 fights: edge won 51.65%; 7 in+ disadvantage won only 37.4%; at 125 and 135 the longer man did slightly worse. Page was down. [B]
  - https://www.bruinsportsanalytics.com/post/mma_reach
- UFC average reach gap between two fighters is 6.5 cm (2.5 in). About 75% of fights have a gap under 10 cm (4 in). Title fights have smaller gaps. [S]
  - https://analytics4mma.substack.com/p/is-reach-difference-a-clear-factor
- 1,178 Nevada MMA fights, 2003-2010 (Estelami 2014): bigger reach edge, significantly more wins. [B, via MMASucka]
  - https://mmasucka.com/news/fight-science-reach-advantage/

Is the effect bigger at distance than inside?
- No direct number anywhere. [B]
- Hint from UFC knockouts: with a big height edge (+40 cm) the finishing punch is far more likely a straight (76% more than uppercut or overhand). The short man's finishers are hooks, overhands, uppercuts. [S abstract]
  - https://europepmc.org (Barley et al. 2025, 264 UFC KO/TKO, DOI 10.1177/17479541251338509)
- Hint from fights: the long man's edge lives in a band of distance. Inside that band reach is zero and weight, chin and angles take over (section 4).

Ape index (reach minus height)
- Across 81 famous pro boxers (my pull from Wikipedia infoboxes, all divisions): mean +2.2 in, sd 2.4. [S, my calc, biased to stars]
  - Light divisions (SB to LW) about +1.4. Heavyweights about +3.2 to +3.6. Welters +2.2. Middles +2.0.
  - Range runs from -3 (Marciano) and -2 (Hopkins) to +11 (Liston), +9 (Jennings), +7.5 (Douglas).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reach_(boxing) for the term.
- Typical gaps in famous fights (reach, infobox, +gap for the longer man): [S, approx]
  - Ali 78 / Frazier 73 (+5). Hearns 78 / Leonard 74 (+4). Hearns 78 / Hagler 75 (+3).
  - Pacquiao 67 / Margarito 72 (+5; Roach said 6) / De La Hoya 73 (+6).
  - Joshua 82 / Ruiz 74 (+8). Fury 85 / Usyk 78 (+7). Inoue 67.5 / Fulton 70.5 (Fulton +3).
  - Bivol 72 / Canelo 70.5 (+1.5, but 4 in taller). Tyson 71 / Douglas 83 (+12) / Lewis 84 (+13).
- Short man wins about half of these famous cases. That is selection bias (we remember upsets), but it says skill and plan can beat 5-8 in.

## 2. Jab range and distance control

- Reach (wingspan) is not jab range. Wingspan = two arms + shoulders. A jab uses one arm, plus shoulder turn and lean. [S, AgentMMA says the same]
- No source gives jab range as a share of reach. [searched, none] My geometry is in "For Last Bell" and marked as a guess.
- Wikipedia: out-fighters "need reach, hand speed, reflexes, and footwork." They keep distance with the jab and win on points. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing
- Wikipedia: taller men benefit from the mummy / long guard (lead arm out). Its weakness is uppercuts and straights that bypass the arm. [S]

CompuBox on tall jabbers
- Wladimir Klitschko vs Brewster (2007): 162 jabs landed in 6 rounds = 27 a round, "nearly five times" the heavyweight average. 346 jabs thrown = 58 a round. 80% of his punches were jabs. [S, Q]
  - Over Peter, Brock, Brewster: about 38 jabs thrown and 14 landed per round, double the division jab average; only 13 power shots a round (half the average). 75% of output was the jab.
  - https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/compubox-analysis-klitschko-vs-brewster
- Lennox Lewis: heavyweight record 213 jabs landed (vs Tua, 2000); 78% of his punches that night were jabs. [S, same page]
- Ike Quartey holds the all-time record, 313 jabs landed in one fight (not tall). [S] Jab volume is not only a tall man thing.
- Klitschko vs Joshua: Klitschko landed 4.5 jabs a round after averaging 8.7 into the fight. [B, ESPN snippet] The shorter-but-similar-reach man shut the jab down.
- Bivol vs Canelo: career jabs landed per round, Bivol 8.3, Canelo 3.5; Bivol landed 152 to 84 total and led every round. [B, snippet] Bivol is only 1.5 in longer in reach, 4 in taller. Jab quality and height both seem to matter.
- Lewis vs Tyson (2002): AP punch count Lewis 193 of 328, Tyson 49 of 211. Lewis "clinching and getting distance, then working the jab" and "a constant stream of jabs made Lennox almost unreachable." [S fightcity, B for the count]
  - https://www.thefightcity.com/?p=7054
- Tall man's style tendency: "offensively focused and defensively irresponsible"; the length is the defense. Leans back instead of moving his head; slow to recover punches. (MMA, but same logic.) [S, Q] https://mmasucka.com/news/james-vick-study-length/

How the long man controls distance (trainer / explainer sources)
- Jab and half-step back; if he gets inside, tie up and lean. [coach_a.md row 14, see also]
- Keep the short man on the end of the jab, step out after throwing, pivot. Fury: "control of the distance negated the danger"; feints and upper-body movement. [S] https://www.badlefthook.com/2020/2/15/21138741/wilder-vs-fury-2-preview-five-questions-ahead-of-rematch-date-ppv-fox-espn-boxing-news
- Tall men sit in a trap near the ropes or in a corner: they lose the use of their reach. [B, expertboxing]

## 3. The short man's plan

What trainers and sites say (mostly agree)
- Do not run straight in. The tall man trained for that. Get in by timing his jab, moving past it, and countering. [S, expertboxing] https://expertboxing.com/how-do-you-beat-a-taller-boxer
  - Quote: "pull your head down or to the side slightly and then focus on moving STRAIGHT into him with a counter punch."
  - Tall men recover the jab slowly: a right hand right after his jab gets there first.
  - Fake a lot (hands, head, feet) to make him throw, and to tire him.
  - Hit the body: "When you cut down a tree, you don't start at the top."
  - Throw harder (he often has less muscle at the same weight). Use volume: long arms tire.
  - Overhand rights work on tall men: they "aren't used to seeing punches come down at them." [B, evolve-vacation snippet]
- Wikipedia: short in-fighters bob and weave to get under and past the arm. Slipping past (not blocking) unbalances the opponent and frees the hands. In-fighters throw uppercuts with full bodyweight (Tyson). [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing
- Wikipedia style rock-paper-scissors: swarmers beat out-boxers, sluggers beat swarmers. Frazier was beaten by Foreman but gave Ali trouble. [S]
- Roach: "Size don't win fights, skill does." Before Margarito (5 in taller, 6 in longer): "The bigger he is, the better it is for us"; Pacquiao: "we will use our advantage in speed." [Q via search snippet, also in coach_b.md] [B]
- MMASucka: Tyson and Frazier show a reach gap "can be turned into a disadvantage" with forward movement and head movement. [S] https://mmasucka.com/news/fight-science-reach-advantage/

Cases where it worked
- Frazier vs Ali I (1971): Futch had Frazier work the body, come in low so Ali had to punch down, then throw the left hook when Ali's right dropped. Round-15 knockdown. [B, search summary; Futch quote "the minute you see his right hand come down, you throw the left hook"]
- Hagler vs Hearns (1985): Hagler (5'10", 75) "stormed Hearns from the opening bell", pinned him on the ropes, took the fight to a pocket where Hearns' 3 in of reach meant nothing. Hearns's legs "were gone" by round 2. Hagler won in 3. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Hagler_vs._Thomas_Hearns
- Leonard vs Hearns (1981): Hearns (78) was ahead on the cards after 12 against Leonard (74). Dundee: "You're blowing it, son." Leonard closed in round 13 and stopped him in 14. [B, ESPN snippet]
- Ruiz vs Joshua I (2019): Ruiz (74) vs Joshua (82). Ruiz 39-23 in power connects; Joshua landed 7 of 25 a round. Ruiz dropped him 4 times. [S] https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/anthony-joshua-vs-andy-ruiz-compubox-punch-stats
  - Joshua won the rematch by staying on the jab: 65 of 270 jabs landed. [B]
- Inoue vs Fulton (2023): Fulton (70.5) had 3 in on Inoue (67.5). "Range advantage gained him very little"; Inoue's head position and speed; stopped in round 8. [B, snippet]
- Pacquiao vs Margarito and De La Hoya: speed and angles. Plan to break down De La Hoya's body. [B]

Cases where it failed
- Tyson vs Lewis (2002): Tyson rushed in, Lewis clinched, got distance, jabbed. 49 landed of 211. [S/B above]
- Tyson vs Douglas (1990, +12 in reach): lost. [B, well known; jab reported as the key]
- Povetkin vs Klitschko (2013): Povetkin used "head movement and aggressiveness to get inside of Wlad early, but to little effect"; he tired and "became an increasingly reachable target." Gassed by round 6. [S, Q] https://www.thefightcity.com/?p=15058
- Cerrone vs Diaz (MMA): Cerrone failed to adjust for a 3 in reach gap, landed 33% to Diaz's 82%. [S] mmasucka link above.
- Lesson: it works when the short man has a chin, gas and a way in (feints, speed, power). It fails when he walks in straight, or the tall man ties him up and leans.
- Cost: the short man eats jabs on the way in and out. "You must cross the danger zone twice: once to enter and again to escape." [B, snippet]

## 4. The tall man who fights inside on purpose

Fundora (6'6", 80 reach, southpaw) [S unless marked]
- Reputation: "a fighter who rarely jabs, instead preferring to fight inside." Uses the long arms to make right angles, uppercuts and hooks. [Q, CBS] https://www.cbssports.com/boxing/news/sebastian-fundora-vs-carlos-ocampo-result-towering-inferno-outpoints-challenger-for-unanimous-decision-win
- His reason: opponents expect to work inside and rip the body, so he meets them there. Quote: "at first the opponents were surprised... I'm sure we're starting to think of plans for more of an outside game... that's fine too because then we can utilise our reach." [Q, Bad Left Hook] https://www.badlefthook.com/2020/9/9/21428612/sebastian-fundora-interview-life-junior-middleweight-inside-fighting-boxing-news
- Lubin: "I hit him with one uppercut and I saw that it rocked him. So, I did it again and again." Lubin dropped in round 2 by a right uppercut; Fundora dropped in 7. [Q, Round By Round] https://roundbyroundboxing.com/news/fonfora-stops-lubin-in-thriller/
- Ocampo: Fundora jabbed early, "tepid start", then dropped the jab in round 4 and went toe to toe. Distance gone, so Ocampo "no longer having to navigate his way past Fundora's jab" could land hooks and overhands. Fundora's heavy uppercuts won the power exchange. He flipped back to jab and straight left late. Ocampo went for "Fundora's long torso with body shots". [S, Q]
- Cost, Mendoza fight: stepping in to throw a left uppercut, Fundora ate a left hook that buckled him; a right hand put him down; stopped in 7. [S] https://www.cbssports.com/boxing/news/boxing-results-roundup-sebastian-fundora-suffers-stunning-tko-loss-shakur-stevenson-shines-with-tko
- Lesson for the sim: inside style = more output and power from a tall man, but his head is a long way up and his hooks counter him on entry, and he gives away the jab wall.

Paul Williams (6'2", reach 78-83 by source)
- Used the length differently: volume and circling. 1,256 punches vs Margarito (~105 a round); Margarito "had a lot of trouble with Williams' height... reach... and southpaw style" and "few occasions to jump in with counters." [S] https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/punishing-battle-williams-beats-margarito
- Vs Winky Wright: 90.5 punches a round to Wright's 42.6. [B]
- Margarito did "plenty of damage with his bodyshots." Tall man's body is a target. [S]

Hearns vs Hagler: Hearns planned to box and look for a late knockout; Hagler would not let him. Hearns slugged in round 1 and broke his right hand. [B/S mix] The tall man who gets pushed into a pocket loses legs fast.

Why a tall man goes inside: comfort, power from leverage (long levers, uppercuts), entertainment, and that the short man has trained for the outside game. What it costs: chin and head exposed on entry, no jab wall, body target, gas.

## 5. Leverage and clinching

What the cases say
- Fury vs Wilder 2 (2020): Fury 273 lb, Wilder 231 (career high). Fury used his weight as "a body rest" on the smaller man; wore him down; Wilder retired in 7. A ref warned Fury for leaning. [B, search summaries of Ring and Bad Left Hook]
- Fury vs Wilder 3: Bisping: Fury had a 30-pound edge. "Because Tyson is taller, Deontay's head was going under the armpit... Tyson was leaning on him... forcing Deontay to carry that 271 pounds. So, that's adding to the exertion." Referee warned "no more headlocks." "Whoever is the freshest... is going to win." [Q, S] https://www.sportskeeda.com/mma/news-tyson-fury-vs-deontay-wilder-3-michael-bisping-breaks-strategy-helped-the-gypsy-king-defeat-the-bronze-bomber
- Klitschko vs Povetkin (2013): Klitschko (6'6") jabbed in, then clinched, pressed down on the neck, leaned all fight. "Throughout the entire bout Wlad leaned heavily on Povetkin during clinches, an illegal tactic which had its intended effect as Povetkin was visibly gassed by the end of round six." Three knockdowns in round 7. Four in the fight. [Q, S] https://www.thefightcity.com/?p=15058
  - Ref Pabon penalized once, a point in round 11. Lewis tweeted "Lean On Me." Writer: Povetkin "would have had considerably more in the tank." [S] https://www.worldboxingnews.com/?p=24286
  - Another view: Povetkin often had free hands while being leaned on and did not punch. [B]
- Steward taught this to Lewis and Klitschko (Kronk lore): tie up, lean, sap energy. [B]
- Lewis vs Tyson: Lewis clinched to make distance; "stern warnings for holding." [S]

Rules
- Boxers are prohibited from holding, pushing, tripping (general rule). After a clinch is broken each man must take a full step back before punching. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing
- State rule (Michigan): holding or deliberately maintaining a clinch is a point-deduction foul; wrestling at the ropes is a separate foul. [B, search snippet] The ABC Unified text on fouls did not load.
- In practice refs vary: one warning, one point at most, often late. This is what the sim's REFS `hold`/`holdN` table models.

How much it tires the smaller man
- No study gives watts or heart rate for leaning. [searched, none]
- Amateur 3x3 bouts: heart rate >93% of max in rounds 1-2, >97% in the last; ~73% aerobic. So a fight is already near max effort. [B, search summary] https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4082828
- Accounts: Povetkin gassed by round 6 of 12; Wilder tired early (fan view). Povetkin was ~4 in shorter than Klitschko. Wilder was 2 in shorter and 42 lb lighter than Fury, so weight did most of it there.
- Plain model: the smaller man carries part of the other man's weight on neck, shoulders, back and legs. Plus he cannot punch back from under the armpit.

## 6. Height and defense

- Evidence is thin; most pages are low quality. [B]
- Claims: taller man is a bigger body target; the short man's bob and weave makes the tall man punch down and lose power; tall men lean back instead of moving the head. Only the last two have a named source (Wikipedia Boxing, Vick). [S]
- Uppercuts: short men throw them well from under; the tall man has to bend. Fundora is the tall exception: his arms are long enough to make angles. [S]
- Overhands on a tall man: tall men are not used to punches coming down on them. [B]
- Tall men's finishing punch is the straight: +40 cm height edge makes a straight KO far more likely than uppercut or overhand. [S abstract]
- Tall man often does not move his head ("length is their defense"). So when the short man gets in, the tall man is easier to hit than he looks. [S, Vick]
- Mummy and long guards help the tall man (discourage hooks and uppercuts) but let straights and uppercuts bypass the lead arm. [S Wikipedia]

## For Last Bell

Assumes: a punch lands if it reaches; reach adds range to his own punches; the brain picks range and plan by style; the clinch exists. All numbers are starting points. Guess = unmarked by data.

### A. Reach to range (engine)
- Give each fighter `ht` (in) and `reach` (in). Draw from the division table, with `reach = ht + ape`, ape ~ N(table, 2.4 in), clamp -3..+9.
- Jab range today is 78 for all. Make it per fighter: `J = 78 * (1 + 0.015 * (reach - 72))`. [guess]
  - Why: one arm is ~0.45 of wingspan, so 1 in of wingspan is ~0.5 in of jab. A jab from stance reaches roughly 34-37 in, so 0.5 / 34 = about 1.5% per inch.
  - Result: 66 reach = 71; 72 = 78; 78 = 85; 85 = 96. A 6 in gap = 7 units (9%).
- Scale every punch's range by the same factor, so hooks and uppercuts stay inside jab range. The free band (only the long man can hit) is `[J_short, J_long]`. It is small, so most of the edge comes from who gets in first and who has to step.
- Height: draw scale `ht / 70` for the sprite only. A 4+ in height gap shifts the short man's body-punch share up (+15%, guess) and the tall man's overhand/downward hook share up (+10%, guess).
- Do not add a flat "tall man bonus." Let range, footwork and the brain make the gap. Then check it against the targets below.

### B. How strong should it be? (sim calibration)
- Equal skill, same style, other things equal. Target win % for the longer man:

| Reach gap | Light divisions | Welter-middle | Light heavy-heavy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 in | 50 | 50 | 50 |
| 3-4 in | 51 | 52-53 | 54 |
| 5-6 in | 51-52 | 54 | 57 |
| 7-8 in | 52-53 | 56 | 60 |
| 10+ in | 54 | 58 | 62 |

  - Built from AgentMMA (excess at 3 in+: 1.6 light, 4.8 mid, 11.7 heavy) and roughly half of the raw boxing numbers (Fight Matrix 58.4% at 2+, 64.6% at 8+), because raw boxing is confounded by quality. [guess]
- Keep size smaller than a skill level. One level down wins 15.8-18% today. A 6 in gap should move a mirror match by about 4-7 points, not 30.
- Winners also land more and are ~8 points more accurate (PLOS). A good test: long man's connect % up 3-5 points, short man's output up.
- Re-run the audit after size: thrown ~51, landed ~15, connect ~29.5%, stops ~26% must stay put on average (the pool is mixed sizes, so means stay the same).

### C. Long man's brain (outboxer / boxer / volume)
- If `myJ - hisJ >= 4` and style is outboxer, boxer, volume or counter: hold the free band. Stay at `hisJ + 2 .. myJ - 1`. Jab share up (Klitschko: ~75% of output; guess +25% jab when he has the edge). Step or pivot out after a throw.
- Slow recovery: the tall man's recovery after a jab/straight is the short man's entry window. Give long arms a slightly longer recovery (+5%, from expertboxing, guess).
- If the short man gets inside (dist < 0.6 J_short): the long man's choice is
  1. tie up and lean (if heavier or taller by 3+ in, and the leaning cost below is on),
  2. pivot or step out to rebuild the band,
  3. stay and trade (only with the "inside on purpose" trait).
- Defensive habit: tall men "lean back" (pull the head straight back) instead of moving it. Model as a defense option that fails more to the overhand and hook than to straights. [from Vick; guess for numbers]
- Near the ropes or in a corner the long man's range edge is cut (he cannot step back). Reduce his `J` edge to half when his back is within 20 units of the ropes. [guess]

### D. Short man's brain (swarmer / pressure / counter)
- If `hisJ - myJ >= 4`: do not trade in the band. Plan = enter, stay on the chest, work the body, leave on your own terms.
- Entry triggers (pick one per approach, weighted by skill):
  1. right after his jab/straight starts or lands (his hand is out, recovery window),
  2. behind a feint or a fake jab (feint share +),
  3. a slip/duck past the jab (a "slip under" defense that on success moves him forward 8-12 units),
  4. when he is backed to the ropes (cut off the ring: step to his off-side).
- Do not walk straight in at constant speed. A straight walk-in costs: +20% chance he eats the jab on the way (Tyson-Lewis, Povetkin early rounds). [guess]
- Inside: body-punch share up (+15-25%), hooks and uppercuts up, cross and jab down. Follow him when he steps out (live on the chest). Overhand right when his lead hand is out or he pulls straight back.
- Gas: a pressure short man spends more stamina per round than the long man (entering, chasing). If he runs low, he cannot keep the plan (Povetkin).
- Danger: each entry costs a hit about as often as the long man's accuracy at range. That is the price; do not make it free.
- Skill levels: Novice short men walk straight in (the brain should mess this up). Pro and up use the triggers above. World level adds feints and slip-and-counter on entry.

### E. Tall man inside on purpose (trait `insidePref` 0-1, style "Fundora")
- Trait, not a style: a high `insidePref` tall man chooses the pocket. A low one boxes outside.
- Fundora pattern (Ocampo): jab early, then abandon it around round 4 and trade; flip back to the jab when ahead late. So: the brain flips by the cards. If losing rounds at range, or an opponent counterpuncher with slow feet, go inside. If winning, go back out.
- Benefits (guess): uppercut and hook damage +8% vs a short man when inside (long levers, angles); more output; the short man loses his entry window because he is already in.
- Costs (guess):
  - the jab wall is gone, so the short man's hooks and overhands land more (+15% hook/overhand hit chance vs him),

### F. Leaning in the clinch (leverage)
