## 4. Mind profiles

Each profile has: philosophy, read phase, if-then rules, a famous mid-fight adjustment, behavior in adversity, and a signature mental move.

### 4.1 Jesse "Bam" Rodriguez (118, Ring No. 4, ESPN No. 3)
- **Philosophy:** "You can go out and watch all the film in the world, and he can change his style up fight night. That's why I do no film studying of my opponents, and just go out there to see what they bring." [src: search summary of a Bam interview, exact outlet not confirmed]
- **Read phase:** live, rounds 1 to 3. He jabs, steps right, and tests the body to see how the opponent reacts. Trainer Robert Garcia gives the plan between rounds ("I like those body shots", round 2 to 3 vs Cafu) [src].
- **If-then rules:**
  - If the opponent covers the head, go to the body with the left (Estrada KO7, Cafu KO10) [src, fighters_b].
  - If the opponent attacks his body, answer with a stiff jab and an uppercut [fighters_b].
  - If the opponent is hurt, finish now (Vargas: down in round 5, finished in round 6) [src].
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Juan Francisco Estrada (2024-06-29). Estrada caught him in round 6 (one summary says a knockdown; unverified). Bam came out in round 7 and stopped him with a left to the body [src].
- **Adversity:** Garcia says he "loses focus for a split second", then recovers right away [fighters_b]. He answers being hurt with offense in the next round, not a shell.
- **Signature mental move:** no fixed plan. He decides from what he feels in the first rounds, so he is hard to game-plan against.

### 4.2 Junto Nakatani (122, Ring No. 7, ESPN about No. 10)
- **Philosophy:** He said he would need a knockout to beat Inoue ([SI](https://www.si.com/fannation/boxing/junto-nakatani-says-he-needs-to-knock-out-naoya-inoue-to-win-potential-super-fight)) [src title]. Finisher's mindset.
- **Read phase:** cautious when his corner tells him to box. His coach said of the Inoue loss, "we started too late" (lost rounds 1 to 4) [fighters_b, Ring].
- **If-then rules:**
  - If the opponent steps into range, intercept with the straight left or a left uppercut on the back step.
  - If pinned, brawl instead of escaping (Sebastian Hernandez, late 2025, out-landed 179-155 in rounds 7 to 12) [fighters_b].
  - If the respect phase is over, raise the volume sharply. He out-landed Inoue 48-34 in rounds 8 to 10 [fighters_b].
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Inoue (2026-05-02). He took over in the middle rounds and had Inoue on the back foot. A round 10 headbutt cut and an orbital fracture ended his run [src].
- **Adversity:** keeps trading when hurt or cut, and accepts damage to stay in the fight. He survived the Hernandez war.
- **Signature mental move:** the late surge. He stays in a fight he is losing and comes on hard in the second half.

### 4.3 David Benavidez (cruiser/175, Ring No. 5, ESPN No. 4 or 5)
- **Philosophy:** pure pressure and volume. "The constant, unremitting pressure" (WBC on the Zurdo fight) [src]. Trained by his father, Jose Benavidez Sr. [mem].
- **Read phase:** short. He pushes from round 1, and his reading is about which punches get through the guard, not whether to attack.
- **If-then rules:**
  - If the opponent covers up, keep throwing combinations and work around the guard (left, right, left finished Zurdo) [src].
  - If the opponent is a mover, cut the ring off and pin him to the ropes (Plant, 2023) [mem].
  - If the opponent is hurt, stay on him until the referee stops it.
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Caleb Plant (2023-03-25). Plant boxed well early. Benavidez stopped following him, cut off the ring, and took over the second half to win a UD [mem].
- **Adversity:** he steps forward when hit. He has never been badly hurt in a major fight [unverified].
- **Signature mental move:** he makes the fight a test of conditioning and will. Opponents fade under the volume.

### 4.4 Jaron "Boots" Ennis (154, Ring No. 9, ESPN about No. 7)
- **Philosophy:** "I can do it all" switch-hitting adaptability [paraphrase, unverified]. Trained by his father Derek "Bozy" Ennis [mem].
- **Read phase:** 1 to 3 rounds of stance tests, with the body as the probe. He finds what lands, then does more of it.
- **If-then rules:**
  - If one stance is getting countered, switch stance (often in the middle of a combo).
  - If the body is open, invest there until the opponent slows (Stanionis: hurt by a body shot in round 6, retired after 6) [src].
  - If the opponent is a runner, he chases and loses his shape (Chukhadzhian 1, 2024) [fighters_b].
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Stanionis (2025-04-12). After a competitive start he went to the body, a body shot hurt Stanionis in round 6, and a flurry put him on a knee. The corner retired him [src].
- **Adversity:** gets frustrated against a mover who will not trade. He showed a calmer version in the Chukhadzhian rematch [mem].
- **Signature mental move:** live A/B testing of stance and target, then a flurry finish (four knockdowns of Zayas) [src].

### 4.5 Devin Haney (147, Ring No. 8, ESPN about No. 8)
- **Philosophy:** win rounds and take no risks. Jab, score, clinch. "Boring" does not bother him [fighters_b].
- **Read phase:** none needed. He opens behind the jab and banks rounds 1 to 6.
- **If-then rules:**
  - If the opponent gets inside the jab, clinch [fighters_b].
  - If the opponent is a body puncher, answer with his own straight right to the body (50 to 9 body landed vs Lomachenko) [fighters_b].
  - If ahead on the cards late, cut output and clinch more.
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Lomachenko (2023-05-20). Loma rallied in rounds 9 to 11. Haney survived with the jab and the clinch and won a UD [mem]. vs Brian Norman Jr. (Nov 2025) he dropped Norman and won a UD (117-110, 116-111, 114-113) for his third division [src].
- **Adversity:** vs Ryan Garcia (2024) he was dropped three times by the left hook and could not adapt. That result was later changed to a no contest after Garcia's positive test [mem].
- **Signature mental move:** risk control. He accepts a dull fight if it keeps the 0.

### 4.6 Jai Opetaia (cruiser, Ring champion, ESPN No. 9, NEW on the ESPN list)
- **Philosophy:** grind and trust his conditioning. Southpaw pressure with long combinations [mem].
- **Read phase:** short. He sets a high pace early and lets the pace do the reading.
- **If-then rules:**
  - If the opponent tires late, sit down on power shots (Mikaelian: a short right hook in round 9, three more punches, then a standing count he could not beat) [src].
  - If hurt or injured, keep the pace and box rather than brawl [mem, Briedis].
- **Famous adjustment / adversity:** vs Mairis Briedis (2022-07-02). He broke his jaw in two places early (round 2 to 4 reports vary) and fought the remaining rounds to win a UD and take the IBF and Ring titles [mem].
- **Signature mental move:** pain tolerance and pace. He keeps going when injured, and opponents break first.
- **Next:** Benavidez is the obvious fight. Whether it is made is unknown.

### 4.7 Oscar Collazo (105/112, Ring No. 10, not on ESPN)
- **Philosophy:** go for the stoppage. Five straight KOs to June 2026 [src].
- **Read phase:** very short against lesser opponents (Valdez down three times in round 2) [src].
- **If-then rules:** if the opponent is outgunned, take him out early. If hurt early, survive first and then take over (a "survives early scare" win over Zapata) [src title, details unverified].
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Haro (2026-03-14). He out-landed him 157 to 57 and the corner stopped it after 6 [src].
- **Signature mental move:** moving up to fight at flyweight while still the 105 king. He looks for the bigger challenge.

### 4.8 Artur Beterbiev (175, off both lists)
- **Philosophy:** quiet professional. He rarely talks and says he lets his work speak [paraphrase, unverified]. Pure pressure with heavy single shots.
- **Read phase:** he walks forward from round 1, and his reading is about where to put the next heavy shot.
- **If-then rules:** if behind early, keep the same pressure and trust accumulated damage. If the opponent is hurt, finish (20 for 20 KOs before Bivol) [mem].
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Bivol I (2024-10-12). Bivol boxed well early. Beterbiev raised the pressure in the second half, won the late rounds, and took a majority decision [mem]. Bivol reversed it in the rematch (2025-02-22, MD) [src].
- **Adversity:** dropped by Callum Johnson (2018), got up and knocked him out in round 4 [mem]. Behind on cards vs Gvozdyk (2019), stopped him in round 10 [mem].
- **Signature mental move:** no panic. Being dropped or losing rounds does not change his plan.
- **Status:** no fight since February 2025 found. Next fight unknown [src].

### 4.9 Gervonta "Tank" Davis (135, inactive)
- **Philosophy:** patience, then one punch. He gives away early rounds to learn timing [mem].
- **Read phase:** rounds 1 to 4 he walks down, throws little, and watches what the opponent does under pressure.
- **If-then rules:** if the opponent leads with the jab, counter with the left. If the opponent is hurt, explode at once. If the opponent is hurt to the body, finish there (Garcia, a body shot in round 7, 2023) [mem].
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Leo Santa Cruz (2020). Behind a close fight, he ended it with a left uppercut in round 6 [mem].
- **Adversity:** vs Lamont Roach (2025-03-01). He took a knee in round 9 to wipe his eye and it was not ruled a knockdown. The fight was a majority draw [src]. Since then: WBA "champion in recess" because of legal issues. A January return against Kambosos is in talks [src].
- **Signature mental move:** the bait. He lets you win rounds until you get comfortable, then waits for one mistake.

### 4.10 Kenshiro Teraji (115, WBO champion)
- **Philosophy:** disciplined, jab-first boxing with a heavy right hand [mem].
- **Read phase:** 2 to 3 rounds behind the jab.
- **If-then rules:** if out-worked, increase output and go to the body. If the opponent has beaten him before, change the plan completely.
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Masamichi Yabuki. He lost by TKO10 (2021), then knocked him out in round 3 in the rematch (2022) by starting fast [mem].
- **Adversity:** lost a split decision to Sandoval (2025). He moved up and won the vacant WBO 115 title (UD over Israel Gonzalez, 2026-07-20), becoming a three-weight champion [src].
- **Signature mental move:** rematch learning. He treats a loss as data and comes back with a new plan.

### 4.11 Zhanibek Alimkhanuly (moving to 168)
- **Philosophy:** southpaw power puncher, Kazakh amateur pedigree [mem].
- **Read phase:** short. He pushes behind the right jab and left hand.
- **If-then rules:** if the opponent is hurt, finish (Gualtieri, 2024) [mem].
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Denzel Bentley (2022). He went 12 hard rounds and won a UD [src].
- **Adversity:** a meldonium finding (Nov 2025) cancelled the Lara fight. One-year WBO suspension. Gave up the 160 title and is moving up [src]. Results at 168 are unknown.
- **Signature mental move:** not enough sources to say. Keep generic.

### 4.12 Teofimo Lopez (147, Ring No. 3 at welterweight)
- **Philosophy:** confidence and power. He is famous for high-emotion fights and for arguing with his father/trainer in the corner [mem].
- **Read phase:** varies. He beat Lomachenko (2020) by winning early rounds while Loma was passive, then won a hard round 12 [mem].
- **If-then rules:** if he feels in control, he throws big shots. If frustrated, he forces things and loses shape.
- **Famous mid-fight moment:** after a close win over Sandor Martin (2022) he asked his father on camera whether he still "had it" [mem, wording unverified].
- **Adversity:** lost a split decision to Kambosos (2021) after being dropped in round 1 [mem]. Lost 119-109 x3 to Shakur (2026), out-landed 165 to 72 [src].
- **Signature mental move:** emotional swings. He can rise to a big moment or sink.
- **Next:** moving to welterweight. A Ryan Garcia fight was talked about but not official. Result unknown [src].

### 4.13 Dalton Smith (140, WBC champion)
- **Philosophy:** body puncher from Sheffield, trained by his father [mem, unverified].
- **Famous adjustment:** vs Subriel Matias (Jan 2026, Brooklyn). He stopped him in round 5 with a right hook to win the WBC title and go to 19-0 [src].
- **Adversity:** a camp injury ruled him out of the June 6 Puello defense [src]. Next fight unknown.
- **Signature mental move:** not enough sources. Keep generic.

Notes for tuning:
- **readSpeed vs adjustAfterRound:** Bam and Ennis are high on both (they solve it live). Beterbiev and Benavidez are low on both, but it does not matter much because their plan is pressure. Teraji reads slowly within a fight but adjusts a lot between fights. The game could model that as a rematch bonus.
