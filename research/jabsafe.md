# Jab safety: jabbing a counter-puncher (Oct 21, research for balance pass 1b)

The problem: our out-boxer eats the cross over his jab (17.6% of the counter's damage), the right hand as he backs out (10.8%), and jab-for-jab (8%). He loses the matchup 34-66. This file is the jabber's side. The attacker's side (how to counter a jab) is `research/jab.md`; the long guard is `research/longguard.md`; skillr tips on jabbing safe are in `research/skillr.md` (lines 11-24). Tags: [S] one source, [M] several agree, [?] unsure. Web articles are mostly small coaching sites; read them as coaching advice, not measurement.

## 1. Not eating the right hand over the jab
- **The hole.** A jab thrown from a low hand travels up and out, and the counter (a right over the top) can arrive before the jab does. The fix in the clip: start the jab from a higher hand, elbow near the chest, so the glove is already at the right level and gets there sooner. [S, HOW2BOXING]
- **Range matters for the hand height.** Same clip: at true long range the hands can sit further out, because he can't reach. Mid-range or inside, hands up high, because you are close enough to get hit. [S]
- **Lead shoulder up, chin tucked behind it, as the jab goes out.** This covers the chin so the overhand has a harder path. Named by several coaches. [M: Expert Boxing, Progressive Combat, a jab-variation breakdown]
- **Rear hand stays on the chin.** Drop it and you eat the counter jab or lead hook. [S Expert Boxing] The same idea is in our skillr notes: free hand up while you punch; Haney's dropped right hand was where Garcia's hook landed. [S, `skillr.md`]
- **Snap the jab straight back.** Don't leave it out. [S Expert Boxing] The Haney-Loma coverage says the extended jab was the worst part: Loma slipped under it while the jab arm blocked Haney's view, and punched around it. [S, via search summary]
- **Head off the centerline.** Jab with a small diagonal step to the outside so the head is not where the straight comes. Lomachenko, Usyk, Crawford and Canelo are named as users of this "offline" jab. [S Evolve, M with skillr "head off the center line"] The Progressive Combat version: push off the rear foot at about 45 degrees, aim for his side, away from his rear hand. [S] Direction note: Evolve says orthodox steps left, southpaw right, but it does not say how that lines up against his rear hand [?]. The geometry depends on stance matchup; in a 2D game, "off the line" is the useful part.
- **Do not stand still.** A stationary jabber is a target; jab on the move or on the way out. [S MartialTalk via search; Evolve counter article says stay defended while exiting, because counter men hit right after you finish a combination. S]
- **Exit sideways, not straight back.** Angling off or dropping level after the jab is described as less predictable than backing in a line; leaning back after a jab is called readable. [S, low-quality sources] Note the game's outboxer already steps back (`ret`); the sources favour an angle over a straight line.
- **Don't telegraph the jab.** Lean or step before it and the counter man times the movement. Start with the hand and let the step happen with it. [S Progressive Combat]
- **Balance.** Bent knees, weight centered; leaning forward on the jab makes you easy to counter. [S Expert Boxing]
- **Move the head after the jab.** Duran moved his head (slip, drop, level change) after almost every jab, so he was already moving when the return came. Barkley's cross over Duran's jab is the cautionary example. [S, Jack Slack via Vice]

## 2. Jabs that beat a counter-puncher
- **Double jab.** The second jab works on the same beat as the first, so a counter timed on jab one lands on air or a swatting glove. In the Progressive Combat drill, if he swats the first, the second has a clear lane, and the two steps carry you to his side. [S] A second source calls the double jab a safe exit that needn't land. [S Phantom Punch via search] Ali threw doubles and triples then moved off. [S Slack] The repo already has "same-hand doubles break the defense's rhythm" (`breakdowns.md` 10). [M]
- **Body jab.** The level change takes the head out from under the over-the-top shot. Wylie: a dipping body jab can make the opponent's jab sail over the lead shoulder. [S, via search] TSS on beating Mayweather: jab sparingly, feint first, aim at the body or chest instead of the head. [S] Mayweather reaching to parry a body jab turns his shoulder and shows his right hand (mmasucka). [S] Castillo and Maidana used body jabs and body hooks to bring his rear hand down, then went upstairs. [S] Inoue's finish on Fulton was a body jab then the right over the top. [S, 2 summaries] Risk: Wylie also says a low jab pulls the guard and eyes down, which suits a looping overhand for the jabber. The same dip leaves his head low and forward for a counter uppercut or hook [?, inference, no source].
- **Feint the jab, then jab.** Feints that look like the real mechanics get the bite; feint a few times and he may stop reacting, then fire the real one. [S Evolve] Counter men are exactly who must be feinted and whose reads must be concealed. [M: Wikipedia, Evolve, expertboxing] Feints are weaker vs a disciplined man and are a risk if overused. [S]
- **Counter the counter.** Ali faked the jab, drew his head back as Liston lunged, and his short right came over Liston's jab. [S Bloody Elbow via search; this is Ali as the counter man] Cotto threw a non-committal lead hook to pull Mayweather's head back, then jabbed off the new posture. [S mmasucka] Stop-and-go jab: throw a third of the way out, freeze, and finish straight; he swats at the fake. [S Progressive Combat]
- **Draw a reaction, then use it.** The jab that works against Mayweather-type defense is the one that makes him parry, pull or duck, and the opening is the reaction. A jab that just reaches is punished (Guerrero). His defense runs on pattern recognition, so disguise or sequence the jab, don't throw it on a set rhythm. [S mmasucka]
- **Range finder / touch jab.** Duran jabbed from touching range to draw reactions, then used them. [S Slack] Bivol keeps a pawing jab into Canelo's high guard (`longguard.md`). [S] Not an anti-counter by itself: no source says a pawing jab removes the cross, only that it is low commitment.
- **Step-in jab from outside his range.** Start about two inches outside his reach and take a small step as you jab; step size small, no reach. [S Expert Boxing] Idea: if you are outside his reach, he has to step to counter, which slows the return. [?, inference]
- **Change rhythm.** Holmes' jab was described as flicking in uneven groups (four, then three, then four). [S SI 1978, via search] Ali kept the lead hand always moving so its start was hidden. [S Slack] Skillr: set a pattern, then jab in the middle of a weight shift. [S, `skillr.md`]
- **Jab after he throws, not first.** Futch to Holmes vs Bey (Bey threw wide rights over the jab): stop waiting, lead with the jab, answer what comes back. Holmes crowded him, stepped to his right to land rights, and denied the room the wide punch needed. [S] This is "lead into the counter man's reply", not waiting for him.

## 3. The plan vs a counter-puncher
- **Make him lead.** Counter men wait for your punch. Draw the punch you want with feints, angles, pressure and openings. An aggressive counter man tends to answer a jab with a jab; a defensive one counters from the side you just punched from. [S Expert Boxing] Don't chase him to the ropes where he is comfortable; make him come after you. [S TSS]
- **Two schools, both in the sources.** (a) Stay out, give him nothing, single shots and feints, jab sparingly, body. [S TSS on Mayweather] (b) Don't match his slow pace: up the volume, cut the ring, keep punching while moving, stay defended on exit. [S Evolve] They disagree; Emanuel Steward's line was a hard pressuring jab. [S TSS quote] The TSS writer calls that a misconception because the jab is the pattern the counter man reads. [?]
- **Jab less, but make it count.** The TSS plan has no pumping jab: pumping gives the pattern. The mmasucka read is the same (Mayweather's reactions are a flowchart). [M, 2 pieces on the same man]
- **Take the lead when waiting stops working.** Fury vs Usyk (`breakdowns.md`, R4-7 plan): Fury switched to starting exchanges and leaving before the answer, making sure he was the last man punching. [S]
- **When to stop jabbing.** No source gives a rule. Closest: Fulton's looping counter to Inoue's jab worked a couple of rounds, then Inoue saw it coming, stepped into it with more jabs and made it too risky to throw. [S] And Haney, whose jab was a non-factor vs Loma early, went back to working off the jab in round 7. [S] Neither says "stop after N counters". [?]
- **Angles beat the straight line.** Angled footwork (shuffle, pivot, stance shift) throws counters off timing. [S Evolve] Opposite case: Munguia and Fundora got countered by staying in front of the man, in range, for too long. [S Boxing Explainers]

## 4. Real fights
- **Ali vs Liston II (Ali the counter man).** Liston leads a jab, Ali pulls his head back and the right goes over. The standard picture of the jab eaten by a right. [S, Bloody Elbow via search]
- **Mayweather vs Mosley.** Mayweather weights his lead foot and leans in to bait the jab, then pulls and counters. Mosley's head kept snapping back from Mayweather's own jab. [S mmasucka] Mayweather vs Pacquiao: Pacquiao's jab went 18 of 193; Mayweather's own 67 of 267. [S ESPN/BoxingScene]
- **Cotto, Castillo, Maidana vs Mayweather.** What worked: wait for the shoulder to open, jab to the body to lower the rear hand, lead hook to pull the head back then jab, attack both angles when he pivots. [S mmasucka]
- **Haney vs Lomachenko.** Haney's jab was a non-factor; Loma slipped under it. Haney's fixes: outside foot position, forearm shove when Loma pivoted, an over-pivot, a tight right uppercut to the body. [S Bloody Elbow, fightprimer]
- **Usyk vs Joshua.** Usyk circled mostly to his right (southpaw) to limit Joshua's right, kept his head off the line, and countered over the top once Joshua's jab became slow and tentative. [S Sky Sports via search] Usyk wins early rounds prodding the jab from different angles. [S]
- **Inoue vs Fulton (Fulton the counter man).** Inoue's jab up and down, body then head, and he stepped, pivoted and moved off after combos so he wasn't caught square. Fulton's jab-counter got read and then shut down. [S, 2 summaries]
- **Holmes vs Bey, Futch.** See section 2. [S]
- **Klitschko.** About 70% of his offense was the jab; the right hand was saved until he was sure of it; the clinch covered the inside. His reach meant opponents mostly could not close at all. [S CompuBox via boxingscene] Fury beat it with feints and movement that made him stop.
- **Hopkins vs Pavlik.** Hopkins stepped away and circled, taking away the setup for the right cross. [S]
- **Whitaker.** Circled while jabbing, set everything up with the jab, then played off his defense; 286 jabs landed vs Nelson (24 a round). Ronnie Shields guessed 85% of his jabs landed [?, trainer's estimate]. [S]
- **Ward vs Kovalev I.** Ward's jab (head and body, many ways) was his best punch; Kovalev dropped him with a counter right in round 2; Ward then used the clinch a lot. [S]
- **Bivol vs Canelo.** Bivol stayed outside on footwork, used a pawing/measuring jab, targeted the body. Tyson: when Bivol jabbed Canelo should have been slipping and moving. [S, via search]
- Not found: Lennox Lewis (nothing specific on jab safety), Hopkins's own jab vs Whitaker, Loma as jabber.

## 5. Numbers
- No source gives the share of jabs that get countered. CompuBox does not track counters.
- Jab connect vs a counter man is low: Pacquiao 18/193 (9%) vs Mayweather, Mayweather's opponents land 21% of power punches (lowest of qualified fighters, ESPN). [S via search, 2 pieces] Haney vs Loma: Loma 29/250 (12%), Haney 20/188 (11%). [S ESPN Deportes]
- For contrast, Foster vs Fulton (Dec 2025): Foster jabs 90/351 (25.6%), Fulton 25/270 (9.3%). [S CompuBox page, via search]
- Repo reference: real jab connect ~17-21%, HW titles 28% (`numbers.md`). Our sim ~27%.
- Klitschko about 70% jabs; Mayweather over half his throws jabs vs Pacquiao.
- Lerner (`jab.md`): a jab that keeps getting countered makes the jabber hesitant to throw it. The attacker's goal, and the thing our opponent memory already does.

## For Last Bell
Ranked by how much the sources back them and how directly they hit the right over the jab. The brain already has opponent memory that discounts a punch that gets countered, feints, `ret` (step-back after punches), a lateral `angleOff` after combos (angle fighter only), and defense picks slip/block/roll/pull/duck.
1. **Jab with the shoulder and rear hand home (a "tight jab" state).** Strongest and most direct: lead shoulder up, chin behind it, rear hand on the chin, snap back. [M] Engine: while the jab is out and for its recovery, the over-the-top cross at the jabber gets a block/cover bonus (his guard) or a damage cut. A sloppy or tired jabber, or one with a low-hand guard (handslow, philly), keeps the hole. Gas should widen the hole (HOW2BOXING: jabbing gets lower as he tires).
2. **Jab from a higher hand.** The cross over the top beats a jab that starts low. Make the jab start position matter: low-hand guards give the counter a shorter window to hit first. Long range: hands can be out, no hole. [S, direct]
3. **Step off the line on the jab (offline jab).** Extend `angleOff` to every outboxer jab, not only after combos, with a small lateral component and the head off the line. The cross at him then misses more often. [M] Cost: the jab is slightly weaker or shorter; the lateral move uses the ring.
4. **Retract fast; do not leave it out.** A jab recovery time (`ret`-like) that is short for a skilled man and long for a novice; the counter man's window is the extended jab. [M]
5. **Do not leave the straight line when stepping out.** A back-out in a straight line is a free target for the right (10.8% bucket). Prefer a diagonal/side step after the jab; add a small chance the counter man's right misses an angled exit. [S, low-quality sources, but consistent with Evolve, Hopkins-Pavlik, Inoue pivots]
6. **Double jab and body jab as the anti-counter jabs.** The second jab beats a swat/counter timed on the first; a dipped body jab takes the head out from under the cross. Give the outboxer `pickCombo` entries (jab-jab, body jab then head) and a lower counter chance on the second. [M for double, S for body]
7. **Feint the jab to bait the counter, then go.** Feint to see the bite, then the real jab or step in. The counter man's counter-on-feint should fall back to a miss (we have feints and the counter man's read). [M] Needs the feint to look like the real mechanics, so a feint should draw his counter at a rate like a real jab for low-level men, lower for high-level.
8. **Jab less vs a man who counters it (plan).** TSS and mmasucka both say pumping the jab feeds him the pattern. The brain's memory discounts countered punches; extend that to a deliberate drop in jab share vs a counter style, with the outboxer switching to feints and single shots until he leads. Balance: don't also kill outboxer volume (the sim's outboxer is already low in the styles table). [M, but the two schools disagree on pressure vs patience]
9. **Take the lead when waiting stops working.** Fury vs Usyk and Futch vs Bey: after a couple of countered rounds, lead behind the jab, crowd him, and become the last man punching. Use the between-round `adjust` (`breakdowns.md`, adjustments answer each other): "countered over the jab" picks a plan from this list. [S]
10. **Reality check.** Real jabbers still lose a lot to counter men (Pacquiao 18/193, Haney 20/188). So the target is "the 34-66 gap narrows", not "outboxer wins". Maybe 42-45% would be the believable range for a careful outboxer vs a counter style; no source gives a win rate. [?]
