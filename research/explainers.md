Country schools (WdeH, a flavour layer for fighter generation, not new styles):
- American: slick defense, shoulder roll, switch, adapts mid-fight (Mayweather, Crawford).
- Mexican: pressure, body hooks, takes shots to land harder, defense serves the attack (Chavez, Canelo).
- Filipino: speed, awkward angles, volume, stamina; Donaire is the counter version (Pacquiao).
- British: upright, jab first, straight punches, range control (Lewis, Joshua).
- Cuban: back foot, slips, point scoring, low risk (Rigondeaux, Stevenson).
- Soviet: pendulum step, angles, pivots, breaks rhythm, a safe zone in the ring (Lomachenko, Usyk).
- Japanese: compact, tight guard, pressure, high volume, steady form round 1 to 12 (Inoue, Hasegawa).
- Puerto Rican: long jab then the left hook, body work (Trinidad, Cotto).
- Argentine: grit, awkward rhythm; low-hand mover (Martinez) or looping brawler (Maidana).
- Thai: Muay Thai base, southpaw pressure, stiff jab, conditioning (Galaxy).
- Irish: pressure, stamina, work rate, emotional drive (McGuigan, Katie Taylor).
- German: jab and clinch, safety first, upright (Klitschko).
Use: a country tag could bias a generated fighter's style, guard and stamina. Cheap flavour.

Lacking in our game (flag):
- Hands-down stillness defender (read-based, no blocks). Closest is handslow.
- Out-of-order combo puncher (rear hand first, hook on the way out; Jones). We have no sequence randomizer for a style.
- Off-balance extender (leans through punches; Navarrete): no balance system.
- Low-output boxer as a deliberate style (we have the output knob, not a "wins by not getting hit" pattern that judges can punish).
- Walk-in, head-still straight-line pressure (Matias, Cruz): our pressure boxer may be close; check head movement.

## B. Weaknesses and flaws

One named flaw per real fighter, in the form "habit -> how it gets found". Best candidates for a "real flaws on real fighters" feature are marked with *.

L4as (Every Boxing Weakness): categories one-dimensional, low output, bad chin, predictable, bad defense, slow feet, no power, poor stamina, emotional.
- One-dimensional * Wilder: all in on the right hand. Parker changed distance and never gave a still target; Wilder had no jab or combos to fall back on. Exploit: shut the one weapon, move. (L4as, WmwW)
- One-dimensional (entries) Romero: explosive entries and power shots. Tank watched, then stepped back and countered the entry. Exploit: time the rush. (L4as)
- Low output * Stevenson / Rigondeaux: defend well, throw too little, so close rounds are lost or boring. Judges need something to score. Exploit: nothing in the ring, but a bold opponent steals the cards. (L4as)
- Bad chin * Khan, Price: one clean shot ends it; once known, opponents take more risks. Exploit: land one. (L4as)
- Predictable entry * Lemieux (loads big shots stepping forward), Ryan Garcia (rushes in on his attacks): Saunders moved off the line; Tank counter-timed the entry. Exploit: wait for the same entry, counter it. (L4as)
- Bad defense * Rios (walks into combos), Fundora (tall, stays in range): Pacquiao hit and moved; Mendoza countered a lapse. (L4as)
- Slow feet * Joyce (stays on the line of Zhang's southpaw left), Munguia (attacks from straight in front of Canelo). Exploit: hit the same lane, uppercut a straight-on man. (L4as)
- No power * Malignaggi, Algieri: the other man walks in without fear. Exploit: pressure freely. (L4as)
- Poor stamina * Price (fades by round 5), Chisora (pressure fades when made to chase). Whyte fades mid-late; Briggs after round 5 (2PeR). Exploit: make him work, survive early, hit late.
- Emotional * Mayorga (stands and trades, hands down), Teofimo (impatience, dropped by Kambosos in R1). Exploit: bait the ego. (L4as)

2PeR (Every Bad Habit):
- Drops hands after punching * Benavidez (after combos), Gatti, Ryan Garcia (wide low hook load), Tank (lead hand hangs low after a hook; Roach dropped him). Exploit: counter right after his last punch. (2PeR; also jauX: Garcia vs Tank R2, Donaire vs Darchinyan)
- Leans straight back * Broner, De La Hoya late, Jermell Charlo, late Canelo. Exploit: follow with a right hand along the same line. (2PeR)
- Telegraphs * Frazier's hook dip, Bowe's wide right, Spence's hook load, Lewis's shoulder twitch (Rahman KO). (2PeR)
- Weak jab * Roy Jones late (no jab to fall back on), Tyson (jab only as entry), Stevenson (jab precise but light). Exploit: walk through the jab and press. (2PeR)
- Bad breathing / gas * Whyte, Briggs. (2PeR)
- Planted feet * Foreman, Andy Ruiz, GGG. Exploit: circle, jab, reset (Joshua rematch). (2PeR)
- Over-committing power * Wilder (falls in), Shavers. Exploit: wait for the miss, counter. (2PeR)
- Stands still after combos * Teofimo (hands drop after bursts; Martin dropped him), Fury (lingers after a 3-punch combo; Ngannou). (2PeR)
- Straight-line attack or retreat * Klitschko, Kovalev, Shavers. Exploit: lateral circling, angles (Fury, Ward, Lyle). (2PeR)

jauX (9 Habits):
- Dropping hands after punching (above).
- Swinging too hard: loaded shoulder, feet set early (Wilder vs Parker; Foreman vs Ali, who rolled, tied up, punished slow wide punches by round 8).
- Backing straight up * Lemieux vs GGG: GGG follows the jab down the same road to the ropes.
- Reaching instead of stepping * Marquez vs Mayweather: arm travels ahead of feet; left hook drops him.
- Moving without punching * Ennis vs Chukhadzhian, Callum Smith vs Canelo: the chaser walks forward for free.
- Following instead of cutting off * Hatton vs Mayweather: arrives square, gets tied up, check hook R10.
- Fighting every exchange * Khan vs Garcia (stays after landing), Teofimo vs Kambosos.
- Chasing the finish too early * Jermain Taylor vs Pavlik: wide punches, tired, countered in R7.
- Proving a punch did not hurt * Gatti vs De La Hoya (face swelling); Pacquiao avoided it vs Margarito.

pJRI (Bad Instincts), each is a decision rule that can be coded:
- Revenge: hit hard, tries to pay it back at once, leaves plan (Tyson vs Holyfield II). 
- Finish: hurts a man and rushes; burns gas, hurt veteran clinches and survives, then lands (Joshua vs Klitschko R5-6).
- Chase: follows instead of cutting the ring; the mover picks the exits (Foreman vs Jimmy Young).
- Panic: hurt man jumps up too fast and stumbles, ref stops it (Judah vs Tszyu).
- Knockout: waits for one big punch; becomes predictable (Lemieux vs Saunders).
- Toughness: absorbs to prove it (Rios vs Pacquiao).
- Show-off: hands low, plays confidence (Jones vs Tarver II, left hand low on the ropes, straight left KO).
- Brawling: leaves the box plan to trade with the harder puncher (Harrison vs Charlo II).
- Escape: backs up in a straight line to the ropes (Joe Smith vs Bivol).
- Desperation: the losing man looks for one big opening and sometimes finds it (Povetkin dropped Whyte with a left uppercut R5 after being dropped twice).

WmwW also gives a flaw per fighter (see D): Khan only speed; Ruiz lazy (30 lb heavier, passive defense); Ryan Garcia throws the hook before he is in position, hand drops; Tank waits too long to engage; Plant scores but does not hurt; Cruz pressure with no jab; Taylor squares up mid-combo and clinches on resets; Spence rigid, cannot adjust; Joshua freezes half a second when it gets messy; Benavidez heavy feet, straight-line; Hamed no system behind the reflexes; Stevenson will not take risks; Bivol is predictable in rhythm; Ennis stacks too much variety; Lomachenko starts slow.

Best for a flaw system (ranked by how clean the exploit is in a sim):
1. Hands drop after punching (Benavidez, Gatti, Garcia, Tank): counter in the half second after his last punch. Fits the existing `same` counter flaw.
2. Straight-line retreat (Lemieux, Broner, Charlo): follow behind the jab; a pull-back with no side step leaves the head in one place.
3. Telegraph with a tell (Frazier dip, Lewis shoulder, Spence hook, Bowe wind-up): a per-punch tell the brain can read at higher levels.
4. Stands after a burst / lingers (Teofimo, Fury): counter at the end of combos.
5. Predictable entry (Lemieux, Romero, Garcia): counter the same entry.
6. One-weapon (Wilder): shut it down by distance, he has nothing else.
7. Pace flaws: fades by round 5 (Briggs), mid-late (Whyte), gas in pressure fighters (Chisora).
8. Emotional (Mayorga, Teofimo): trades when stung or taunted; link to the mind games in E.
9. Slow feet (Joyce, Munguia): same lane gets hit again.
10. Weak jab (late Jones, Tyson): the other man ignores it and presses.

## C. Counters and defenses

Thin: the counters and defensive-styles videos were blocked. What the pulled videos show:
- Check hook against a charging man: Hatton R10 vs Mayweather. Beats rushing pressure that arrives square. (jauX)
- Counter left over a rushing right: Tank vs Romero R6; step back then short left. Beats a rushed right-hand entry. (L4as)
- Pull-back counter (Mayweather): lean about 4 inches, right hand down the middle while the other arm is still out. Beats a power man loading the right (Mosley). Weak when copied without footwork: Broner, De La Hoya late, Charlo, late Canelo get timed (2PeR). (sl0s)
- Jab intercept (Crawford): right hand beats the jab by reading the lead shoulder; short right over the top. Beats a jabber with a rhythm. (sl0s)
- Counter over the jab hole: the biggest hole is jabbing with the hand low; the rear hand goes over. Fix is the lead elbow near the chest in midrange. (ntuV (other); also 2PeR on weak jabs)
- Pivot reset (Usyk): plant the lead foot, hinge the rear foot around, jab from the new angle. Beats a loaded right hand. (sl0s)
- Angle step after punching (Rigondeaux): diagonal step, ends in the blind spot. Beats a straight-line chaser. (sl0s)
- Head-dip faint into straight left (Pacquiao): dips as if to the body, elbow drops, straight left upstairs. Beats a man who covers ribs on instinct. (sl0s)
- Peekaboo bob and weave with faint (Tyson): dip is the faint; read what the other man does, hook up. Beats a man who commits to a punch or guard shift. (sl0s)
- Canelo's one wind-up for body and head: the other man guesses the level. Beats a guard that must pick. (sl0s)
- Bump uppercut (Ajagba): lean on a clinching heavy, step back, uppercut up the gap he made. Beats a smotherer. (sl0s)
- Lead-hand frame (Ward): forehead, shoulder, neck hook, glove pin; takes away sight, rotation, retreat. Beats a hard puncher who needs to load. (sl0s)
- Shoulder roll / Philly (Mayweather, Toney): lead shoulder deflects, counter off the roll. Beats a straight attacker without volume; volume and pace (Calzaghe) break it. (WdeH, 2HR_)
- Slip, roll, step across the front foot into the hook (Frazier). (2HR_)
- Back-foot slips and counters (Cuban). (WdeH)
- Rope-a-dope (Ali vs Foreman): lean back on the ropes, roll, tie up, wait for the slow wide punches. Beats a one-gear puncher that never adjusts. (jauX, 4sM3)
- Tying up / clinching (Klitschko, Hopkins, Young). Beats a chaser; Calzaghe threw through the clinch gaps. (WdeH, 2HR_)
- Ice-cold recovery: take the count, tighten defense, watch entries, counter the next wide attack (Inoue vs Nery). (9OQu)

Defenses by name from the pulled videos: shoulder roll, Philly shell, peekaboo, pull-back, pivot, slip and counter, low-hands read (Martinez, Locche), long guard (other). Which beats which, in short: pull-back is beaten by following with no side step; long hand is beaten by body shots, guard manipulation, rear-hand side (other).

## D. The levels of skill (WmwW)

Seven levels, the channel's own scale, from worst to best. Our four map as noted.

1. Trash: wild, off-balance, no defense, gasses in two rounds, thinks he is good (Mayorga, Jake Paul, McGregor's boxing). Maps to Novice.
2. Bad: power hides bad habits. Wins by knockout, loses the exchanges between; one trick (Wilder power, Khan speed, Ruiz lazy). Maps to low Novice / high Novice.
3. Mid: real skill, can't hold control once adjusted to; looks elite in moments (Ryan Garcia, Tank, Plant, Cruz). Maps to Intermediate.
4. Skilled but flawed: real craft, flickers; depends on rhythm, cannot adapt (Teofimo, Taylor, Spence, Joshua, Benavidez, Hamed). Maps to high Intermediate to Pro.
5. Professional / master: polished, consistent, handles any style (Canelo, Stevenson, Bivol, Ennis, Usyk, Lomachenko); each has one limit (Canelo vs range, Stevenson no risk, Bivol predictable, Ennis excess). Maps to Pro and World class.
6. Great: controls pace, distance and emotion; redesigns the fight mid-way (Crawford, Inoue, Pacquiao, Tyson, Fury). Maps to World class.
7. GOAT: redefines boxing; defense, tempo, IQ (Ali, Robinson, Hopkins, Whitaker, Hagler). Past World class.

What separates levels, as the video frames it:
- Trash to bad: balance and defense exist at all.
- Bad to mid: more than one weapon; stamina.
- Mid to skilled: setups and angles, but still pattern-bound.
- Skilled to professional: adjusting when the other man changes. Skilled men freeze or reset under pressure (Joshua), or cannot change the plan (Spence).
- Professional to great: shifting plans mid-fight, controlling rhythm, not just distance.
- Great to GOAT: reinvention and time/IQ control.
For our four levels: the useful axis is how fast he adapts and whether he repeats his entry (WmwW). A Novice can't switch tools; Intermediate repeats patterns; Pro adjusts but has one limit; World class changes the fight itself.

## E. Mind games and IQ

9OQu (mind games):
- Pretend not tired: stay standing in the break (Foreman), push-ups in the corner (Hopkins vs Pascal R7). Shows no fatigue to the other corner.
- Exaggerated feint (Kovalev's hip/crouch body feint vs Cleverly): works because the faint comes from a puncher; the reaction tells him if the head or body is open. Useless without power.
- Talking in the fight (Mayweather vs Berto): speaks after the other man misses; wide win 232 of 410 vs 83 of 495.
- The stare (Tyson vs McNeeley): still face, then dropped twice in under 90 seconds. Pre-fight only.
- Smiling after a hit (GGG vs Canelo R5): the puncher gets no sign it landed.
- Personal taunt just before the bell (Tarver on Jones: "got any excuses tonight?"): Tarver KO'd Jones R2 with a counter left.
- Ice cold after a knockdown (Inoue vs Nery): up at once, defend, wait for the next wide attack, knocked him down R2.
- Ignoring the entrance (Collins vs Eubank) with headphones; a fear-blocking story.
- Clowning (Augustus): dances after a miss, throws from the same loose motion; the rhythm keeps changing.
- Inviting the punch (Mayorga vs Trinidad): backfires, he gives up defense, balance and surprise. See B.

- Canelo wind-up that looks the same for body and head (GGG R4 body, R8 head).
- Crawford jab intercept (rear hand beats the jab by reading the lead shoulder).

## G. Out-boxers

From 4sM3: the whole game is making you miss and making you pay. Ali (footwork, reflex, rope-a-dope), Holmes (the jab controls every fight), Plant (clean range and angles), Mayweather (shoulder roll, distance, reads your next move).

Weak spots (L4as, WmwW, 2PeR, jauX):
- Low output costs rounds (Stevenson, Rigondeaux).
- Moving without punching gives the chaser a free walk (Ennis vs Chukhadzhian, Smith vs Canelo).
- Straight-line retreat (Klitschko).
- Pull-back with no footwork (Broner, Charlo).
- Light jab: pressure walks through it (Stevenson, Plant).
- Reaching with the arm (Marquez).
- Fades when men walk through his jab (Plant).

How an out-boxer avoids being countered over the jab (other channels):
- Jab with the lead elbow near the chest in midrange, not from low. The low jab leaves a hole above it for the right hand (ntuV).
- Move the head after the jab, step-jab with a slip, double jab, jab with an intention (gKKK).
- Long guard: lead hand out to control and block lanes (the shorter man can do it too, TOwf). Keep contact light so the other man throws; then counter (TOwf). The trade-off: a long guard gives up the lead hand as a weapon; Tank held Garcia to 3 of 21 jabs in R1 and nothing landed in R2 (TOwf).
- Beat the long guard with: feints, guard manipulation (peel the lead hand, outside parry), body work, the rear-hand side (s3d6). These are what an opponent does to an out-boxer who leans on it.

Out-boxer sub-types seen: low-output defender (Stevenson, Rigondeaux), jab-controller (Holmes, Plant), reflex/slick (Ali, Mayweather), angle stepper (Rigondeaux, Usyk, Lomachenko), long-guard hand fighter (Tank, Ward).
