# Fighter DNA cards, batch E (105 to 122 lb: Japanese and Mexican wars)

Research for Last Bell's per-fighter AI knobs, from Ed's Reddit thread on "the most exciting active fighters". Researched Oct 2026. In-game names will be altered. Same format and tags as `fighters_a.md`, but shorter cards.

**How this was gathered.** Only web search summaries were used, because fetches of boxing sites are blocked. Tags: [src: site] means a search summary of that site, [snippet] means search-result text whose outlet is unclear, [mem] means from memory and not re-checked, and (est.) means my own judgement. Results from champinon.info are single-source and often stale: treat them as "probably". Punch numbers: 1 jab, 2 cross, 3 lead hook, 4 rear hook, 5 lead upper, 6 rear upper, b = to the body. Brain trait values are all (est.) unless the text says otherwise.

**Status note.** Several names in this batch are past their peak or coming off losses (Nery, Ioka, Julio Cesar Martinez). The thread picked them for their *style*, not their current form, and the cards describe the style.

## 1. Seiya Tsutsumi ("the late-rounds grinder who won't go away")
**118 (bantam), orthodox [mem], Japan (Kumamoto).** Status: 13-0-3 (8 KO) [src: champinon/Wikipedia]. Took the WBA belt from Takuma Inoue by UD on 2024-10-13 (117-110, 115-112, 114-113) [src: ESPN, BoxingScene]. Drew Daigo Higa 114-114 three times on 2025-02 [src: Ring]. Beat Donaire by split decision on 2025-12-17 [src: Bad Left Hook, Ring]. In 2026 the WBA made him "champion in recess" for a medical condition and elevated Antonio Vargas [snippet, unverified detail].
- **Style:** a volume pressure fighter who is busy without being sharp early: "circling and poking", beaten to the punch in rounds 1 to 4 [src: Ring/BLH on Donaire]. From round 5 he walks forward behind the jab, throwing 2- and 3-punch combos and investing in the body; he "did damage to the body in the sixth" vs Inoue [src: 3kings]. Mid range. Punch diet (est.): ~55-65/rd, jab 35%, body 25%. Signature: 1-2-3b, 2-3-2, then planting his feet for a straight right (2) counter when backed into a corner (the Higa round 9 knockdown) [src: Ring].
- **Why exciting:** Both of his title fights turned into wars. Ring Round of the Year 2025 (Higa round 9): Higa dropped him with a left hook, then Tsutsumi planted and floored Higa with a straight right, and nearly finished him [src: Ring]. He was badly rocked by Donaire in round 4 and still won the fight [src].
- **Mind:** He is patient and starts slowly, then grinds and ramps his work rate in the second half. He is composed when hurt, and he answers damage by firing back rather than covering up. Flaw: slow starts, and he is hittable by a cleaner, faster puncher early (Donaire won the early rounds with the jab and right hand) [src]. Being outworked is not one of his flaws.
- **Library:** T. Inoue 2024 (W UD12; took over from round 5, with a disputed round 10 count). Higa II 2025 (D12; both men down in round 9). Donaire 2025 (W SD12; rocked in round 4, won rounds 6 to 9 with combos).
- **Knobs:** output .75, jab .55, power .55, body .65, combo .65, initiative .7, patience .5, shell .4, slip .4, legs .5, clinch .3, composure .85, risk .6, killer .6, range .45, circle .35. **Base: swarmer (pressure boxer). Guard: standard.**
- **Sim hook:** a "slow start, late surge" pace curve, where output and accuracy rise by round (×0.85 in rounds 1-4, ×1.15 from round 6).

## 2. Masamichi Yabuki ("the finisher who saves it in the 12th")
**112 (fly), orthodox [mem], Japan. Tall for the weight [mem].** Status: IBF flyweight champion. Stopped Felix Alvarado in round 12 on 2025-12-27, then beat Rene Calixto by UD (118-108 x2, 116-110) on 2026-06-06 with two knockdowns [src: Ring, boxingonly, Wikipedia]. Record about 20-4 (18 KO) [snippet; sources disagree].
- **Style:** a rangy pressure puncher with a KO ratio of about 90% [snippet]. He uses the jab and body work to stay in fights, and his heavy left hook (3) and straight right (2) do the damage. Finishing pattern: 1 then 2 (the Alvarado KD in round 12) [src: Ring]. Mid to long range. The Calixto win was "too slick", a boxing performance [src: Ring], so he can box as well as brawl.
- **Why exciting:** late rescues. He was behind or level against Alvarado until a round 11 left hook knockdown and a round 12 finish [src: Ring "repeats rescue mission"]. In 2021 he took Teraji's WBC 108 title by TKO 10, unloading volume when Teraji came on late and seemed close to finishing him [snippet]. His stoppages come in deep rounds.
- **Mind:** He keeps the same intent through 12 rounds and stays dangerous as long as the fight lasts. When the opponent opens up late, he trades instead of holding. Flaws: he can be outworked early by a busy roughhouser (Alvarado landed in bunches early) [src], and Teraji destroyed him in round 3 of the rematch when Teraji came out fast (2022) [src: WBC]. An early storm is the way to beat him.
- **Library:** Teraji I 2021 (W TKO10, the upset). Teraji II 2022 (L KO3). Alvarado 2025 (W KO12: KD round 2 with a left hook, KD round 11 with a left hook, finished in round 12 with 1-2). Calixto 2026 (W UD, 2 KDs).
- **Knobs:** output .65, jab .6, power .75, body .55, combo .55, initiative .6, patience .6, shell .45, slip .35, legs .5, clinch .3, composure .7, risk .6, killer .85, range .6, circle .4. **Base: boxerpuncher. Guard: standard.**
- **Sim hook:** a "late killer" profile, where KO hunting rises when behind on the cards in rounds 10-12 (killer ×1.3, risk ×1.2).

## 3. Kazuto Ioka ("the body-shot craftsman, Japan's first five-division hunter")
**118 now (was 108-115), orthodox, Japan.** Status: L UD12 to Takuma Inoue on 2026-05-02 at the Tokyo Dome (120-106, 119-107, 118-108; down in rounds 2 and 3) [src: boxingnews.jp, BOXSPORT]. Before that he won KO4 over Maikel Ordosgoitti on 2025-12-31 in his bantam debut, with body shots [src: Ring, BLH]. He lost twice to Fernando Martinez (2024, and 2025-05) [src: Ring].
- **Style:** a counter-puncher and body puncher behind a tight high guard [mem]. Signature weapons: the left hook to the body (3b) and uppercuts. "Savvy shot selection, particularly uppercuts and hooks to the body" [src: Ring]. When his head counters don't land, he switches to the body [src: Ring]. Versus T. Inoue (CompuBox): 105/492 landed, 21%, so ~41/rd thrown (calc). He was out-jabbed 82-28 but **won the body count 44-12** [src: CompuBox via search]. Body share of landed was ~42% (calc). Signature combos: 3b-3, 5-3b, and a counter 3 off the opponent's 2.
- **Why exciting:** the liver-shot finish (Ordosgoitti, "gut-churning") [src: BLH], and his body attack gets stronger as a fight goes on [src: BLH]. A veteran's savvy against younger power. He dropped F. Martinez with a counter hook in round 10 of the rematch [src: BLH].
- **Mind:** a patient, read-first craftsman with medium risk. He adjusts by changing levels. He is now 37 [mem] and slower: fast straight punchers beat him to the punch (T. Inoue's right hand off the ropes and his right uppercut) [src]. High-volume pressure (Martinez) also outworks him.
- **Library:** Ordosgoitti 2025 (W KO4, body barrage). F. Martinez II 2025 (L UD, KD'd Martinez in round 10). T. Inoue 2026 (L UD, down twice, but won the body war). [mem] Older classics include the 2012 unification with Akira Yaegashi and wins over Donnie Nietes-era rivals; these are not verified here.
- **Knobs:** output .5, jab .45, power .6, body .85, combo .55, initiative .4, patience .75, shell .7, slip .45, legs .4, clinch .4, composure .8, risk .4, killer .65, range .4, circle .35. **Base: counter (body counter). Guard: high.**

## 4. Luis "Pantera" Nery ("the reckless southpaw bomber")
**122 (super bantam), southpaw, Mexico (Tijuana).** Status: L TKO4 to Johnriel Casimero on 2026-06-06 in Tokoname, Japan. He was down 6 times (3 in round 1, starting with a double left hook to the body) [src: boxingnews.jp, Spin.ph, Philstar]. He missed the 124 catchweight and got a 6-month JBC suspension [src: Spin.ph]. Record 37-3 (28 KO) [src]. The year before, he beat Kyonosuke Kameda TKO7 (2025-02) [snippet].
- **Style:** a stalker who touches to close distance, then opens up with power [src: BoxingScene]. Signature weapons: the straight left (2) and the right hook (3 for a southpaw). The Yamanaka rematch ended with a right hook in round 2 after four KDs [src: Ring TV]. Counter left hook against an aggressor: he dropped Inoue in round 1 (2024) [fighters_a]. Mid range. Body: moderate. Diet (est.): ~50/rd, jab 25%, power 75%.
- **Why exciting:** knockdowns both ways. He floored Naoya Inoue, and then got dropped three times and stopped in round 6 [fighters_a]. He was dropped 6 times by Casimero. Nearly every Nery fight has a knockdown.
- **Mind:** He takes the initiative with very high risk and wants to "fight in his own aggressive style" [src: BoxingScene]. His killer instinct is strong when he lands first. Composure when hurt is poor: he keeps trading instead of tying up (Casimero round 1: three KDs in one round). Discipline flaws: he missed weight twice (forfeited the WBC belt on the scale vs Yamanaka in 2018 [src], and again in 2026) and failed a drug test vs Yamanaka in 2017 [mem]. He struggles with a southpaw who will trade back (Figueroa KO'd him in round 7, 2021, with body work [src/mem]).
- **Library:** Yamanaka I 2017 (W TKO4, straight lefts). Yamanaka II 2018 (W TKO2, 4 KDs). Figueroa 2021 (L KO7). Inoue 2024 (L TKO6; dropped Inoue in round 1). Casimero 2026 (L TKO4, down 6x).
- **Knobs:** output .6, jab .35, power .85, body .45, combo .6, initiative .8, patience .25, shell .3, slip .4, legs .45, clinch .15, composure .3, risk .85, killer .8, range .45, circle .3. **Base: slugger. Guard: standard (southpaw).**
- **Sim hook:** low composure + high risk means he answers a knockdown by trading at once. A "glass-cannon" profile with high KD-for and KD-against rates.

## 5. Ramon "Dinamita" Cardenas ("the underdog who dropped the Monster")
**122, orthodox, USA (San Antonio).** Status: about 28-2. W SD10 over Leonardo Carrillo on 2026-08-29 in LA (98-92, 96-94, 93-97) [src: champinon, single source]. W TKO over Erik Robles on 2025-12-18 [snippet]. L TKO8 to Naoya Inoue on 2025-05-04 [src: AP].
- **Style:** a compact, tough, durable fighter who absorbs pressure and counters [src: AP]. He bobs down and drives back up into a left hook (3) [src: SI "bobbed his body down and drove back up"]. Against Inoue (CompuBox): 80/290 landed, ~36/rd thrown, 27.6%. Jab 35/158 = 54% of thrown, power 45/132. Body: 15 of 80 landed [src: CompuBox via search]. Mid range. A shell-and-counter fighter against better men.
- **Why exciting:** He dropped Inoue in round 2 with a counter hook off a dip, only the second knockdown of Inoue's career [src]. He took a barrage for 6 more rounds and never quit until the stoppage on the ropes in round 8 [src].
- **Mind:** patient, with high heart and composure, and risk in the counter moments only. Flaw: low output against volume. Inoue raised the pressure (137 landed from round 4 on) and shut out the dip-hook by staying off that line [src: Ring]. He was trapped on the ropes and covering at the finish.
- **Library:** Inoue 2025 (L TKO8, KD'd Inoue in round 2). Carrillo 2026 (W SD10, close). Little else found.
- **Knobs:** output .45, jab .55, power .6, body .35, combo .45, initiative .35, patience .7, shell .65, slip .6, legs .4, clinch .35, composure .85, risk .5, killer .55, range .45, circle .3. **Base: counter. Guard: high.**
- **Sim hook:** a "dip-and-hook" trigger: on the opponent's 2, slip down, then a 3 counter with a big damage multiplier. He is the template for an underdog with a puncher's chance.

## 6. Francisco "Chihuas" Rodriguez Jr. ("the punch-stat machine")
**112 (fly; was 105 champ), orthodox [mem], Mexico (Monterrey).** Status: W UD12 over Angelino Cordova on 2026-05-30 in San Diego, a WBC flyweight final eliminator (116-111, 115-113 x2) [src: WBC]. Record about 41-6-1 (28 KO) [snippet].
- **Style:** a relentless high-volume pressure fighter. He says he fights "Mexican style... throwing all my punches" and "the only way to win: to crush him" [src: WBC]. **CompuBox vs Galal Yafai (2025-06-21): 575/1,087 landed, 48 landed/rd of 91 thrown/rd, a flyweight record. 528 of the 575 were power punches (44/rd), also a record** [src: Dan Rafael, Boxing News]. That makes ~92% of landed power and little jab. He dropped Yafai in round 12. The result is clouded: he returned an adverse finding after the bout, and one database lists it as an NC [src: DAZN, champinon]. In his 2014 105-lb FOTY vs Takayama, he countered with "a splendid left hook", scored the only KD in round 3, and "took his stings effortlessly" [src: WBO, asianboxing].
- **Why exciting:** non-stop exchanges. He throws 90+ punches a round and is happy to be hit to land his own. A 2014 Fight of the Year winner.
- **Mind:** maximum initiative and toughness, steady composure, no tactical retreat. He adjusts by doing more. Flaws: a slick technician can pile up scoring early (Cordova was competitive), and an elite pressure-puncher beats him (stopped by Chocolatito in 7 in 2013 [src]). He is also a doping risk.
- **Library:** Takayama 2014 (W UD12, FOTY, unified WBO/IBF 105). Yafai 2025 (W UD12, record output, KD in round 12; doping cloud). Cordova 2026 (W UD12, pressure vs skill). Chocolatito 2013 (L TKO7) [src].

- **Sim hook:** the volume ceiling. A sim fighter at output .95 should reach ~90 thrown/rd, but only with a gas tank to match.

## Patterns: what makes these fighters exciting (rules a sim can use)
1. **Two-way danger beats one-way dominance.** Every "exciting" pick has scored *and* suffered knockdowns (Nery, Tsutsumi, Ioka, Olascuaga, Sasaki, Cardenas, Chocolatito). Rule: at least one KD each way in ~15-20% of fights between exciting archetypes. Knockdowns within the same round, like Tsutsumi-Higa round 9, are the peak moment.
2. **Hurt fighters fire back instead of shelling.** Tsutsumi, Chocolatito and Olascuaga answer damage with punches. Map this to composure × risk: high composure + high risk means a counter-surge next exchange; low composure + high risk means a reckless trade, the Nery glass-cannon.
3. **Late surges and late finishes.** Tsutsumi from round 5, Yabuki's KOs in rounds 10-12, Chocolatito's 93 to 125 thrown/rd ramp, Olascuaga raging in rounds 7-8. Give exciting fighters a pace curve that rises with rounds (if gas allows), plus a "behind on cards → killer up" rule in rounds 10-12.
4. **Volume makes action.** Flyweight wars run 80-110 thrown/rd (Chihuas 91, Chocolatito 82-110), against a ~55 norm. Small fighters with output ≥ .9 should throw roughly 1.6x the audit norm, at ~25-45% connect.
5. **Body war as a subplot.** Ioka (44-12 body vs T. Inoue), Chocolatito (128 body landed vs Robles) and Casimero's double 3b dropping Nery. Body damage should slow the victim, so a body-first fighter turns the fight late, which reads as a comeback.
6. **The underdog's one weapon.** Cardenas' dip-and-hook dropped Inoue. Counter-style underdogs need a rare, high-damage signature counter, so that upsets and scares happen without the underdog winning on the cards.
7. **Flaws make fights.** Slow starts (Tsutsumi), getting outworked early (Yabuki), defensive gaps when reaching (Sasaki), and weight or discipline problems (Nery, JCM). Exciting fighters should carry one exploitable habit so that momentum swings both ways.
