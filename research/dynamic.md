# Ed's paste: the head-on-cheek inside position (Oct 22)

A breakdown video (not Atlas; a clip Ed pasted) of the inside position Duran, Bowe, Hatton and Golovkin all used. Orthodox vs orthodox.

**The position.** Get a bit lower than him and off to the right of his centre line. Glue your forehead to his left cheek: your head sits where his lead hand would be in a closed guard. Hips low and close, torso angled forward to drive the head in. Pressure on the head goes up or down by what you want.

**The lead (left) hand: six ways to stop his right hand.**
1. Glove on glove: your left on his right glove; push, pull or just cover it.
2. Hand on his rear shoulder: you feel the punch start before you see it; press the crook of the elbow or the bicep.
3. Forearm across his rear hand (or across both: one arm traps two).
4. Collar tie: controls his posture, holds him for the uppercut, the hanging elbow blocks body shots on that side.
5. Underhook: kills that side, can lift him off his base; costs you the left hand's punches (sets up the shoulder bump).
6. Overhook: stops that arm, lets you stay on that side; Hatton held one arm and dug with the other.
**The rear (right) hand:** keep it simple, trap his lead (glove on glove, or press the bicep / elbow crook), hand on top so you can punch and he can't.

**What it gets you.**
1. **Built-in defence.** Low, tight, right of centre = his blind spot; your head pushes his eyes off you; you're around the corner from his right and too close for his left to mean anything. A place to rest and run the clock out.
2. **Hand traps win the hand fight.** He has the same trap options at this range, but your head blinds him to them: by the time he feels the trap you already have it.
3. **Safe scoring.** Keep the head glued and spam the body: hooks and short stabbing uppercuts from either hand; the free hand stays on trap duty.
4. **The hidden right hand.** Your head is blocking the target on that side, so he doesn't expect a shot there. Throw the uppercut / overhand / short right hook and let the punch pull your head off his cheek (head slides left and a bit back, toward your left hip): the lane opens at the last instant and it lands flush. Pulling back on the uppercut is fine here because you start leaned in; it's really coming up to neutral.
5. **Bully him.** Hands already on him for shoves and off-balances; the head itself steers him around the ring or lifts him out of his stance.
6. **Door to the advanced stuff.** The door handle: grab his elbow, shove it across, step out the side door and load the left hand (also scrambles his guard, and he can't turn to face you while you hold it). Chicken wing (with forearm control) or shoulder bump (with an underhook) knock him back a step to make room to punch.

**For Last Bell.** There's no inside game today beyond the clinch (`skillr.md` batch 1: entry costs exposure, size decides inside; `size.md`: leaning). This is the swarmer's / slugger's inside home: a state, not a punch. The pieces that fit a side-view sim:
- A head-on-chest/cheek **inside lock** a pressure man can take at MIN_D: his own head defence goes up (blind spot, eyes off), the other man's long punches go down; the free hand traps one of the other man's hands.
- From it: body hooks and short uppercuts at low risk, and the **hidden rear hand** (the punch moves the head and opens the lane: a surprise shot, harder to read).
- The other man's answers: push off / pivot out (Atlas's "exiting the pocket"), tie up, or his own lock if he's the bigger inside man.
- Size: works for the short man vs a tall one (he lives under the chin); the big man's version is the lean (`size.md` "Leaning").
- Reading it: an inside man who lands this every time is a flaw-finder's target too (uppercut on the man who ducks in, `docs/PLUGIN.md`).
Parking lot for the brain pass after 3D, unless s2 (Size) wants the short man's inside game sooner.

# Batch 1: Teddy Atlas (Oct 22)

22 pulled (cut to the important ones on Ed's word; "Setting Traps" has no captions). One-minute fight breakdowns plus his named concepts. Short clips, so one idea each.

## Size: the tall man's plan and the short man's way in (for `docs/SIZE.md` s2)
- **Fundora beat Tszyu (2024): the full-extension jab and the double step.** Not a jab from too close (that gets countered): full extension, held at the end of it. Every time Tszyu came in 6 inches, Fundora went back 12; Tszyu reached in and ate counters. Fundora had only just learned this (KO loss the fight before: he used to fight inside, `size.md` "Fundora").
- **What Tszyu didn't do: jab back.** He walked in without a jab and let Fundora jab free all night. You don't have to out-jab the tall man, just make him respect yours: head off the line, double the jab to close the gap.
- **Fury outscored Ngannou (2023): own the geography.** Pick the range your body is built for (tall = outside), full jabs, not three-quarter ones, and little steps back as he comes forward so the range never changes. Same move as Fundora.
- **Two ways in for the short man (Atlas's ESPN line).** The standard: work in behind the jab, moving the head, bring the feet. The other: **make the tall man come to you** ("get the tall guy to be small"): entice him in, make him miss, hit him; "you don't have to go through a bad neighbourhood". Going in is the bad neighbourhood: be alert the whole way.
- **The short southpaw's throwaway (Ramirez KO'd Nova).** Ramirez threw a throwaway right hook to close the gap; Nova pulled straight back, still in the path, and got caught. The answer to the short man's entry: weave and step to the side, not straight back ("now you can catch him, he can't catch you").
- **Inside on a bigger man: around the mountain.** He's dangerous only when his feet are set, so keep them moving: drop, short punches, short steps around him. Same as **Benavidez beat Andrade**: Andrade stood in front of the bigger man and got broken down; the plan was head on the side, short punches, then step around him, never parked in the pocket.
- **Usyk beat Fury (2024): the slip that takes his eyes.** The southpaw pressed, got inside the bigger man, landed the straight left. The trick: as he got in he slipped to his right and threw at the same moment; Fury's eyes went with the head, and the shot you don't see hurts even a 6'9" man.
- **What Fury should have done:** vs a southpaw, move to your left, away from the left hand, jab from there. He drifted right into it, or stood in front.
- **Loma vs Haney (2023): the smaller man has to be the quicker man, starting with the feet.** Loma saw Haney's left hang low and need a beat to come up before the jab: when he saw it low, double jab to close fast, left hand to finish, before Haney got "the gun out of the holster". (A tell seen, a trick that works it: the flaw loop, `docs/PLUGIN.md`.)
- Gilfeather (not Atlas), short hooks vs a taller man: once you're close, square up and dig the body with short hooks off a hip rhythm; staying in the long boxing stance close in gives him the edge. "You box a fighter, fight a boxer."

## Pressure, counters and traps (the brain)
- **Inoue beat Fulton: aggressive in spots, read the feet.** You can't just walk in on a good counter-puncher. Inoue went hard only when he saw Fulton's feet drift back: that was the green light to attack safely. He also took the jab away: a small slip and a counter over it (sometimes an uppercut from a new angle), until Fulton stopped offering it.
- **Crawford beat Spence: walk in the rain without getting wet.** Gave ground, invited Spence in, made him miss and pay. Either stance.
- **Ngannou dropped Fury: catch and shoot.** Fury's one-two came up a bit short on the right: Ngannou caught the end of it and came straight back with his own.
- **Tank beat Romero: the trap.** Romero was hunting the big right hand, anxious; Tank made him reach and KO'd him. Answer: feint first, make him move back or show his hand, then come in behind the jab instead of loading up wide open.
- **Tank beat Santa Cruz: size needs a delivery system.** The bigger man won as an aggressive counter-puncher (Duran): slip, then the uppercut (not a hook) into a man who kept his head in the middle after his punch. Same KO as Povetkin on Whyte. Rule: in the punching zone you're all the way in or all the way out; the middle is no man's land.
- **Feint to bring him in** (vs an aggressive man): weight on the back foot, a small feint forward, then pull out as if you changed your mind; he follows you in, into the web. Pick the feint by his temperament.
- **Ride the rails** (timing a predictable jab): you've read his rhythm and he jabs from a bit too close. Don't go over it: ride in along it, head moving left so you're outside his right hand, and land as it comes. He never sees it, and you stayed responsible on defence.
- **Selling** (a feint built into the punch): show the right hand to the head, drop on an angle and land it to the solar plexus. Low enough that his counter to the head goes over you. Easier for a short man.

## Ring cutting and inside work
- **Don't follow the mover.** Following walks you into things and he leads you around (Louis cut Conn down in round 13: "he can run but he can't hide"). Move like a linebacker: step parallel to keep him in front, shrink the ring a step at a time, and stay alert: he can stop and jab, nobody hands out a "cutting the ring, don't hit me" badge.
- **Inside fighting** (Atlas's own drill): get in behind the jab, or let him come to you. Inside, the power comes from turning the shoulders, not pulling the hands back (that opens holes). Head to the side, never in the middle (the uppercut lane); work one side, then visit the other. **The foxhole:** drop low, work, pop up.

## Mind (corner talk material)
- **Pressure's two lies:** it says this lasts forever (it's moments; only what you do in them lasts), and it says it's not your choice (it always is).

## For Last Bell
1. **Size s2: the tall man's plan, by the book.** Full-extension jab + a double step back on his entry (he steps in X, you go back 2X), counters on the reach. The short man's two plans: jab his way in (a jab back so the tall man can't jab free), or **draw the tall man in** (feint, make him miss). Tall man's mistakes to model: three-quarter jabs, fighting inside (Fundora before Tszyu), standing in front.
2. **Short man inside: keep moving** (around the mountain / step around), never parked in front of the bigger man. Fits the head-on-cheek lock above as the inside home.
3. **Read the feet**: attack when the other man's weight drifts back (Inoue). A cue the brain can see; maybe already in `perceive`: check before building.
4. **Exit or commit**: after his punch, a man who stays in the middle is a counter target (Tank-Cruz). A brain habit by level.
5. **Feints by temperament**: the come-in feint vs an aggressive man (fits the clips pile, `docs/NOTES.md` "Handoff (Oct 21, research...)").
6. **Ring cutting as a parallel step** (cut-off angle, not chase): check what `cutK` does today.
7. Corner line: the two lies, for a hurt or rattled man between rounds.
