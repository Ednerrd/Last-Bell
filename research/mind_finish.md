# Finishing and surviving: the hurt-man exchange (Oct 2026)

Seventh research pass. Goes deeper than `mind_pace.md` §5 (confirm, target, cut off; clinch, knee, move, fire, bell) and `mind_vision.md` §8d (shook + panic pick). This file adds the **timing, the failure modes, the numbers and the rules** behind both sides, then maps them to `brain/brain.js` `hunt` / `survive`.

**Tags:** [source] = read in a fetched page, [snippet] = search-result text (most of this file: the proxy blocked every page fetch this pass), [mem] = model memory, worth a check, (est.) = my estimate for the engine. Round numbers and quotes from [snippet] are cross-checked across 2+ results where noted.

## 1. Finishing

### 1a. Recognizing the hurt man (the tells)
| tell | what it looks like | note |
|---|---|---|
| Legs | knees dip, stumble, wide or crossed feet, "drunk" steps to the side | the most reliable public tell; Fury "stumbled across the ring" after Usyk's left, R9 [snippet] |
| Eyes | blank stare, loss of focus, looks to his corner | "they can wave you forward after a solid punch, but their eyes may tip you off" (Title Boxing blog) [snippet]; Taylor "only looked at his corner" when Steele asked "are you OK?" [snippet] |
| Holding | grabs right after the shot, not as a tactic | Inoue vs Donaire 1 R9: "a stunned Inoue tried to hold Donaire right after that shot" [snippet]; Mayweather vs Mosley R2: "his knees dipped and he had to hold" [snippet] |
| Hands drop / no return fire | stops punching, shells, or arms hang | Gatti R9 vs Ward: "staggering back with his hands down" before going to a knee [snippet] |
| Fake tells | waving him on, shaking the head, smiling, glove touch | read as a bluff more often than not (est.); the eyes and legs beat the face |
| Ropes holding him up | leaning, arms hooked, sagging | ref can rule it a knockdown (Fury R9 vs Usyk, Marquez R1 vs Pacquiao) [snippet] |

Key point for the engine: the tell shows **0.2 to 0.6 s after the shot** (legs go first, then the clinch reach) (est. from footage [mem]). A finisher with a good read sees it at once. A poor one either misses it or "smells blood" that isn't there.

### 1b. Jump now or wait?
Three real patterns:
1. **Jump at once** (killer, he's clearly gone): Mayweather vs Hatton R10, after the check hook and the 8 count, "almost immediately got him on the ropes and landed another flurry", stopped at 1:35 [snippet]. Leonard vs Hearns R14: a right to the body hurt him, then 23 to 28 unanswered (count disputed) until the stop at 1:45 [snippet].
2. **Confirm first, then go** (elite default): one clean, sure shot to test, then commit. Inoue vs Nery: dropped Nery R2, R5, finished R6 with a combination at 1:22 [snippet]; not one wild flurry, a series of measured attacks.
3. **Wait / let him come** (counter-punchers vs a hurt man who still charges): Usyk vs Dubois 2 (Jul 2025) R5: Dubois charged and forced him to the corner; "instead of unleashing a vicious onslaught, Usyk countered with an overhand lead right" for KD 1, then a short left when Dubois "walked into the pocket again" for KD 2 [snippet]. The hurt man came to him.

### 1c. What they throw
- **Short and straight, not wide.** Wide loaded shots are what get countered (1d). Elites tighten up: short hooks, straight right down the middle, uppercuts through a high guard (est., common coaching [mem]).
- **Body first when he shells or holds.** "Kill the body and the head will die" (Sam Langford to Joe Louis, per Barry Popik) [snippet]. Leonard finished Hearns off a body right [snippet]. Ward dropped Gatti with a left hook to the body R9 [snippet]. Inoue finishes many fights to the body (`mind_pace.md` §5).
- **Uppercuts into the shell, hooks around it.** A high guard covers the front; the gap is under the chin and at the temple (est. [mem]). Matches the engine's `nextInCombo` uppercut-after-duck rule.
- **Variety beats volume.** Change levels and angles so the shell can't sit on one spot (est.).

### 1d. Finishing goes wrong: the famous counters
| fight | what happened | lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Joshua vs Ruiz 1 (Jun 2019) R3 | AJ dropped Ruiz with a left hook, kept pressing; Ruiz's left hook to the temple dropped AJ, then a straight right dropped him again; saved by the bell [snippet] | a hurt man who still throws is live; finishing with the guard down |
| Joshua vs Klitschko (Apr 2017) R5-6 | AJ dropped Wlad R5, "punched himself out with over two minutes left"; Hearn: "his legs just emptied after going wild"; Wlad dropped AJ in R6 [snippet] | a 2-minute flurry empties the tank; the finisher becomes the hurt man |
| Pacquiao vs Marquez 4 (Dec 2012) R6 | Pacquiao, having dropped JMM in R5, pressed late in R6, missed a right, ate the counter right, out cold. "I got hit by a punch I didn't see." [snippet] | pressing after a KD invites the counter-puncher's best shot |
| Mayweather vs Hatton (Dec 2007) R10 | Hatton charging in with a speculative hook, right hand low; check hook. Hatton: "I was forcing it." [snippet] | forcing it is the attacker's version of the trap |
| Gatti vs Ward 1 (May 2002) R9 | after the body KD Gatti got up "retaliating with looping shots" that stumbled Ward [snippet] | the hurt man fires back, the finisher has to respect it |
| Wilder vs Ortiz 1 (Mar 2018) R7 → R10 | Ortiz had Wilder "out on his feet", the bell saved him; Wilder dropped Ortiz twice in R10 [snippet] | one failed finish can cost the whole fight |
| Pacquiao vs Marquez 1 (May 2004) R1 | three KDs in R1, no three-KD rule in title fights; JMM "collected himself by the third" and won most rounds; draw [snippet] | the finisher's window closes; a recovered counter-puncher adapts |

The pattern: **the finisher gets caught when he (a) loads up wide, (b) drops his guard, (c) keeps going after his gas is gone, or (d) walks straight in on a counter-puncher.** Very common, no single stat (est.).

### 1e. How long the flurry lasts
- Leonard–Hearns R14: 23 to 28 punches before the stop [snippet]; at elite pace that is ~6 to 9 s (est.).
- Whittaker vs Cameron 2 (Apr 2025): forum count of **11 unanswered punches over 9 s** before the stop [snippet, fan count].
- Joshua R5 vs Klitschko: the flurry plus the chase lasted ~1 min, and that's what emptied him [snippet].
- Rule of thumb (est.): a real finishing flurry is **4 to 10 s of bursts of 3 to 6 punches**, with tiny resets between; past ~15 s of all-out finishing the finisher's gas falls off a cliff.

### 1f. Cutting off a running hurt man
- Step with him and cut the angle instead of following; trap him on the ropes or in a corner, where he can't clinch on the move or run (`mind_pace.md` §5).
- The ropes are both ways: they hold up a hurt man (Fury R9, Marquez R1) and a ref can score that as a knockdown [snippet].
- A runner on wobbly legs can't keep running long: the legs go first, so running costs more than shelling (est.).

### 1g. When the ref stops it
- **Rule text has no number.** The referee is "the sole arbiter"; the trigger is not "intelligently defending" himself (e.g. Idaho rule) [snippet]. No fixed punch count.
- In practice: a run of unanswered clean shots (the Whittaker fan count, 11 in 9 s), the man not punching back, eyes/legs gone, no answer to "are you OK?" (Steele stopped Chavez–Taylor with **2 seconds left**: Taylor didn't answer, looked at his corner) [snippet].
- After a knockdown the ref asks for proof: Reiss told Fury he wouldn't stop it if Fury "could step left and right and say he was all right" [snippet].
- **Rules:** ABC Unified Rules: mandatory 8 count after any KD, NO standing 8, NO three-knockdown rule; some states keep the three-KD rule for non-title bouts; major title fights don't use it [snippet, secondary]. A knee counts as down [snippet].
- Corners stop it too: the towel came in as Cortez waved off Hatton [snippet].

## 2. Surviving

### 2a. The options, with real examples
| option | how | who | engine status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clinch / hold** | grab right after the shot, tie up both arms, lean on him | Mayweather vs Mosley R2 ("knees dipped and he had to hold, but never touched down") [snippet]; Inoue vs Donaire 1 R9 "held on to make it through the round" [snippet]; Bowe in R10 vs Holyfield "could only hang on and ride out the storm" (SI) [snippet] | built (`clinch`) |
| **Bite down and keep working** | ignore it, keep jabbing, show nothing | Mayweather: "All I did was bite down and say, 'OK, it's OK'" and went back on the attack, won 119-109 x2 [snippet] | partly (`fire` is the reckless version; this is the calm one) |
| **Take the count** | stay down to ~8, use the time | "take the 8" is standard coaching [snippet]; Inoue vs Nery R1: "took a mandatory eight count calmly", "From the moment I went down, I was able to recover calmly" [snippet]; Fury vs Wilder 1 R12 up at ~9 [snippet]; AJ vs Ruiz R3 up at 8 [snippet] | new: KD getting-up timing |
| **Take a knee (no KD)** | go down on purpose for the 8 | Fundora vs Lubin: "took the knee to collect himself" [snippet]. Steward: going back down after rising is "a waste of time and energy" and can look like you can't go on; ref Neumann agreed [snippet] | new, rare, low IQ-cost only |
| **Fire back** | throw to deter and to show the ref | Gatti R9 vs Ward [snippet]; Ruiz R3 vs Joshua (fired back and dropped him) [snippet] | built (`fire`) |
| **Run / circle** | move, stay off the ropes, head off center | coaching: "move laterally, keep your head off the centerline", "losing a round for inactivity is better than losing by KO" [snippet] | built (`run`) |
| **Shell on the ropes** | high guard, roll, let the ropes take weight | Ali's rope-a-dope [mem]; risky: ref sees no punches back | built (`shell`) |
| **Stall (gray area)** | spit the gumshield, slow walk to the corner | Corrales vs Castillo 1 R10: spat it after both KDs, got extra time, 1 point taken, then stopped Castillo [snippet] | skip (fouls) |
| **Freeze** | no response at all | Taylor's last seconds vs Chavez [snippet] | built (`freeze`) |

### 2b. How long until he's back
- **Mild stun clears in seconds to minutes** (WBA neurologist: tissue "regains its shape and function after a few minutes") [snippet]. Real fights show the in-round part:
  - Inoue vs Nery: down in R1, then out-landed Nery 21-4 in R1 (CompuBox) and dropped him in R2 [snippet]. Full recovery by next round.
  - Inoue vs Cardenas (May 2025): down R2 (left hook counter as Inoue tried to "return the aggression"), "calmly pulled myself together", "focused on distance"; took Cardenas' best shots without trouble after R2; KD R7, stop R8 [snippet].
  - Marquez vs Pacquiao 1: 3 KDs in R1, "collected himself by the third" [snippet]: ~2 rounds for a full reset.
  - Fury vs Wilder 3: two KDs in R4; Fury: "I was never thinking, 'Oh, this is over'"; SugarHill: "just trying to calm that man down and gain control back" [snippet]; Fury won R5+ and KO'd Wilder R11.
- Engine-ready shape (est.): legs back in **5 to 15 s** if he isn't hit again (clinch or count), head clear in **20 to 40 s**; the **1-minute break** gives most of the rest (Wilder R7 → R10 vs Ortiz; AJ R3 → fought to R7 vs Ruiz); a full return to sharp takes **1 to 2 rounds** after a bad KD.
- Every clean hit while hurt resets part of that clock (est.).

### 2c. The bell
- Saved by the bell: Wilder R7 vs Ortiz, AJ R3 vs Ruiz, Fury R9 vs Usyk [snippet]. The minute is the biggest single recovery in boxing.
- So the clock changes both sides: a hurt man with 20 s left should hold and run; a finisher with 20 s left must go now or lose the window (est.).
- Late in the round the finisher rushes (Ruiz charging after the first KD on AJ, late R3) [snippet].

### 2d. Corner instructions for a hurt fighter
- First 30 s of the break: breath and calm, then one instruction (UK trainer, Boxing News) [snippet].
- SugarHill (Fury, R4 Wilder 3): "calm that man down and gain control back" [snippet].
- Fury's corner after a KD (unverified context): "get on your bike and have a rest" but win the next rounds [snippet].
- Golub to Joshua after two R1 KDs vs Prenga (2026 [snippet, verify]): "Stay in the ring, don't fall", move, hands closed.
- Inoue's own fix vs Cardenas: more distance [snippet]. The same lever as `plan.range` in the brain's adjust.

### 2e. The low-blow rule (recovery time, not a hurt state)
Usyk vs Dubois 1 (Aug 2023) R5: ruled low, Usyk got up to 5 minutes and used ~3:45 to 4:00 [snippet]. Not for the brain; a rules note.

Engine-usable estimates (est., from the examples, not stats):
- After a **first** KD in a round: ~35 to 45% are stopped that round, higher at heavyweight, lower for elite-composure men.
- After a **second** KD in a round: ~60 to 70% stopped (refs and towels move faster).
- A hurt (not down) man with < 20 s left in the round survives ~80% of the time.
- Heavyweight stoppage rate ~1.5x a flyweight's; the game sits at welter (audit: 25-30% stoppages).

## 4. For the brain

What exists (`brain/brain.js` ~400-420, 140-150; `index.html` ~2074-2232):
- `updMode`: on `me.hurt`, picks `hurtResp` from clinch/shell/run/fire/freeze weighted by traits and composure; `survive` lasts `rnd(2.5, 5)` s and ends when no longer hurt.
- `hunt` (aggr 3, range -14, guard .45, cut 1) starts at rate `killer * read * (1-.4 fear) * dt * 4`, lasts `rnd(4, 8)`, ends fast if he's no longer hurt. `pickCombo` in hunt favours power (`1 + power * 2.5 * killer`).
- Engine: `unanswered` counts non-jab clean hits on a stunned/low man (resets when he lands, 2.5 s window); ≥5 rolls a TKO. Three-KD stop, ref stop after a bad KD exist. Body KD = takes a knee (render).

### 4a. Hunt: new rules (finisher)
1. **Confirm before commit (new).** On `him.hurt`, a `confirm` sub-phase of `lerp(.8, .2, k.read)` s: throw 1 to 2 short straight shots, guard normal. Killer + low read skips it (Joshua-type). If the confirm shot lands and he's still hurt → full hunt; if he still throws back → `huntSafe`.
2. **Two hunt flavors (new).** `hunt` (current) vs `huntSafe` = aggr 1.8, guard .7, range -8, power weight halved, straight/short punches x1.5, wide hooks/overhands x.6. Pick `huntSafe` when the hurt man is in `fire` mode, or his `risk`/power is high, or I've been countered in the last 5 s. Chance of picking the plain reckless hunt = `killer * (1 - composure)` (est. .15 to .5).
3. **Body vs head (new knob).** In hunt, if his guard > .8 (shell) or he's clinching: body weight x2, uppercut x1.6. Hooks to the head x1.3 vs a runner.
4. **Flurry shape (new).** Bursts of 3 to 6, then a 0.3 to 0.6 s reset look (re-confirm). Hunt cost: stamina drain x1.6 while in hunt. If my gas < .35 → drop to `huntSafe` or `press`; this is the Joshua R5 lesson. Cap all-out hunt at ~10 to 12 s per hurt spell (est.), then `press`.
5. **Clock (new).** Round time left < 20 s and he's hurt: hunt chance x2, skip confirm (go now, the bell is his friend). Left < 5 s with no clean shot: stay in hunt anyway (it's free).
6. **Cut-off (partly built).** Hunt already has `cut: 1`; add: if he's in `run`, use `k.cutoff` x1.5 and aim to his escape side (ropes trap).
7. **The counter trap (new, defender side).** A counter-puncher being hunted with composure > .6: a `bait` chance (.2 to .4): stays still and waits for the overcommit, counter bonus x1.5 vs `hunt` (not vs `huntSafe`). This makes reckless hunting really cost (Marquez 4, Hatton, Usyk–Dubois 2, Cardenas on Inoue).
8. **False alarm.** Misreading a hurt: chance `(1 - k.read) * .15` per big landed shot to enter hunt on a man who isn't hurt (est.). Feeds the counter trap.

### 4b. Survive: new rules (hurt man)
1. **Phases (new).** Survive = 0 to 1 s "first reaction" (the current pick: shell/run/fire/clinch/freeze), then a **second pick at ~1 to 2 s** weighted by composure: high composure moves to `clinch` or `steady` (Mayweather "bite down"); low stays put or slides to `freeze`/`shell`. Freeze capped at 0.3 to 0.8 s, then forced to `shell`.
2. **New response: `steady` (new).** Composure > .7 only: guard .85, keeps jabbing at aggr .6, normal range, footwork normal. Mayweather R2 vs Mosley, Inoue vs Nery. Weight `composure^2 * k.read * 1.2`.
3. **Clinch timing (built, tune).** Real hurt clinches happen inside 1 s of the shot. Clinch chance when the hunter steps in < 40: `.5 + .4 * k.clinch` (vs .5 now). Each clinch buys ~2 to 4 s of recovery while the ref breaks it (est.).
4. **Fire back resets the stoppage (built, by the engine).** `unanswered` already resets when he lands. Make `fire` and `steady` aim to land one punch per ~2 s ("show the ref"). In `shell`, after 3+ unanswered, 40% chance to switch to `fire` or `clinch` (shell is what gets you stopped) (est.).
5. **Ropes (partly built).** Shell is the only response that stays on the ropes (`lateral *.3`). Add: run/steady push off the ropes first (`lateral x1.5`) when hurt.
6. **Recovery clock (new, engine side).** If `hurt` is a timer: base 5 to 15 s (`lerp(15, 5, composure)`), x.6 while clinched or in a count, +2 s per clean hit taken. The break between rounds clears most of it: leave a small residue (head x.9 next round) after a KD (est.).
7. **Clock awareness (new).** Round time < 20 s: run/clinch weights x1.5, fire x.6 ("ride it to the bell"). Losing late on the cards (`F.lead` bad, last 2 rounds): fire x1.8 (Gatti, Ruiz).
8. **Knockdown get-up (new).** Down with composure high: get up at 7 to 9 (take the 8). Low composure: up at 3 to 5 (pride) and still hurt. A strategic knee without a KD: only if `hurt` and on the ropes and composure > .5 and IQ high, ~3% per hurt spell (rare, est.); costs a 10-8.
9. **After-hurt adjust (new, small).** After a KD or big hurt, `plan.range += 8` for the rest of the round and the next (Inoue vs Cardenas "focused on distance"). Hooks into the existing between-round adjust list.

### 4d. Targets to watch
- Stoppages stay 25-30%, body KDs 5-8% (audit).
- Hurt-to-stop rate: of hurt spells, ~20 to 30% end in a KD or stop (est.).
- **Finisher-caught rate:** of hunt spells, ~5 to 10% end with the hunter hurt (est.; the Ruiz/Marquez moments). Log it in `tel`.
- Same-round stop after a first KD: ~35-45% (est., §3).

### 4e. Commentary hooks
"He's holding on!" / "Bites down, keeps the jab going" / "He's punching himself out!" / "Careful, he's still dangerous" / "Walked right onto it!" / "Saved by the bell!" / "He takes the full eight" / "The ref's looking hard at him."
