## 1. Hurt (head): legs, eyes, response

What the officials actually write down:

- **ABC referee manual (Jul 2022), "Stopping the bout".** "Are the boxer's hands up? Is his head snapping back from the punches? How are his legs (wobbly sturdy)?" [S][Q] https://www.abcboxing.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/box-ref-manual-july-2022.pdf
- Same manual, after a knockdown: wipe the gloves, "Look into their eyes. Are they looking right back at you?", ask him to walk toward you, or left or right: "Is the boxer steady? Is their balance good?" Then: "A general rule is that if the referee has any doubt, stop the bout." [S][Q] (same URL)
- Same manual: a boxer "bent over (not fully standing) is considered up... it may be a sign that this bout should be stopped." Ropes alone holding him up can score a knockdown. Towel from the corner does not stop a pro fight. [S] (same URL)
- Same manual, between rounds: "See how the boxer walks back to the corner." A wobbly man at the bell is in the rest period, but the ref can still stop it then. Concussion list includes "Delayed response to questions", "Appearing dazed", "Fatigue". [S][Q] (same URL)
- **Bayless (Nevada ref) Q&A.** "you look at body chemistry. When a fighter is sitting down on his punches before, he might be pushing his punches now. We also look at his eyes. The fighter's eyes can become very glassy and it is hard for anyone else to see that if they are not in the ring." [S][Q] https://tss.ib.tv/boxing/the-tss-q-n-a-referee-kenny-bayless/ (JSON copy: https://tss.ib.tv/boxing/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/18196)
- Bayless, same piece: between rounds the ref can go to the corner, "look at how he responds to us and to his trainer", and the doctor tells him "keep a close eye on this guy". To a beaten man he says "you're going to have to show me something." Then: "It's a trigger in us... you just don't pull back from it." And: "I would rather stop a fight sooner than later." [S][Q]
- **Reiss (Fury-Wilder 1, R12).** "tired but not extremely hurt" going into R12. After the knockdown he watched the eyes during the count ("his eyes popped open like I startled him"), then made Fury walk away and back. His reason: "any drunk can walk in a straight line. Doctors taught us it is hard to hide things are off when they have to change direction." Also: "The 10 count doesn't mean 10 seconds... to make sure the fighter who is hurt can intelligently defend himself." [S][Q] https://english.stadiumastro.com/news-sports/referee-reiss-rejects-fury-longcount-claims-116960
- **Steele (Hagler-Hearns, 1985).** Hearns got up at 9 after three knockdowns: "Steele said that Hearns' eyes were glazed and that his legs were wobbly... 'I wasn't asking questions. I knew he'd had enough.'" So eyes + legs both gone = stop with no talk. [S][Q] https://www.bostonglobe.com/2020/04/14/sports/hagler-stops-hearns-3-champion-bloodied-remains-unbowed
- Steele (Chavez-Taylor): asked "are you OK?" twice; Taylor did not answer or let go of the ropes, and looked at his corner. Stopped with 2 s left. Not answering, or looking to the corner, is a stop sign. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Steele_(referee)
- **Weeks (Canelo-Angulo).** Made up his mind at the start of R10, waited for "the opportune moment". Stopped when an uppercut snapped the head back: "His eyes rolled back, and that was it." On a one-sided fight where the loser fights back: "there's nothing on his punches." The doctor had told him "At the next hard punch, stop the fight." [S][Q] https://www.badlefthook.com/2014/3/15/5511446/tony-weeks-discusses-canelo-angulo-there-was-no-question-that-i-had
- Dazed rule text (Michigan): if dazed and "unable to defend himself", ref gives a standing 8 or stops it. No number of punches anywhere. [S] https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/michigan/Mich-Admin-Code-R-339-225
- Refs differ on how early: Bayless "sooner than later"; Smoger worried from R7 and stopped Khan-Malignaggi late in R11 [snip]; Cortez said nine in ten refs would have stopped Wilder-Fury R12 while Reiss gave Fury the benefit [snip]. This is real spread for an early / late ref setting. https://www.ringtv.com/178933-lems-latest-smoger-on-matthysses-ko-of-peterson/

What the ringside doctor checks:

- **NYSAC / ARP protocol (Sethi summary).** Between rounds (1 minute): Maddocks questions (venue, opponent, round, who he fought last), symptoms (headache, dizziness, vision, "feeling off-balance"), pupil size and reaction, two-step command. Stop flags: confusion, amnesia, abnormal exam ("ataxia, impaired balance, pupil size asymmetry and/or reactivity"). Vomiting alone is not a reason. [S][Q] https://combatsportslaw.com/2019/06/20/nysacs-chief-medical-officer-calls-for-standardized-concussion-screening-in-boxing-and-mma/
- **World Boxing Medical Handbook 2025.** The doctor has about 1 minute at the neutral corner and cannot do a full concussion exam, so he judges: state "immediately after the blow - stunned, unbalanced, uncoordinated?", the "approach to corner - unbalanced, swaying", pupils and nystagmus, speech ("incorrect, slurred?"), a rudimentary balance test. Any doubt: stop. [S][Q] https://worldboxing.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/WB-Medical-Handbook-2025.pdf
- Legs go first because they run on the longest nerve pathways; cerebellum hits give ataxia and nystagmus (ABC "Anatomy of the Knockout" slide deck, Wisconsin copy). [snip] https://dsps.wi.gov/Credentialing/BoxingMMA/2022-ABC-THE-ANATOMY-OF-THE-KNOcKOUT-FINAL.pdf (link now 404)
- Ringside doctor on a delayed legs-out: Barboza's clean right, Burgos "seemed to just brush off"; about four seconds later his legs gave out. Dr. Abbasi: the brain was "on the border", and his normal head movement "probably made things worse". [S][Q] https://www.lowkickmma.com/ringside-physician-breaks-down-shane-burgos-bizarre-knockout-ufc-262/ (MMA, same brain)

Other sightings of the tell set:

- Leonard-Hearns 1: Leonard's counter left in R6 "and the legs of 'The Hitman' buckled", then a battered Hearns survived; in R13 "For the first time since the seventh, Hearns' legs buckled." So a hard head hurt showed in the legs for about a round, then cleared enough to win R9-12. [S][Q] https://www.thefightcity.com/?p=8442
- Fury-Usyk 1 R9 (analyst): "he was out on his feet, but he was okay. He was moving and trying to avoid shots... His legs weren't there." Legs bad but still defending = a ref can let it go. [S][Q] https://boxingnewsonline.net/news/macklin-backs-referee-controversial-call-during-usyk-fury
- "Chicken dance", "stutter steps", "flat feet", "wide base": **not found in any source**. [B] Only "wobbly", "swaying", "unbalanced", "stunned" are in official lists. Closest on wide base is Wikipedia's neurology of ataxia, which I did not open.

## 2. Hurt, but hiding it (the bluff)

Real cases:

- **Mayweather-Mosley R2 (May 2010).** Overhand right, "a swoon, about as close as he could get to hitting the canvas." Then "after recovering his footing, Mayweather just smiled, as if to say: 'You didn't hurt me.' A lie, of course. He'd been hurt, badly." Mosley: "He was hurt real bad. I think that's the most he's ever been hurt in his whole career." Mayweather: "I just went back to my corner and told myself to relax," then switched to the pull-counter. [S][Q] https://amp.foxsports.com/stories/boxing/mayweather-gets-most-impressive-win . A second angle: he also held and moved to survive the round; Wikipedia says he "bounced back" and controlled R3-4. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd_Mayweather_Jr._vs._Shane_Mosley . Opponent (a vet) saw it, but could not finish: Mosley says Floyd kept his hands high and the body shots did not do enough [snip, ESPN]. So reading the bluff is not enough; a covered, moving man is hard to finish.
- **Rosado (detached retina, vs Lemieux).** "I can't let him know that I'm hurt," and he came back stronger in R4. That is an injury, not a head hurt, but it is a first-person admission that hiding is a deliberate tactic. [S][Q] https://dazn.com/en-CA/news/boxing/billy-joe-saunders-couldnt-back-up-his-talk-against-canelo-alvarez-says-gabriel-rosado/1lmgkicdx2ql01at7nbiij6s7n
- **Ali-Foreman.** Foreman: "I hit him hard to the jaw and he held me and whispered in my ear: 'That all you got, George?' I realized that this ain't what I thought it was." A hurt-or-not bluff with words, and it worked on the opponent's head. [S][Q] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rumble_in_the_Jungle
- **Fury-Wilder 1 R12.** Fury lay still, then got up fast and moved on request; the ref did not read him as gone. Fury was "tired but not extremely hurt" per the ref. Wilder says he saw his eyes roll back. Real hurt and good recovery look different from a bluff only through the eyes and the direction-change test. [S] (Reiss URL above)
- **Burgos.** The opposite of a bluff: a man who really looked fine for 4 s, then his legs left. "Looks fine" right after a flush shot proves nothing for about 2-5 s. [S] (Burgos URL above)
- **McCracken / Joshua-Ruiz.** From coach_c: saw "glazy-eyed" Joshua after the R3 knockdown. Already sourced there, I did not re-open it.

The documented teaching on tells vs the face:

- Title Boxing (gear/coaching blog): "They can wave you forward after taking a solid punch, but their eyes may tip you off... Some fighters can put up a good front, but there's no hiding the moment, no matter how brief... a sudden lack of focus, fear, uncertainty or could just be a blank stare." [S][Q] https://www.titleboxing.com/blogs/news/an-eye-for-an-eye-behind-your-opponents-exterior
- **"Watch the legs, not the face" as a saying: not found.** What is documented is "eyes AND legs, and make him move or turn" (ref manual, Reiss, Steele, Bayless, World Boxing). Nobody in the official lists uses the facial expression (smile, nod, wave). That is the nearest confirmation. [S]
- **Who bluffs, vets or novices: no source compares.** [B] Mayweather is the case I have. Related, not the same: skilled boxers made MORE false alarms to fakes than low-skilled ones (Ripoll 1995, as cited in a review), but skilled athletes lose less accuracy overall. Combat-sport meta-analysis (27 studies): experts anticipate more accurately (effect 1.51) and react faster (-0.91), with fewer fixations. [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5449506 and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9650920
- Poker face as a learned "boxer cool" look: a book chapter exists (Lewandowski, *On Boxing*) but the PDF was blocked. [snip] https://library.oapen.org/bitstream/20.500.12657/77042/1/9781003196693_10.4324_9781003196693-3.pdf

## 3. Tired / gassed

- **Bayless:** the loaded punch ("sitting down on his punches") turns into a pushed punch. [S][Q] (Bayless URL above)
- **Mayweather-McGregor (2017).** McGregor's later rounds: "His reactions slower, his hands held low... he would be looking up at the clock shortly after attempting to evade Mayweather's pressure"; R9 "skirting across the ropes", R10 on "wobbly legs". McGregor himself: "I get a little wobbly when I'm tired, it is fatigue." So tired can look like hurt. [S][Q] https://cagesidepress.com/2017/08/27/mayweather-vs-mcgregor-recap/ and https://www.sportinglife.com/boxing/news/mayweather-stops-mcgregor-in-10th/100731
- Mosley (same Mayweather fight): "By the seventh round, Mosley look winded"; "look of resignation" walking to the corner after R9. [S] (Wikipedia Mayweather-Mosley)
- **Foreman (Ali, 1974).** Burned energy on the ropes; staggered at the start of R4 and several times late in R5; "after the fifth round, he looked increasingly worn out"; in R8 "punching and defense became ineffective", then down. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rumble_in_the_Jungle
- **Klitschko-Brewster (2004).** R5: "Klitschko appeared somewhat fatigued", two hooks, ropes knockdown, down again after the bell, stopped. A gas crash can go from "somewhat fatigued" to stopped inside one round. (Later claims about blood sugar, disputed.) [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wladimir_Klitschko_vs._Lamon_Brewster
- **Hearns-Leonard 1 (R13-14).** Steward later: Hearns "was out of fuel" [snip]. Leonard's 25 unanswered punches in R13 landed on a tired, legs-buckling man. [S] (Fight City URL above)
- **Hands/clinch numbers (amateur, 3 x 3 min, 26 elite boxers).** Time with guard visibly lowered: R1 24.1 s, R2 31.0 s, R3 34.1 s (+29% then +41%). Clinch time: 11.1, 17.1, 19.8 s. Bounce (on-the-spot) time fell, step time flat, total punches the same. Fatigue rating rose each round. Winners and losers both did it. [S] Dunn et al. 2017, PLOS ONE: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5747423
- **Pro output.** Heavyweight title fights, 1990-2020: 37.6 punches thrown and 13.3 landed per round (34.6%); winners 44 thrown, losers 31. No by-round fade in the paper. [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8786172
- De La Hoya-Sturm CompuBox: over 100 in R1, then 72 a round for five rounds, then 54 a round in the last six. Sturm held about 45 all the way. A fade of about 25% shows up for the man who spent early; a paced man shows none. Other fights go the other way (Broner averages 39 then 47; Melikuziev closed the gap late). [snip] https://www.boxingscene.com/telling-facts-with-hard-numbers--1604
- Eyes drift off the opponent when tired (to the clock, corner, crowd). Only coaching blogs / forum posts say it. [B] https://expertboxing.com/how-to-fight-tired . Forum claim in comments: tired men breathe with mouth open and keep knees straight, legs usually do not wobble, and a pro with good muscle memory can look the same as a hurt man except in the eyes. [B]
- Mouth open, head up, flat feet: nobody official lists them. [B]

## 4. Hurt to the body

- **De La Hoya-Hopkins (2004).** "It was a perfect body shot. Boom, right there. It paralyzes you. It's like you're stuck, you can't move." Ended at 1:38 of R9; he also said "I couldn't recover. Believe me, I wanted to." Arum: "That kind of shot does paralyze you, even though it only lasts for 30 seconds." Mayweather Sr. (his trainer): "I'm not saying I expected him to go down on a body shot, but that is pretty common in boxing." [S][Q] https://lasvegassun.com/news/2004/sep/20/hopkins-calls-the-tune/
- **Davis-Garcia (Apr 2023), delayed.** "Garcia remained on his feet before backing up a few steps. Then he went to his right knee." Garcia: "I couldn't breathe. I was going to get back up, but I just couldn't get up." Davis read "his facial expression" and shook-head "No". CBS: a man whose body won't work looks like he quit. [S][Q] https://www.news4jax.com/sports/2023/04/23/gervonta-davis-knocks-out-ryan-garcia-with-body-shot-in-7th/ and https://www.cbssports.com/boxing/news/gervonta-davis-vs-ryan-garcia-results-takeaways-what-we-learned-about-each-fighter-and-where-they-go-next/
- Hatton-Castillo 1: left hook to the liver "took a few seconds to bring Castillo to his knees" (listicle). [S, weak] https://evolve-mma.com/blog/15-of-the-most-epic-body-shot-knockouts-in-boxing-history/
- Ward-Gatti 1, R9: body hook KD about 15 s into the round, Gatti got up. [snip]
- **How long it lasts:** Wikipedia: liver pain "mostly lasts only about 30 seconds to one minute"; Arum "30 seconds". Not enough for a 10 count, which is why they stay down. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_shot
- **Delay size:** from these cases, 0 s (De La Hoya, Ward) to about 2-5 s (Davis, Castillo, Burgos head). Three of four famous body KOs (all but Gatti) stayed down for the 10, but that sample is the famous finishes, so it is biased. (guess: engine body KDs stay down more often than head KDs.)
- **Ref rule:** a man who goes down from a body shot and claims a low blow forces an immediate call; picking up the count means "legal". A bent-over man is "up". [S] (ABC manual)
- **Does the head guard drop?** Coach writers: "striking the body often forces one to drop the hands, which can then open up the head" (Breaking Muscle). Elbow behaviour: a liver punch often starts as a body hook, "the defending boxer puts his elbow down and begins to roll with the punch", exposing the side/back (Wikipedia). "Elbows clamp": **not found** as such; the defensive move named is "crunch down" to shield. [S][B] https://breakingmuscle.com/analysis-of-the-liver-shot-throwing-and-defending/
- **Atlas "knock knock".** Throw a body jab as a single punch and "analyze how they react and how long that reaction takes. If the opponent reacts big and they leave their left side of the chin open"... then go upstairs next exchange. So the cue is his reaction to a probe, not a general body-damage state. [S][Q] https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/start-low-and-end-high-with-creative-combinations-from-teddy-atlas
- "Kill the body and the head will die": Sam Langford to Joe Louis; Louis in 1935 before Carnera: "I will concentrate my attack on the mid-section for the first three rounds and then switch to the head." [S][Q] https://barrypopik.com/blog/kill_the_body_and_the_head_will_die_boxing_adage
- **Numbers:** body punches were about 12% of punches at amateur worlds 2013/2015 and "much more effective than strikes to head" (thesis abstract). CompuBox: body is about 26% of landed on average (Pacquiao threw 20%). **No numbers found on body-then-head KO share or on how often body KDs are delayed.** [S] https://dspace.cuni.cz/handle/20.500.11956/101201 and https://www.badlefthook.com/2019/1/18/18188388/pacquiao-broner-by-the-numbers
- Turning away / bending at the waist / taking a knee late as listed tells: **not found** in an official source. [B] Davis-Garcia is the one clear "backs up, then kneels" sequence.

## 5. Fake hurt, fake tired, rope-a-dope

- **Rope-a-dope** is a defensive tactic, not a faked injury: lean on the ropes, cover, let him burn gas. Ali-Foreman, Holmes-Mercer (1992), Watson-Benn (1989), Locche; Pacquiao used it to test Cotto's power. Needs "a strong chin". [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rope-a-dope
- Mayweather-McGregor: "Our game-plan was to take our time, let him shoot all his heavy shots early on and then take him down at the end." A pace plan; one recap calls the first 9 minutes "playing possum". [S][Q] (Sporting Life, Cageside URLs above)
- **Playing possum** (act hurt or tired to draw him in) is in glossaries. [snip] https://www.titleboxing.com/pages/boxing-dictionary . **I found no verified case of a pro faking a head-hurt to draw a knockout.** [B]
- Accused fakes are about stopping the fight, not baiting: Hopkins-Dawson (Oct 2011) shoulder, ruled no contest, Dawson says faked; Hopkins produced AC-joint documentation. [S] https://www.inquirer.com/philly/sports/boxing/20120417_Hopkins_wants_to_beat_Dawson_to_clear_his_name.html . Broner "totally faked being hurt" after a headbutt: "a delayed reaction... went down about a second or two later", penalised point. A fake looks late. [S][Q] https://larrybrownsports.com/boxing/adrien-broner-flop-headbutt-marcos-maidana-video/214044
- Fury faked injuries in camp to catch a leak. [snip] Not a ring tactic.
- Rarity: no counts exist. My read is feigning to bait is under 1% of fights (guess); covering up and letting him burn is common (rope-a-dope style).

Against coach_a / coach_b / coach_c / mind_finish / mind_vision.

Now sourced (was B / D):
- "Eyes first (glazy), then legs" (coach_c 4; McCracken). **Confirmed** by Bayless, Steele, ABC manual, World Boxing handbook. New: eyes are hard to see from outside the ring (Bayless), so range matters.
- Legs show a bluff (coach_a 5, coach_b 7, coach_c 3). **Partly confirmed:** Reiss says the hurt shows when he must change direction; ABC manual asks for walk, left, right. "Wide base", "30% slower reset", "two of three signs" stay design.

- mind_finish 1g "ref has no number": **confirmed**. ABC rule: "if the referee has any doubt, stop the bout."
- Tired man's hands drop (coach_a 2, coach_b 8, coach_c 7). **Confirmed** for hands (guard-drop time +41% by R3 amateur; McGregor hands low). **Head rises / mouth open / flat feet: still B.** The 25% output-drop rule (coach_c 7): only one pro example (De La Hoya), not general.
- Mild stun clears in seconds to minutes (mind_finish 2b). **Consistent:** Hearns hurt R6, legs buckled in R7, won R9-12; Mayweather hurt R2, in charge R3.
- Corner "show me something" (Bayless), also coach_c 6 (limit on "get him through"): **sourced** in spirit.

Still unverified:
- **"Elbows clamp when body-hurt" (coach_a 1, coach_c 8):** no source. Closest: crunch down to cover, elbow down when rolling. Treat as design.
- "Turns away / bends at waist / late knee": no official source.
- **"Chicken dance", "stutter steps":** nothing.
- "Watch the legs, not the face" as a saying: not found.

Wrong or needs changing:
- **mind_finish 1a "tell shows 0.2-0.6 s after the shot".** Too fast for the tricky cases. Burgos' legs went at about 4 s; Davis-Garcia backed up a few steps first. Use 0 to 5 s.
- coach_a 6 "hurt man who clinches / turns away / looks at ref is buying time": holding right after the shot and looking at the corner are read by refs (Steele, Taylor) as hurt, not stalling. Spitting the mouthpiece is the stall, as mind_finish says.
- mind_vision "experts read deception better": for boxing the one study cited says skilled boxers made more false alarms to fakes. That is feints, not hurt-bluffs, small sample, secondhand.

## For Last Bell

The engine sends the watcher: cut, swell, body, arms, fold, legs, wobble. Numbers marked (guess) are mine; everything else is from the sources above.

**Reading a hurt head**
1. If the man is hurt in the head, legs and wobble are the honest cues. Face and gloves are the bluff channel. A vet weights legs, wobble and the eyes; a novice weights arms (hands up or down) and face. Evidence: Reiss, ABC manual, Title Boxing; Ripoll gaze (mind_vision).
2. If he is hurt and must turn, pivot or change direction, then legs/wobble cue goes to full strength. If he is planted on the ropes or walking straight, then the cue is about 40% of full (guess). Evidence: Reiss "hard to hide... when they have to change direction".
3. If a flush shot lands, then wait 2-5 s before deciding "he's fine". A delayed legs-out is real (Burgos 4 s, Davis-Garcia). Also treat "he fired back in the first 1-2 s" as no proof. (guess: 2-5 s window.)
4. Eyes cue ("glazy") is short range: only inside about clinch or mid range, or from the ref and own corner. Beyond that range, nobody reads it. Evidence: Bayless "hard for anyone else to see that if they are not in the ring".
5. Bluff behaviours (smile, nod, wave in, head shake, fire back) carry no information for a vet and mislead a novice. Rough weight: novice reads them at about 50% (guess), vet at 0-10% (guess). No source gives who bluffs more; assume vets bluff slightly better (guess).
6. If a hurt man covers up, keeps hands high and moves, then the hunter's finish chance drops even when the read is right (Mosley). Hunting a hurt-but-covering man should cost more gas per landed shot than a hurt man with hands down. (guess)
7. If he is hurt and looks at his corner, does not respond to "are you OK", or leans on the ropes, then stop probability goes up. (Taylor, Fury-Usyk, ABC ropes rule.)

**How long a hurt shows**
8. A head hurt shows heavily for 1 round at most without more hits; the 1-minute rest clears most of it. Evidence: Hearns R6 to R7, Mayweather R2 to R3, Marquez (3 KDs R1). Rough decay (guess): wobble cue half-gone in 10-15 s, legs cue in 30-60 s, "slightly off" through the next round. Each clean hit while hurt resets part of that clock.

**Ref and doctor**
9. Ref stop order, from the sources: can't defend / no punches back > legs gone when he rises or turns > eyes not tracking > no answer to "OK?" > looks to corner > unsteady walk to corner. If two or more hold after a knockdown or a run of shots, an average ref stops. If eyes + legs both gone, stop with no questions (Steele).
10. Ref style knob: early ref ("rather stop sooner") stops on two cues. Late ref wants three, or an unanswered flurry. Both exist in real life.
11. Doctor, at the bell: looks at the state right after the last big shot, the walk to the corner (sway), pupils/nystagmus, speech, balance. Rule: if wobble was high at the bell or he swayed to the corner, flag; stop on any abnormal sign. Rare in practice (guess: under 2% of rounds).

**Tired**
12. If a man is tired, then watch hands (arms cue: more time lowered, guard drops by about 30% in R2, 40% by R3 in amateurs), more clinching (about +55% R2, +80% R3 amateur), slower reactions and looking away. Scale to 12 rounds with sim (guess).
13. Output only falls much if he spent early. Over-spenders fade about 25% (De La Hoya); paced men hold. So: output drop is driven by early gas spent, not by round number. Pro heavyweight base is about 38 thrown per round (aside).
14. "Pushing" punches: when tired, loaded punches turn to arm punches (Bayless): lower damage per landed punch before output falls. (guess: -10 to -15% damage.)
15. A tired man can look hurt: wobbles on big swings (McGregor). The legs cue from gas should be smaller and appear mainly after a miss or a big swing, while eyes stay focused. A vet tells tired from hurt by the eyes at close range.
16. Gas crash is sudden: Klitschko "somewhat fatigued" to stopped in one round; Foreman after R5; McGregor R9-10. When stamina is very low and the opponent has gas, raise hunt weight fast. Watchers should see tired cues 1-2 rounds before the crash.

**Body**
17. A body-hurt man reacts in one of three ways: (a) down at once, (b) delayed 2-5 s: stands, backs up, then kneels, (c) stays up bent over (fold) and covers or turns away. Stay-down after a body KD: high (famous cases mostly stayed down; guess 60%+). "I couldn't breathe... I was going to get up."
18. Body pain lasts about 30-60 s (Arum, Wikipedia): body KD is a 10-count loss unless he is up on a very short fuse.
19. Body to head: no number in any source. Use the reaction-to-a-probe rule (Atlas): throw one body jab; if his arms cue (guard) reacts big and the head is open, go upstairs next exchange (a Pro-and-up read; lower levels do not probe). Guard drop after repeated body hits: coach-writers say yes, number is design: about -5% guard height per landed body shot, cap -20% (guess; coach_b).
20. A bent-over (fold) man is ruled "up" and the count stops, but the manual calls it a sign the bout may need stopping. If a body-hurt man claims a low blow, the ref must call it at once: count picked up means legal.

**Bluff and fake**
21. Rope-a-dope = cover, high guard, hands up, take punches on arms. It is a style, not a faked hurt. Needs a strong chin. A hunter who sees a covering man at full stamina should not read it as "gassed". (guess: faked hurt or tired as a tactic: under 1% of fights; do not build a dedicated state.)
22. A fake looks late (Broner "a second or two later"); a real body shot also looks late (2-5 s). Not separable by timing alone, only by the eyes and breath.
