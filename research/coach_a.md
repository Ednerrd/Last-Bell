## 1. Cus D'Amato

### Philosophy / system
- Defense first. "Peek-a-boo": gloves tight to the cheeks, arms in against the torso, head moving, then explode forward. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cus_D'Amato (via search summary)
- Constant head movement: bob, weave, slip. Months of evasion work before punching in the Catskill gym. [S] https://www.leslieparke.com/blog/desire-and-disipline-the-day-job
- Move forward and cut the ring. Short, hard punches once inside. [S] https://evolve-mma.com/blog/breaking-down-mike-tysons-style-of-boxing/
- Tyson quoted: punch through the target, not at it. [S] https://shoyojack.substack.com/p/method-to-the-madness-mike-tyson-death-blow
- Critics: an efficient attack cannot come from that tight a guard. Tyson's head movement faded after Rooney was fired. [S] Wikipedia, Cus D'Amato / Kevin Rooney pages.
- The guard doubles as a lure: the high guard invites the opponent to throw. [S] (same search; unnamed source)
- Fear is a tool: "fear is like fire", control it and it works for you. Wording varies across sites, so not a verified original. [S] https://blog.revgear.com/famous-fight-quotes-fear-is-the-friend/
- Heroes and cowards feel the same fear; the difference is what they do with it. Attribution weak. [S] https://jamesmartialartsacademy.com/blog/30-quotes-from-cus-damato/

### Reading an opponent
- Sourced: nothing specific found.
- [B] Cus watched eyes and posture before the bell. A man who stares, blinks a lot or backs off a first feint is afraid. Fear shows early; go at it.
- [B] He treated the opponent's mind as the target as much as the body. Tyson's walk-in and stare were part of it.

### Traps / baiting
- Guard-as-lure, above [S].
- [B] Patterson's "gazelle punch" (leaping left hook) was a surprise entry built from a pattern. Unverified.

### Corner work
- Not documented in results. [B] Cus rarely worked the corner in Tyson's later fights; Rooney did.

### Finishing vs surviving
- [B] Cus's creed: a man is only beaten when he gives up. Pressure and a finish mindset. Unverified.
- Defense is how you survive: cover, move the head, tie up. [S] (general peek-a-boo summaries)

### Teaching / flaws
- Long defensive grounding first, punching second. [S] leslieparke link above.
- [B] Drills: shadow-box in front of mirror, rolling under a hanging rope/bag, slip-and-counter on a partner. Unverified.

### Size
- Peek-a-boo suits the shorter man: slip, close the gap, work inside. [S] https://evolve-mma.com/blog/breaking-down-mike-tysons-style-of-boxing/ (commentary, not a D'Amato quote).

## 2. Eddie Futch

### Philosophy / system
- Emphasis on defense, ring intelligence, and keeping the fighter's long-term health. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Futch
- Studied the opponent's holes and built a plan around them. Frazier beat Ali in 1971; Norton broke Ali's jaw in 1973. [S] https://www.ringmagazine.com/news/patrick-connor-its-been-25-years-since-we-lost-legendary-eddie-futch-1luKKXr8DWzo4OsqDwgnHW
- Connected with each fighter personally instead of forcing a template. [S] same Ring piece.
- Trained Frazier, Norton, Holmes, Berbick (four of the five men to beat Ali), plus Bowe and Montell Griffin. [S] Wikipedia / Boxing Hall of Fame.

### Reading an opponent
- Vision as a fight-ending tell: Futch judged Frazier could not see Ali's punches coming, and stopped it. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrilla_in_Manila and https://tss.ib.tv/boxing/boxing-articles-2005/2704-thrilla-in-manila-30th-anniversary
- Futch's own account: he began deciding in round 13 when a right knocked Frazier's mouthpiece out; gave Ali one more round hoping he would slow down; he did not. [S] same TSS piece.
- Spotted Ali's illegal habit of holding behind the neck (he claimed 133 times in the rematch) and worked to get the referee changed. [S] same sources.
- [B] Signs a vet reads: eyes, a man who stops answering, one hand held out, slow punch return. Unverified.

### Traps / baiting
- Norton: cross-armed guard, jab thrown up from a low hand, which Ali found hard to read. [S] https://www.boxing247.com/boxing-news/ken-norton-more-than-just-the-man-who-broke-alis-jaw/251000
- Against Ali's rope-a-dope Futch told Frazier to be patient and work the body. It gave Ali hip hematomas, but Ali's head shots closed Frazier's eye. [S] Wikipedia, Thrilla in Manila.

### Corner work
- Round 11, Manila: Frazier could barely see. Futch told him to stand more upright rather than bob and weave. Ali used the straighter target in round 12. [S] same Wikipedia piece. (The advice looks like a mistake in hindsight; that is the source's framing.)
- Stoppage line, reported: "Joe, the fight's over, I'm stopping it." After round 14. Frazier: "I want him, boss." Futch: "Sit down, son. It's all over. No one will ever forget what you did here today." The second half is [B], the first [S] https://archive.thedailystar.net/2005/10/02/d51002041238.htm
- Ali's corner: Dundee told Ali to keep going when he wanted to quit after 11. [S] Futch quoted in the Dundee search result.
- Holmes vs Cooney 1982: Futch and Ray Arcel together in corner; Holmes stopped Cooney. [S] Ring/Connor piece.

### Finishing vs surviving
- Futch protected the man: would rather lose the fight than risk permanent damage ("all it takes is one"). [S] Futch's own later account via TSS piece.
- [B] When a man is hurt, cover and keep the head moving; do not stand upright (see Manila).

### Teaching
- Norton said he learned more in two years with Futch than in all other years. [S] Wikipedia/Norton search summary.
- Considered Bowe the most naturally talented he trained; dropped him for lack of discipline. [S] https://www.secondsout.com/news/eddie-futch-best-he-trained/

### Size
- [B] Bowe, a big man: use the jab and height, tie up and uppercut when inside. Unverified.

## 3. Emanuel Steward

### Philosophy / system
- Kronk: aggressive boxer-puncher, knockout mindset, even in sparring. [S] https://www.pressreader.com/uk/the-independent-1029/20151215/282540132283857 (Andy Lee quoted)
- Defense taught as "hit and not get hit." [S] https://www.ringtv.com/?p=175529 (Eddie Mustafa Muhammad)
- Jab first, then power. With Klitschko: dominate behind the jab and take a half-step back until comfortable, then free the hands. [S] https://www.pressreader.com/uk/the-sunday-telegraph-sport/20170416/281513636023663
- Called Wladimir the most accurate single-punch knockout guy. [S] https://www.boxing247.com/weblog/archives/135429
- Kronk was a unit: other trainers worked the floor while Steward called instructions during sparring. [S] https://www.si.com/boxing/2012/10/25/emanuel-steward-nack-1981

### Reading an opponent
- Tired man: Hearns in 1981, seated in the corner with his head dropped. Steward: "He was out of fuel." [S] https://www.thefightcity.com/leonard-vs-hearns-sugar-ray-leonard-thomas-hearns-emanuel-steward-angelo-dundee-roberto-duran-las-vegas/ and https://www.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/29218877
- Leg state matters: Steward believed a massage before Hagler-Hearns weakened Hearns's legs. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Hagler_vs._Thomas_Hearns
- [B] Kronk lore: a man hurt to the body brings the elbows in and the head comes down; go to the head. Unverified.

### Traps / baiting
- Nothing sourced. [B] Steward leaned on a long jab and a right hand behind it; the jab hides the right.

### Corner work
- Send your fighter out first for the next round; waiting in the center gives the other man the edge. Quote: "Send your fighter out first for the next round." [S] https://www.ringtv.com/?p=175529
- Style: calm and clinical in technique, or an expletive-laden scolding to spark action. [S] same.
- Klitschko vs Chambers: urged the passive fighter to let his punches go; Klitschko stopped him in 12. [S] same RING source.
- 1981 Hearns-Leonard: Steward told Hearns to box and go back to fundamentals (jab, move). Hearns was stopped in 14. Steward called it the most painful experience of his life. [S] https://www.si.com/boxing/2012/10/25/emanuel-steward-nack-1981

### Finishing vs surviving
- Kronk: finish. Stoppage by KO prized. [S] Andy Lee, above.
- 1981 lesson: a puncher who abandons the ring for a slugfest can run out of fuel. [S] SI piece.

### Teaching
- Fundamentals first, tailored to the man (Lewis, Klitschko, McClellan). [S] Telegraph piece. McClellan's body-shot finish of Williamson: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_McClellan
- Criticized for too little conditioning work at Kronk. [S] Andy Lee, Independent.

### Size
- Klitschko / Lewis: long men use the jab and the half-step back to keep distance. [S] Telegraph piece.
- [B] Hearns (tall, long) had the same blueprint. Unverified.

## 4. Angelo Dundee

### Philosophy / system
- Finesse over force. "The only time I told him what to do was in the ring." Let the fighter's own style lead. [S] https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/split-gloves-and-loose-ropes-remembering-angelo-dundee
- Britannica: astute cornerman; closed and dressed cuts, picked opponents, psyched them out. [S] https://www.britannica.com/biography/Angelo-Dundee
- Soft-hearted temperament per Ferdie Pacheco. [S] https://grantland.com/features/the-life-legendary-boxing-trainer-angelo-dundee/

### Reading an opponent
- Noticed Foreman's footwork in clinches left him off balance. Ali used it. [S] https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/dundee-angelo
- [B] Dundee read the man's habits from film and sparring; he looked for what a man does when he is tired (flat feet, hands low, head upright).

### Traps / baiting
- Rope-a-dope: lean on the ropes, let Foreman punch himself out. Ropes loosened (Boxing Scene) or "tightened" (Britannica): sources conflict. [S] boxingscene link above and Britannica link above.
- [B] Leonard-Duran II ("No Mas"): Dundee and Leonard used showboating and lateral movement to make the pressure fighter chase and lose his head. Unverified.

### Corner work
- Liston I, 1964: Ali could not see after round 4. Dundee shoved him off the stool: "Run!" Keep moving until vision clears. [S] boxingscene link.
- Frazier III: Ali wanted to quit after 11; Dundee talked him into continuing. [S] Futch quoted on Wikipedia.
- Leonard-Hearns 1981, behind on cards, left eye swollen: "You're blowing it, son. You're blowing it." Also "This is what separates the men from the boys." [S] https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/dundee-angelo and https://www.npr.org/transcripts/146405577
- After round 12 Hearns led 116-112, 117-111, 117-112; Leonard was told to change the fight. [S] https://www.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/29218877
- Cooper 1963: Dundee reportedly split the glove to buy recovery time. Legend, unconfirmed. [S] boxingscene link.

### Finishing vs surviving
- Hearns-Leonard round 13-14: Leonard "like a man possessed" attacked a tired Hearns and it was stopped in 14. [S] https://tss.ib.tv/boxing/boxing-articles-2006/4331-hearnsleonard-i-the-warning
- Surviving: when blinded, move and stay out of range (Liston). [S] above.

### Teaching
- [B] One instruction at a time, short, repeated. Unverified.

### Size
- [B] Ali (tall) kept the jab and distance; Leonard against Hearns lost the jab battle because Hearns was longer, so Dundee ordered him to close and work inside. Partly supported by the 1981 summary ("Hearns's jab").

## 5. Ray Arcel

- "Nobody quits in my corner" after "No Mas". [S] same.
- 22 world champions (Benny Leonard, Barney Ross, Braddock, Zale, Duran, others). [S] same.

### Finishing vs surviving

## 6. Teddy Atlas

### Philosophy / system
- Body work: "You can be tough all you want to the head, but you can't be tough to the body." Body shots accumulate and the body starts to shut down. [S] https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/hard-hitting-body-shots-with-teddy-atlas
- Cupid punch: uppercut under the heart that freezes the man a moment longer, long enough for follow-ups. [S] https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/hit-them-in-the-heart-with-teddy-atlas
- Burp shot: solar plexus, "a terrible place to take a shot". [S] https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/the-burp-body-shot-with-teddy-atlas
- Body punching course: liver is the most common cumulative-damage target; solar plexus the less-discussed one. [S] https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/how-and-why-you-need-to-target-the-solar-plexus-according-to-teddy-atlas
- Courses are paid products, so wording is marketing-tinged. [S] same.

### Reading an opponent
- Nothing sourced on hurt / tired / bluff tells. Searches returned only sales pages.
- [B] On commentary he often says a hurt man holds, turns away, looks at the ref, or throws wild to buy time. Unverified; consistent with common boxing knowledge.

### Traps / baiting
- "Fishing": put out a lure and tempt the fish. [S] https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/lure-your-opponent-into-a-trap-with-teddy-atlas
- Simplest trap: jab, step back, invite him to step in with the straight right; when he misses, counter into the open man. [S] https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/setting-traps-by-teddy-atlas
- Jab, hop back, then hook or cross. The jab gets attention and makes him aggressive. [S] same.
- "Knock on the front door, come in the side": feint, then angled attack (to Masvidal, 2019). [S] https://www.sportskeeda.com/mma/news-watch-when-teddy-atlas-showed-jorge-masvidal-feinting-technique-nate-diaz-fight
- To Poirier: feint, get him to show his hand before you show yours, do not walk straight into a counter-puncher. [S] same.
- Inside counters timed to his entry. [S] https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/how-to-time-your-counter-punches-on-the-inside-with-teddy-atlas (title only)
- Practice: with a partner, or visualize while shadowboxing. [S] https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/why-you-need-to-know-how-to-set-traps-according-to-teddy-atlas

### Corner work
- Moorer vs Holyfield 1994: angry that Moorer was "just doing enough" and "not digging"; sat in the corner during the rest. Criticized as overly dramatic; Atlas says he did what he believed that fighter needed. [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Atlas and https://www.baltimoresun.com/1994/04/23/moorer-stuns-holyfield-to-capture-title/
- Moorer won on a majority decision. [S] Baltimore Sun.

### Finishing vs surviving
- Cupid / burp shots as a way to freeze a man and follow up. [S] above.
- [B] Atlas has often said when the man is hurt you must hurry and throw with purpose, not tentatively. Unverified.

### Teaching
- Footwork course ("Boxing's Transit System") includes the Setting Traps chapter. [S] https://dynamicstriking.com/products/footwork-boxings-transit-system-by-teddy-atlas
- Gym origin: Cus's gym, then Tyson. [S] https://www.lowkickmma.com/teddy-atlas-pulling-gun-15-mike-tyson/ (background)

### Size
- Nothing sourced.

## 7. Gil Clancy

### Philosophy / system
- Never change a fighter from who he really is. [S] radio comment via https://tss.ib.tv/boxing/emile-griffith-goes-the-distance/
- Fundamentals: if Foreman punched as fundamentally correctly as Quarry he would never have lost. [S] same.
- Former NYC schoolteacher; master's degree in teaching (NYU). Taught like a teacher. [S] https://bendbulletin.com/?p=97718
- Trained Griffith, Quarry, Foreman, De La Hoya, Cooney, Valdez. [S] same.

### Reading an opponent
- Valdez was the only man to floor Monzon as champ (round 2, 1977 rematch). [S] TSS Griffith piece.
- [B] Clancy, a TV analyst 1988-2006, often described a hurt fighter by his legs and his eyes. Unverified.

### Traps / baiting
- Nothing sourced.

### Corner work
- Griffith-Paret III (1962): before the bout, told Griffith to keep punching once inside. In round 12 Griffith landed 17 punches in about 5 seconds with no response (Clancy counted from replay). [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Paret_vs._Emile_Griffith_III
- Held Griffith back after the weigh-in insult. [S] same.
- Cited in Dave Anderson's *In the Corner*. [S] same.

### Finishing vs surviving
- Keep punching while the man is open and hurt: Paret case. The cost was Paret's death. [S] above.
- Griffith vs Monzon: stopped in 1971, lost a disputed decision in the rematch. [S] TSS Griffith piece.

### Teaching
- Teaching-first approach, amateur system (Golden Gloves for Griffith). [S] https://bendbulletin.com/?p=97718

### Size
- Nothing sourced.

## For Last Bell

How to read: **evidence** is S (a sourced statement behind it), B (common boxing/trainer lore, unverified) or D (design inference). Numbers are starting values to tune in the sim, not trainer quotes. Trainer tags in brackets.

### AI boxer

1. If the opponent's body is hurt (stamina or body damage past about 40%), his elbows clamp and the head opens. Go upstairs for 1-2 punches after the body shot, then back to the body. Evidence: B + S (Atlas on body accumulating). [Atlas, Steward, Futch]
2. If the opponent is tired (late rounds, stamina below about 35%), expect his hands to sag and his head to rise. Raise odds of straight shots down the middle and an uppercut by +15-25%. Evidence: B + S (Steward: Hearns "out of fuel" with head dropped). [Steward, Dundee]
3. If he is tired and you are not, change the fight to pressure and volume (+punch rate, +20%). Leonard did this in rounds 13-14 and it stopped the fight. Evidence: S. [Dundee, Steward]
4. If your own stamina is low, stop trading. Move, jab and hold; do not abandon the boxing plan for a brawl. Steward's 1981 lesson with Hearns. Evidence: S. [Steward]
5. Bluff check: a hurt man who "acts fine" still shows it in the legs (wide base, slow reset) and in how fast he returns his hands. If his recovery after a landed shot is slower than his baseline by about 30%, treat him as hurt regardless of what the stance says. Hide chance should fail for a vet-level observer, work against a rookie. Evidence: B, D; no sourced tell lists. [Futch, Atlas, Clancy]
6. A hurt man who clinches, turns away, looks at the ref or throws wild is buying time. Do not wait: throw 3-4 punch bursts right at the break, and punch when he lets go. Evidence: B (Atlas commentary, unverified). [Atlas, Clancy]
7. Keep punching when he is open and hurt. After a clean flush hit on a hurt man, commit to 4-6 punches before breaking off (Griffith got 17 in 5 seconds). The ref's stop is the limit; the real risk is the unanswered punches stop. Evidence: S (Clancy). [Clancy, Steward, Dundee]
8. Bait a known habit. If he reliably steps in and throws the straight right after a jab-and-step-back (seen 3+ times), throw the jab, step back about one step, and counter his miss with a hook or a cross. Evidence: S. [Atlas, Dundee]
9. Feint to make him show his hand. If he counters every first feint (seen 2+ times), feint twice and punch the open side on the third. Evidence: S (Atlas to Poirier: show your hand before you show yours). [Atlas, Cus]
10. Do not walk straight into a counter-puncher. Shift angle (come in from the side) before entering. Evidence: S. [Atlas]
11. Guard as a lure. A fighter in a high guard can leave the front hand slightly open to invite a punch he is ready to slip. Use only for fighters with strong head movement. Evidence: S, D. [Cus]
12. Hurt and blind in one eye or cut: do not stand upright and square (Futch's Manila advice made Frazier an easier target). Cover the bad side, circle toward the good eye, keep moving. Evidence: S for Manila, S for Dundee/Liston "run until vision clears". [Futch, Dundee]
13. Hurt: survive. Move away from the ropes, tie up, keep the head moving, and refuse the exchange for 1-2 exchanges. Do not quit: only a corner can stop it. Evidence: S (Liston, Arcel "nobody quits"). [Dundee, Arcel, Cus]
14. Shorter man vs longer: slip and close, work inside and to the body, avoid trading at the end of the jab. Taller man: jab and half-step back, tie up inside, uppercut on entry. Evidence: S (Cus/Tyson commentary, Steward/Klitschko), D for the tie-up. [Cus, Steward, Futch]

### AI corner

15. Between rounds give 1-2 instructions, one clear plan change, not a list. Evidence: B (Dundee, Steward style), partly S ("only time I told him what to do was in the ring"). [Dundee, Steward]
16. If your man is behind on the cards after round 6-8 by 2+ points, the call is harder: "you're blowing it, change the fight now". Raise aggression +15%; if he is also fresh, push pace. Evidence: S (Dundee, Leonard-Hearns). [Dundee, Atlas]
17. If your man is ahead and the opponent is hurt or tired, tell him to finish (more volume, body then head). If he is ahead on the cards and fresh, tell him to box and keep the jab. Evidence: S (Steward at Klitschko-Chambers: finish; Hearns lesson: box). [Steward]
18. If your man's eye is closing or he cannot see (swelling shut or cut over one eye), the corner reduces the target: tell him to move and keep range, and stop the fight if he cannot avoid punches. Futch: give him one more round, then stop. Rough threshold: stop after 2 rounds of landing nothing and taking every punch cleanly. Evidence: S (Manila). [Futch, Dundee]
19. Send your man out first for the next round. Small psychological edge; in the sim a first-step bonus at the bell. Evidence: S. [Steward]
20. Do not change a fighter's natural style in a corner talk. Orders must fit the guard and style (a pressure fighter gets "keep working", not "box"). Evidence: S (Arcel on Duran, Clancy). [Arcel, Clancy]
21. Easy learnable flaws in weaker fighters, to exploit and to drill: (a) steps in straight after a jab and gets countered; (b) comes forward upright (Manila); (c) hands drop after missing or after tiring; (d) stops guarding the body when the head is busy. Rookie mistakes the AI corner can call out by name after 2-3 sightings. Evidence: S (Atlas trap, Futch Manila), B for (c), (d). [Atlas, Futch, Cus]

### Priority for the next build

- First: rules 1, 2, 5 (body-hurt / tired / hurt-bluff reads). They match what the sim already tracks (stamina, body damage, recovery).
- Second: rules 8, 9 (habit bait). Needs opponent memory of repeated actions, which the brain has.
- Third: corner rules 15-19.
- Everything marked B should be tuned by sim, not trusted as a historical fact.
