# How a man gets a title shot

By Muse (Stephie), Nov 16, 2026, from the bodies' own rulebooks (WBC and IBF read in full; WBO and WBA through official resolutions quoting their rules) and fight history. Why: the career's gate is top 5 and gave 0 title shots in 54 test careers. Pairs with `title_path.md` (when the first shot comes). Condensed by Claude; **Muse's message was cut off in the real-examples table** (after Usyk and part of Stiverne): the rest is missing.

**Headline:** the real gate is **top 15, not top 5**, and even top 15 has written exceptions. A top-5-only gate is far stricter than reality: most of why the career never gives a shot.

## The gate

- **WBC rule 3.3:** top 10 freely; 11–15 with a majority Board vote; also a champion or highly rated man from another division, another WBC champion (Diamond, Silver, Emeritus, in Recess), a former world champion or "elite challenger", another body's champion, or with a 2/3 vote under "special circumstances", anyone.
- **IBF:** optional defenses against "any contender rated in the top fifteen" with the Chairman's OK; other bodies' champions can be "elite contenders" for unifications.
- **WBO:** between mandatories, any of the 15 best, with Committee approval.
- **WBA:** no written top-15 floor found; the Committee has broad say (flag: don't assume one).
- **Outside the top 15, documented:** Andy Ruiz Jr. vs Joshua (June 1, 2019) was WBC #18 and unrated by the WBO (WBA #12, IBF #14); the WBA moved him #12 → #5 the day he signed. Pacquiao for the WBC welterweight title (July 19, 2025) after four years out: put in at #5, then taken out of the rankings, the fight still allowed under the rules for legendary former champions. Amir Khan for the WBC middleweight title (2016) without ever having fought at middleweight.

## The mandatory

The contender a body orders the champion to face by a deadline; refuse (without an exception) and he's stripped. **Being #1 doesn't make you the mandatory automatically**: each body names him separately.

| Body | Who's the mandatory | How often the champ must face one |
|---|---|---|
| WBC | President + Board name him: a final eliminator, a named qualified challenger, or an ordered unification ("the highest rated boxer is never, solely by virtue of such position, the official mandatory") | At least once a year; a new champ inherits the mandatory of the man he beat |
| WBA | The Committee, or an eliminator; "generally the highest rated boxer, or a regional champion rated in the top 5" | 9 months (12 at heavy) if the champ was the official contender; 120 days (180 at heavy) if not; unified 18 months, undisputed 21, super 24 |
| IBF | The Chairman names the "leading available contender" ~60 days before it's due (injured, signed elsewhere, suspended = unavailable, next man up) | Every 9 months (heavy: first within a year); the strictest |
| WBO | The Committee picks "the highest classified available boxer"; a ranked man can petition in writing | 9 months (12 at heavy); a Super Champion 18; at most two in that time, 90+ days apart |

- **Champ refuses:** stripped. IBF: no purse-bid contract inside 15 days = vacated + out of the ratings 6+ months. Usyk vacated the IBF belt (June 2024) rather than face mandatory Dubois; Hamzah Sheeraz vacated the WBO 168 belt (Sept 2026) rather than face Alimkhanuly after the purse bid.
- **Challenger refuses:** WBC can take his status; IBF names the next man and drops the refuser below #10 for 6+ months; WBO picks the next man.
- **Step-aside money:** a mandatory gets paid to wait while the champ takes a bigger fight (common practice, in no rulebook).

## Eliminators

A fight between high-ranked contenders the body orders; the winner becomes the mandatory, or gets closer.
- **WBC:** only a **final** eliminator makes the mandatory; it can order another one after a disputed result, and take the status away for a bad result, a poor showing or inactivity. Purse 50/50.
- **IBF:** #1 and #2 stay **empty** until two top-5 available men (ranked 10 or better) fight a 12-rounder; a man in shorter fights can't rank above #3. The Chairman can order a box-off among the top four available. Refuse = below #10 for 6+ months. Purses: #1 vs #2 50/50; #1 or #2 vs anyone 75/25; #3 or lower vs #3 or lower 60/40.
- **WBA:** the Committee or an eliminator. **WBO:** the Committee can order one; an interim title fight can be the eliminator.
- An eliminator win doesn't promise a quick shot: unifications, exceptions and turns between the bodies push it back.

## Voluntary defenses

The champ (and his promoter) picks the man, within the rankings, with the body's OK.
- **WBC:** against qualified challengers (top 10 free, 11–15 with a vote, the exceptions above); about 3 defenses a year (every 120 days), voluntaries fill the gaps.
- **IBF ("optional"):** any top 15 with approval; done 60+ days before the mandatory's due.
- **WBO:** any top 15 with approval. **WBA:** in the time before the 9-month deadline; no non-official challenger within 60 days after it runs out.
- Used for bigger money or less risk, staying busy while the mandatory is out, or buying time. Lose a voluntary and the new champ inherits the mandatory.

## Purse bids

Ordered fight → a free-negotiation window (30 days WBC, IBF, WBA; 20 WBO) → no deal = sealed promoter bids, the highest wins, the fight inside 90 days (WBC, IBF).

| Body | Champ / challenger | Vacant |
|---|---|---|
| WBC | 70/30 of the 90% left after a 10% winner's bonus | 50/50 |
| WBA | 75/25 | not verified |
| IBF | 65/35 vs a #1 or #2; 85/15 vs #3 or lower | 50/50 |
| WBO | 75/25 at home or neutral; 80/20 in the challenger's country | not verified |

Usyk-Dubois (WBA, May 2023): K2 won at $8,057,000, Usyk $6,042,750 / Dubois $2,014,250. Wilder-Povetkin (WBC, Feb 2016): $7.15M, $715,000 held as the winner's bonus, the rest 70/30. Refusals: the champ forfeits (WBC, 2/3 vote) or is vacated and out 6+ months (IBF); the challenger loses his status or is out 6+ months. No bids: WBC extends ~15 days; IBF rebids, then may vacate (an eliminator with no bids drops both out of the top 10 for 6+ months); WBO can cut the minimum bid up to 50% or give the champ 120 days for a voluntary. A promoter who defaults loses his 10% deposit (WBC) or is barred 6+ months (IBF).

## How rankings move

**All four use committees; none publishes a points formula.** They weigh title activity, eliminators, regional belts, the quality of opposition and judgment.
- **WBC:** a committee of the continental federations, by consensus: "Ratings are and may be largely subjective." Monthly; a yearly three-day open floor. **Rates 40 a division** (the others 15).
- **WBA:** "the best opinion of the Association."
- **IBF:** written criteria (record, opposition, amateur record, activity, following the rules), committee judgment, no published table (one source calls it a point system: disagreement).
- **WBO:** ABC / Ali Act standards, "based on merit, not opinion" (their words).
- The IBF leaves #1 and #2 empty until an eliminator; the WBO and IBF rate a man in one division only; the IBF drops men signed to fight for other bodies' titles.

**One big win:** Agit Kabayel entered the WBC at #30 (May 2017); one win over Chisora (Nov 2017) put him straight into the top 15; beating unbeaten Makhmudov (Dec 2023) took him to #8; beating Zhilei Zhang (Feb 2025) made him interim champ. Zhang, WBO #13, beat interim champ Joe Joyce (TKO6, April 15, 2023) and was the mandatory in one fight. Ruiz #12 → #5 for signing. Regional belts count: Kabayel's EU title had him #30, the NABF #8. **Rule of thumb:** a win over a top-10 man is worth 5–15 spots or straight into the top 5; beating a champ or interim champ can make him the mandatory; wins over unranked men barely move him.

**A loss:** Wilder, WBC #1 for years → #8 after losing to Parker (Dec 2023) → #14 after Zhang KO'd him (June 2024), down to #15; The Ring dropped him from its top 10. Losing to an unranked man hurts most (WBO: he drops and the winner comes in; WBA: can be demoted or removed). A close title loss often keeps a man in the top 5 (Zhang after Parker, March 2024; no body publishes a number). Refusing a mandatory or eliminator: IBF below #10 for 6+ months.

**Inactivity:**

| Body | Rule |
|---|---|
| WBC | No fight in 9 months = can be lowered; must fight a rated man within 18 months to stay top 15 (Wilder slid #10 → #13, June-Oct 2025, just inactive) |
| WBA | Can demote for inactivity; no fixed time in the rules |
| IBF | A fight every 12 months, within 6 lb of his weight, or lowered / removed |
| WBO | A fight every 12 months and a top-rated man every 18 (injury exception); #1 and #2 defend their spot yearly |

Enforced unevenly: stars keep their spots through long layoffs (Wilder stayed WBC top 15 on one win in 30 months; Pacquiao #5 after four years out).

## Shortcuts

- **The name fighter:** the champ picks a money opponent as a voluntary (the WBC allows a "former world champion or elite challenger" whatever his rank); rankings bent to fit the fight (Ruiz, Pacquiao); WBC Champion Emeritus can ask for an immediate shot, and a "special bout" can jump a mandatory. Promoter and TV pressure drive it; the bodies take ~3% sanction fees (that motive is interpretation, not a rule).
- **Interim and regular belts:** an interim title when the champ can't defend in time (injury, inactivity, unifications); the interim man is next in line, and if the champ vacates, is stripped, retires or moves, he's usually **raised to full champ without a fight**. IBF interim: the mandatory vs the next available man, held 12 months max. WBA rule C.28: interim to champion at their discretion. WBC: interim titles to final-eliminator winners; Kabayel raised in June 2026. WBO: the interim man is the mandatory (Zhang, 2023). **WBA "Regular":** once there's a "Super" champion (several belts or 5+ defenses) a Regular title fills in; its holder is the Super's mandatory, ordered to a consolidation fight (Dubois won it June 2022 and became Usyk's mandatory). **WBC "Franchise"** (2019, for Canelo): lets an elite champ skip mandatories while another man holds the full belt.
- **Moving weight:** moving up is the classic shortcut: other divisions' champions count as qualified challengers (WBC 3.3.4, 3.3.7), the WBO makes a moving Super Champion its mandatory (Usyk, 2019). Canelo up two divisions to win the WBO 175 belt in his first fight there (Nov 2019); Khan up two for a middleweight shot (May 2016). Moving down: the same in principle, no verified case. A man changing divisions "is not assured of a ranking in his new category" (WBA), but elite movers are ranked at once on reputation.
