## 1. Shakur Stevenson ("take away everything, then take the rounds")

**Philosophy.** Hit and don't get hit, the Mayweather line he grew up on ([Boxing Showtimes profile](https://boxingshowtimes.com/boxers/shakur-the-fearless-stevenson)). He owns the cost of that: after the booed De Los Santos fight, "I talk so much sh!t, I gotta eat whatever come with it, man" ([BoxingScene](https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/stevenson-i-talk-much-sht-i-gotta-eat-whatever-criticism-come-with-bad-performance)). He picks one main weapon before the fight: "I knew my jab was going to kill him" (Lopez, Jan 2026) ([WBN / press conference summary](https://www.worldboxingnews.com/?p=147974)).
**Risk.** Very low by default. When the opponent is dangerous and patient (De Los Santos, 2023) he goes almost silent rather than open up. He takes risk only when he chooses to "prove a point" (Zepeda).
**Read phase.** Rounds 1 to 3 are for the lead hand: he extends it, hand-fights, and learns which punches the opponent can still throw. He will give away early rounds to a volume puncher (Zepeda won 2 and 3 on volume, per fighters_a).
**What he looks for.** Which hand the opponent leads with, how they react to a touch of the lead hand, and whether they reset their feet after missing.
**Decision rules.**
- If he jabs and you reach for my lead hand, I punch around it (lead hook) while your hand is busy.
- If he steps in with a jab or lead hook, I pull back and fire the straight left.
- If he is a big puncher and I'm ahead, I shut the fight down: jab and pull counters only.
- If he gets me on the ropes, I shell, roll, and counter each miss, then pivot out.
- If he is hurt to the body, I go back to the body (Padley 2025: three body knockdowns in round 9, per fighters_a).
**Adjustments.** Zepeda (July 2025): chose to stand in the pocket "to prove a point", took more than usual, then from round 6 tightened the pull counters and ran away with it. He said: "It wasn't the performance I was looking for because I came in here trying to prove a point, I was trying to fight. So, I took more punishment than usual" ([ESPN](https://global.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/45728180/shakur-stevenson-showed-boxing-fight-second-none)). Lopez (Jan 2026): went jab-first all night, Lopez landed 15% and Shakur won 119-109 on all cards ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teofimo_Lopez_vs._Shakur_Stevenson)). His trainer Wali Moses said Lopez had no Plan B ([Ring](https://www.ringmagazine.com/news/shakur-stevenson-trainer-explains-teofimo-lopez-s-biggest-mistake-25PKH8nDKAA4xBsreHs0Pa)).
**Adversity.** Never down as a pro. Zepeda knocked him off balance in round 3 and he insisted he was not hurt ([Ring](https://ringmagazine.com/en/news/shakur-stevenson-swears-he-wasnt-hurt-by-william-zepeda)). His response to trouble is to go MORE defensive, not to trade.
**Pace habit.** Low and flat. Accuracy rises after round 6, output barely does.
**Signature mental move: "the hand trap tax."** He makes you pay attention to his lead hand so long that you stop thinking about the left behind it.
**2026 status.** Active. Won the WBO and Ring 140 titles from Teofimo Lopez on Jan 31 2026 (4-division champion). No fight since. Dana White (Zuffa Boxing) said in July 2026 he returns before the end of 2026, opponent not named; Tank Davis was floated ([BoxingNews24](https://www.boxingnews24.com/2026/07/dana-white-confirms-shakur-stevenson-return-before-end-of-year/)).

## 2. Vasiliy Lomachenko ("plan three moves ahead, break the will")

**Philosophy.** Boxing as a thinking contest he trains for directly: "I train mentally, I train that for if sometimes I need to make very quick decisions in the ring, the mental training helps me a lot" ([ESPN trainers' take](https://www.espn.co.uk/boxing/story/_/id/26414794/trainers-take-how-beat-lomachenko)). Sparring partner Sam Maxwell: "He plans three or four moves ahead" (same source). On opponents who quit: "If you want to win, if you want to fight, you're willing to die in the ring" ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/21738354/vasiliy-lomachenko-no-mas-chenko-not-just-regular-fighter)).
**Risk.** Medium. Takes positional risk (stepping in to angles) but rarely trades in the pocket.
**Read phase.** Long. He needs to "feel the distance" before he commits. Against Lopez: "I couldn't feel the distance at the very beginning. I needed to keep him off-balance and start attacking but I couldn't feel it. But when I felt it in the second part of the fight I started coming out more active" ([Boxing Insider](https://www.boxinginsider.com/headlines/vasiliy-lomachenko-explains-his-game-plan-in-teofimo-lopez-upset-defeat/)).
**What he looks for.** The opponent's lead hand (to tap or trap it), their balance after punching, and how they turn when he takes the outside angle.
**Decision rules.**
- If he plants and punches, I step off the line to his side and fire a burst before he turns.
- If he reaches with the lead hand, I trap or tap it and come in behind it.
- If his head guard is good, the body ("You need to go to the body," his father in the Linares corner, [ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/23485907/vasiliy-lomachenko-lomachenko-stops-jorge-linares-tko-10th-round)).
- If he stops answering back, keep the bursts coming until he quits.
**Adjustments.** Linares (2018): dropped by a counter right in round 6, needed about a round to settle, then switched to the body and stopped Linares with a body-led flurry in round 10 ([CBS](https://www.cbssports.com/boxing/news/vasyl-lomachenko-tkos-jorge-linares-to-become-fastest-three-division-boxing-champion)). Lopez (2020): almost nothing thrown in rounds 1 to 6, big rally from round 7, too late; he also blamed a torn right shoulder: "I couldn't use the straight jab, or the right hook or the right uppercut" (Boxing Insider above). Haney (2023): same slow start and late surge, lost a close decision (unverified detail).
**Adversity.** Gets up and resets calmly (Linares). The weakness is the opposite: he can be too patient and leave it too late.
**Pace habit.** Burst fighter. Long quiet spells, then 3 to 6 punch explosions, then reset.
**Signature mental move: "No Mas-chenko."** He makes you miss and gives you nothing to hit until you stop wanting to fight ("Maybe I change my name. My name is now No Mas Chenko", ESPN above).
**2026 status.** Retired. Announced June 5 2025, aged 37, after a back injury; last fight a TKO 11 of Kambosos in May 2024; record 18-3 ([Dan Rafael](https://danrafael.substack.com/p/3-division-champion-lomachenko-announces)).

## 3. Naoya Inoue ("patient sniper who learned to hold back")

**Philosophy.** Alert, careful, and honest about fear: "I am afraid of the feeling that Tapales will take a shot at me so I will be very careful about that" ([Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2023/12/16/keep-your-hands-up-inoue-fears-tapales-sneaky-counters)). In 2025 he named his own flaw: "Opposing teams ... think my desire to entertain makes me attack aggressively at times, and that those moments are openings. So, I've started thinking that maybe I don't need to prioritize entertaining the fans in every fight" ([Ring](https://ringmagazine.com/en/news/naoya-inoue-held-himself-back-for-first-time-in-career-against-akhmadaliev)).
**Risk.** Medium-low while reading, high once he smells a finish. Since late 2025 he caps that second part.
**Read phase.** Jab-heavy first 2 to 3 rounds (65% jabs vs Akhmadaliev, per fighters_a). He studies the counter threat before he loads up.
**What he looks for.** The opponent's best counter (what can hurt him), and whether the body opens when they cover the head.
**Decision rules.**
- If I see a finish but he still has a live counter, I hold back and keep boxing (Akhmadaliev 2025: "even when I saw moments where I could have gone for the finish, I held myself back. That was the first time in my career", Ring above).
- If he drops me, I take the count, stay calm, and win the next exchange.
- If he catches me at one distance, I never stand at that distance again (Cardenas: "I got a little loose, I made sure I didn't get that close again", [BoxingScene](https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/inoue-cardenas-quotes)).
- If he covers up, I go to the body until the hands come down.
**Adjustments.** Nery (May 2024): knocked down in round 1 (first pro knockdown), dropped Nery in rounds 2 and 5, stopped him in 6 ([CBS](https://www.cbssports.com/boxing/news/naoya-inoue-vs-luis-nery-fight-results-highlights-the-monster-rallies-to-stop-mexican-foe-retain-titles)). Cardenas (May 2025): dropped in round 2 by a left hook, fixed the range, stopped him in 8. Nakatani (May 2026): won rounds 1 to 4, then had to hold off a late push (Nakatani out-landed him 48-34 in rounds 8 to 10, per fighters_b) and won 116-112, 116-112, 115-113 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/sports/2026/5/3/japans-inoue-beats-nakatani-to-retain-undisputed-super-bantamweight-title)).
**Adversity.** Elite. "From the moment I went down, I was able to recover calmly. I think it was precisely because I went down that I was able to fight the way I did." He credited "my usual image training" ([Namibian/AFP](https://namibian.com.na/?p=721408)).
**Pace habit.** Builds. Steady jab early, power share climbs every round once the read is done.
**Signature mental move: "the knockdown reset."** Going down makes him calmer and sharper, not wilder.
**2026 status.** Active and unbeaten. Beat Nakatani by UD at a sold-out Tokyo Dome on May 2 2026: "It was different to the fights I've had before, with the pressure. So I'm relieved that I won" ([Flashscore](https://www.flashscore.co.uk/news/inoue-admits-he-was-relieved-after-beating-nakatani-by-unanimous-decision/zkgYNvYp/)). No next fight confirmed in my searches.

## 4. Oleksandr Usyk ("calm maths teacher who finishes strong")

**Philosophy.** Calm as a tool: "I see no reason to be nervous as there is no constructive use behind it. It all comes down to doing what you can and what you've prepared for." And: "One of the principles of my preparation is to concentrate on myself, my preparation, spirit and belief" ([liga.net](https://life.liga.net/en/rozvagy/article/how-usyk-lives-and-why-he-doesnt-look-for-motivation-the-champions-rules-before-the-fight-with-dubois)). A sparring partner: "Usyk fights like a maths teacher. He has his own mathematics happening in his brain" ([DAZN](https://www.dazn.com/en-US/news/boxing/usyk-fights-like-maths-teacher-viktor-jurk-sparring-partner-rico-verhoeven/clmo984j1gtw1xgygbpohkeh9)).
**Risk.** Medium. Accepts losing early rounds and taking shots to set up a late surge.
**Read phase.** Feints, foot taps and lateral movement for 3 to 4 rounds. Lets the bigger man show his best weapon early.
**What he looks for.** Fatigue. He watches the opponent's output and feet, and attacks the moment they square up or slow down.
**Decision rules.**
- If he is bigger and fresher, I move, feint and bank energy.
- If his output drops or his feet square up, I step to the front foot and throw the straight left.
- If he hurts me, I hold my shape and keep moving; I don't trade back.
- If the late rounds are close, I raise output; my engine is better than his.
**Adjustments.** Joshua 2 (2022): Joshua hurt him with a body-and-head barrage in round 9, Usyk answered by out-working a tired Joshua in rounds 10 to 12 and won a split decision ([Bloody Elbow](https://bloodyelbow.com/2022/08/20/results-highlights-oleksandr-usyk-takes-split-decision-over-anthony-joshua-boxing-news)). Fury 1 (May 2024): lost several middle rounds, came alive in round 8, then wobbled Fury with a left in round 9 when Fury squared his feet ([Yahoo](https://uk.sports.yahoo.com/news/fury-v-usyk-world-title-031009922.html)). Verhoeven (May 2026): behind on cards (95-95, 95-95, 96-94 against him) and dropped the kickboxer with a right uppercut, stoppage at 2:59 of round 11 ([NST/AFP](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/sports/others/2026/05/1447102/usyk-rocked-beats-verhoeven-retain-heavyweight-titles)).
**Adversity.** Absorbs it and keeps the plan. "This fight was hard. It was a good fight" (Verhoeven, [Kuwait Times/AFP](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/44130/sports/other-sports/usyk-rocked-before-stopping-verhoeven-to-retain-belts/)).
**Pace habit.** Back-loaded. Rounds 8 to 12 are his best.
**Signature mental move: "count his breaths."** He tracks the other man's fatigue and strikes the round it shows.
**2026 status.** Active but near the end. Beat Verhoeven May 23 2026, vacated the WBC, WBA and IBF belts on June 26 2026: "I'm leaving the belts, but not the sport, because I still have my last dance." Deontay Wilder named as the wanted opponent, no date ([SI](https://www.si.com/fannation/boxing/oleksandr-usyk-vacates-titles-ahead-of-last-dance)).

## 5. Terence Crawford ("download, then hunt")

**Philosophy.** Read live, not on tape: "I don't watch too much film because I'm going to make my adjustments on the fly inside of the ring" ([AOL/LA Times](https://www.aol.com/news/terence-crawford-win-over-canelo-223052051.html)). On finishing: "we can sense a hurt prey when we're on the prowl" and "It's just a feeling you get in your stomach. You have to push a little harder, step to them a little more" ([Front Proof Media](https://www.frontproofmedia.com/boxing/a-look-at-the-fighting-life-of-terence-bud-crawford)).
**Risk.** Low early, very high once the opponent is hurt.
**Read phase.** Classic slow start. Gamboa (2014): "I was warming up, getting used to his style. In the first couple of rounds, I just wanted to test him out and see where he was at and adjust" ([Round by Round](https://roundbyroundboxing.com/news/crawford-vs-gamboa-results-and-analysis/)). Against Canelo he said beforehand he would try to win every round "from one to twelve", and after: "The first round went by, the second round went by ... and then I thought, 'I've got him'" (AOL above).
**What he looks for.** A habit he can punish (a dropped hand, a lunge), and whether the opponent adjusts after he punishes it.
**Decision rules.**
- If orthodox is not working, switch to southpaw (Gamboa round 5: "he's always dropping his left hand and I could get into rhythm by catching him with the jab from the southpaw stance", Round by Round above).
- If he lunges in with his lead, counter jab or counter hook.
- If he is durable, use a firm power jab to stop his feet (Spence: "The jab hit him and stopped him in his tracks", [BoxingScene](https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/crawford-spence-hit-hard-everyone-said-he)).
- If he is hurt, go now, and keep going.
**Adjustments.** Gamboa (2014): lost early rounds orthodox, switched to southpaw in round 5, stopped him in 9. Porter (2021): an even, rough fight until round 10, then a left uppercut and a right put Porter down twice and his father stopped it; "I could see in his face that he was really hurt ... I was coming with a vengeance" ([Ring TV](https://www.ringtv.com/630801-terence-crawford-scores-career-defining-tko-10-win-over-shawn-porter/)). Spence (2023): saw it early, dropped him three times, TKO 9. Canelo (2025): steady jab volume, then 39 landed in rounds 11 and 12 (fighters_b).
**Adversity.** Rarely in real trouble. Losing rounds early does not bother him; he treats them as the price of the read.
**Pace habit.** Slow start, middle takeover, strong finish.
**Signature mental move: "the mid-fight switch."** Changes stance or main weapon once he has the answer, and the fight flips.
**2026 status.** Retired at 42-0 (31 KOs), announced in December 2025 after the Canelo win: "walking away as a great with nothing else left to prove." He has denied comeback talk: "I'm happily retired"; trainer BoMac: "Terence Crawford is not coming back" ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/sports/a/bernadette-giacomazzo/terence-crawford-comeback-rumors-debunked)).

## 6. Dmitry Bivol ("the jab, then the perfect moment")

**Philosophy.** Simple and safe. "The jab, it's easy advice, but it works every time" and, on Canelo, "We just need to hit Canelo and don't get punched! Easy!" ([3 Kings Boxing](https://3kingsboxing.com/bivol-not-buying-canelo-hype/); [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/pro-boxing/news-boxing-news-dmitry-bivol-reacts-mike-tyson-s-advice-canelo)).
**Risk.** Low. He waits for a perfect moment, and after Beterbiev 1 he admitted that was a flaw.
**Read phase.** Short. He starts sharp with the jab and distance from round 1.
**What he looks for.** Where the opponent's feet are (in range or not), and whether they reset after he jabs.
**Decision rules.**
- If he walks forward, jab, step back, jab again.
- If he loads up, step out and counter with a short combination as he resets.
- If he is a puncher, don't stand still in range, but don't run either (rematch lesson).
- If I'm in a close fight late, act: don't wait for the perfect shot.
**Adjustments.** Canelo (2022): kept Canelo at the end of the jab and took over in the second half (fighters_b). Beterbiev 1 (Oct 2024): started well, then faded late and lost a majority decision. He said: "I had to act, not just waiting. I had to act, but I didn't" and "I was trying to find perfect moment ... the time passed very fast" ([BoxingScene forum thread of the interview](https://www.boxingscene.com/forums/boxing-forums/non-stop-boxing/32370498), wording unverified). Beterbiev 2 (Feb 2025): promised to be "more open", stood flat-footed, threw more, and won a majority decision ([Boxing Showtimes](https://boxingshowtimes.com/boxing-news/bivol-knows-he-needs-to-change-his-approach); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Beterbiev_vs._Dmitry_Bivol_II)).
**Adversity.** Calm, but the risk is passivity: when hurt or tired he waits instead of acting.
**Pace habit.** Even, steady output. After Beterbiev 1 he tries to keep the late rounds busy.
**Signature mental move: "learn and come back changed."** He fixed his own late-round passivity between fight 1 and fight 2.
**2026 status.** Active. Back surgery (herniated disc) in Aug 2025. Returned May 30 2026 and beat Michael Eifert 120-107 on all three cards, with a counter left hook knockdown in round 1 ([Ring](https://www.ringmagazine.com/news/bivol-dominates-eifert-to-defend-light-heavyweight-crown-3WwFP8F85MKOjpQc1lXCGB)). Wants big fights; a Beterbiev trilogy is the obvious one ([CBS](https://www.cbssports.com/boxing/news/dmitry-bivol-undergoes-back-surgery-sidelining-unified-light-heavyweight-champion-until-2026)).

## 7. Canelo Alvarez ("patient trap, then body")

**Philosophy.** Patience and doing only what is needed. After Kovalev: "We had to have patience. We knew we had to have patience" ([BoxingScene](https://www.boxingscene.com/patience-pays-off-with-canelos-ko-kovalev--143993)). On why he stopped moving so much: "If I know I don't need something, why am I going to do it? If I need it, I'm going to do it" (same summary, [Ring TV](https://www.ringtv.com/?p=529892), wording unverified).
**Risk.** Medium. Takes a few shots to land his own, trusts his chin and guard.
**Read phase.** Waits and counters. "Punched and waited, punched and waited" vs Liam Smith ([Ring TV](https://www.ringtv.com/465563-canelo-alvarez-provides-payoff-liam-smith/)). Admits the first rounds can be hard.
**What he looks for.** An opponent who stands in front of him and lets him set a trap; slow feet he can walk down.
**Decision rules.**
- If he stands in front of me, I slip and counter, then dig to the body.
- If he is slower than me, I walk him down; I don't need to move.
- If he jabs from range and moves, I wait for him to stop (this is where he fails against fast movers).
- If he is hurt to the body, keep investing downstairs and wait for the head to open (Kovalev KO 11).
**Adjustments.** Golovkin 2 (2018): flipped the first fight and came forward as the aggressor ([ESPN guide](https://africa.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/24675118/boxing-ringside-seat-complete-guide-gennady-golovkin-canelo-alvarez-2)). Mayweather (2013), Bivol (2022) and Crawford (2025): could NOT find a fix against a mover with a jab. On Crawford: "I just couldn't figure out his style" and later "Mentally, I was not there" ([Boxing News](https://www.boxingnewsonline.net/news/canelo-sums-up-crawfords-ability); [Ring](https://www.ringmagazine.com/news/canelo-reflects-on-crawford-loss-i-wasn-t-there-mentally-6bD2hatRMdBJNDXuU1a8lr)).
**Adversity.** Tough and calm when hurt, but slow to change his plan when it isn't working.
**Pace habit.** Low output (28 thrown per round vs Crawford, fighters_b), bursts in short exchanges, often fades in volume late.
**Signature mental move: "the waiting trap."** He makes you think it's safe to stand in front of him, then counters and hits the body.
**2026 status.** Active. Elbow surgery after the Crawford loss. His September 2026 date was postponed; he faces WBC 168 champion Christian Mbilli on Oct 31 2026 in Riyadh ([Ring](https://www.ringmagazine.com/news/canelo-alvarez-vs-christian-mbilli-now-set-for-oct-31-pp5U3W2aggZTpnh1zlXif)).

## 8. Floyd Mayweather (historical benchmark: "the instant adjuster")

**Philosophy.** "Boxing is like chess. Every move has to be thought about before you make it. I like to study my opponents first" ([Inquirer](https://sports.inquirer.net/?p=179787)). "I can make adjustments instantly and that is one of the pluses on my side" ([Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/story/408253/undefeated-mayweather-tweaks-game-plan-to-beat-maidana)). The plan "to hit and not get hit" ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/9243021/floyd-mayweather-jr-proves-now)).
**Risk.** The lowest of the group. Wins rounds by margin, not damage.
**Read phase.** 2 to 4 rounds of jabs, feints and pull-backs. He will lose one or two of them to learn.
**What he looks for.** The opponent's rhythm and their favourite lead punch, so he can time the pull counter.
**Decision rules.**
- If he jabs, pull back and land the right before his jab returns ("Before his jab gets back you already hit him with a right hand", [Fox Sports](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/boxing/mayweather-beats-mosley)).
- If he rushes, turn him at the ropes and tie up.
- If he is wild, take my time and adjust (Maidana: "He came out kind of wild, and I took my time making adjustments", Gulf Times above).
- If I'm well ahead, take no chances.
**Adjustments.** Mosley (2010): badly hurt by a right hand in round 2, grabbed, then "went back to my corner and told myself to relax" and won almost every round after with the pull counter (Fox Sports above). Maidana 1 (2014): roughed up early, moved more and used the ring in the second half.
**Adversity.** Composure is his best weapon. Hurt in round 2 against Mosley, cruised the rest.
**Pace habit.** Low output, very high accuracy; rounds become more one-sided as the fight goes on.
**Signature mental move: "the round-two reset."** Whatever happened, the next round he has the answer.
**2026 status.** Retired from official fights since 2017 (50-0), but announced a comeback in 2026 at 49. A Mike Tyson exhibition in Congo (April 25) appears not to have happened (unverified). The Pacquiao rematch moved from the Sphere (Sept 19) to T-Mobile Arena (Sept 26) and was then postponed again, possibly to January ([WBN](https://www.worldboxingnews.com/mayweather-pacquiao-2-september-off/); [Yahoo](https://sports.yahoo.com/article/floyd-mayweather-vs-manny-pacquiao-180348572.html)).

## Cross-mind patterns (for game design)

- **Everyone reads first.** All eight give up some early output to learn. The difference is how long: Bivol and Mayweather read in 1 to 2 rounds, Crawford and Lomachenko take 3 to 6.
- **The best adjust between rounds, not mid-exchange.** Crawford (Gamboa round 5), Mayweather (Mosley round 3), Inoue (Cardenas round 3) all made the fix after a corner. This fits `nextRound()` as the natural place for a plan swap.
- **Knockdowns split them.** Inoue and Mayweather get calmer; Lomachenko needs a round; Canelo and Bivol tend to get more careful.
- **The one known failure mode** is waiting too long: Lomachenko vs Lopez, Bivol vs Beterbiev 1, Canelo vs Crawford. A mind with low riskTolerance AND low adjustAfterRound should lose close fights it could have won.
- **Corner talk hook:** "act, don't wait" (Bivol), "go to the body" (Lomachenko's father), "relax" (Mayweather) are real corner-style lines that could map to shouts.

## Engine translation

Knob meanings (all 0 to 1):
- **readSpeed**: how fast the AI learns the opponent's habits (1 = full read in round 1).
- **riskTolerance**: willingness to trade, load up, or stand in range.
- **adjustAfterRound**: chance and size of a plan change between rounds when losing the exchange.
- **composure**: how little a knockdown or a hurt moment changes the next minute (1 = no panic).
- **finisherInstinct**: how hard it jumps on a hurt opponent.
- **counterBias**: share of attacks that are counters instead of leads.
- **pressureWhenAhead**: whether it keeps pushing (1) or coasts (0) when up on the cards.
