# What happens between fights: rest, rust, wear, habits

Scope: the gaps left by `mind_learn.md` (which has loss case files, rematch odds, camp, XP rules, after-KO caution, opponent memory, style familiarity). Not repeated here.

Honesty note: web search found almost NO boxing-specific data for rust, chin wear or age curves. The hard numbers come from UFC studies (closest proxy) and state rules. Everything else is case evidence, [mem], or (est.). Treat the "For the game" numbers as tuning knobs, not facts.

## 1. The post-fight routine

### 1a. Film review and debrief
- No source found on a standard debrief. Searches for trainer-boxer film review returned nothing usable. [snippet: search came up empty]
- What is known: Roach took one day off after Mayweather-Pacquiao; trainers live in the fight cycle too. [snippet, ringtv.com/390893]
- Typical practice (all [mem]/est.):
  - Night of the fight: corner talk is short and emotional. Win or lose, little is learned.
  - Day 1 to 3: hospital/doctor checks, hands and cuts looked at.
  - Week 1 to 2: trainer watches the tape alone first, then with the fighter. Looks at: what hurt him, which punch landed most on him, what the opponent did in rounds he lost, stamina fade round, and whether the game plan was followed.
  - The fix list is short: 1 to 3 items (matches `mind_learn.md` pattern 1).
- Fighters differ on watching tape. Some refuse to watch a loss for weeks [mem]. Some (Marquez) rebuild a whole plan around one tape. Quality of the teacher matters (see `mind_learn.md` 2a pattern 3).

### 1b. Time off and medical suspension
| Rule | Value | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Louisiana: KO loss | min 60 days suspension, no sparring in that time | [snippet, Louisiana Admin Code XI 126] |
| Louisiana: TKO from head blows | 30 days | [snippet, same] |
| Georgia (older rule): 1st KO | 60 days; 2nd KO 60 days + CAT scan/EKG may be required; 2+ consecutive KO losses 6 months | [snippet, Ga. R. 85-1-.04] |
| New York: KO with head trauma | min 30 days, until neurological clearance | [snippet, 19 NYCRR 208.11] |
| New York: 3 straight KO/TKO losses | automatic suspension until medical exam | [snippet, 19 NYCRR 208.10] |
| Georgia (2024 rule): 6 straight KO/TKO losses | 365 days | [snippet, Ga. R. 85-5-.04] |
| Florida | ringside doctor's call, exam after any KO | [snippet, 61K1-3.020] |
| Interstate | ABC registry suspensions are honored in other states | [snippet, Georgia rule] |

- Common rule of thumb: 30 days for a stoppage, 60 for a clean KO, more after a bad one or a run of losses. (est. from the above)
- Voluntary rest: most fighters take 2 to 4 weeks nearly off, then 1 to 2 weeks light work before a new camp [mem]. Camp is 6 to 12 weeks (`mind_learn.md` 2d). So the shortest sensible gap is ~3 months; faster turnarounds are rare at the top. (est.)

### 1c. How long between fights
| Stage | Typical gap | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Champion / top contender | 1 to 2 fights a year; heavyweight champs ~6 months "when nothing disrupts scheduling" | [snippet, Bleacher Report; Straight Dope thread] |
| Gary Russell Jr. (champ, extreme) | 23-month layoff before a defense | [snippet, ESPN 2021] |
| Low-profile / journeyman | ~1 fight every 2 months | [snippet, Sportskeeda] |
| Prospect, first 2 years | 4 to 8 fights a year, 4-rounders then 6s | [mem] |
| Old era | Sugar Ray Robinson 198 bouts in 25 years; Frazier 37 in 11 years | [snippet, Fox News] |
| UFC comparison | 1.69 fights per active year, median 2 | [snippet, AgentMMA 8,714 bouts] |

- Long gaps are mostly business: finding an opponent and a deal, not recovery. [snippet, Straight Dope]
- Pro careers are shorter now: average career fell from ~19 years (1930s) to ~5 years in a UK/Australia review. [snippet, search summary of a medical review; weakly sourced]

## 2. What wins teach

### 2a. Confidence and "learning on the job"
- Wins build confidence, but the evidence is mostly case-based. No boxing study found. (see 3)
- Real "first time" lessons are common:
  - First 8, 10, 12-rounder: pacing and gas. Pros step 4 to 6 to 8 to 10 to 12 [mem]. Loma's "pro game" took 5 to 6 fights. [snippet, from `mind_learn.md`]
  - First southpaw or odd style: both stances win less against southpaws (340 pros, 2014). [snippet, from `mind_learn.md`]
  - Mayweather vs Castillo 1: a fight he won (on cards) but had to change his plan. A hard win teaches too. [snippet, from `mind_learn.md`]
- A win teaches what WORKED: the user's go-to tools get reinforced (more use, more success). That also entrenches habits. This is why one-plan fighters (Wilder) stay one-plan: winning with one punch kept paying until it didn't. [mem]
- A win over a stronger man teaches more than a win over a weak one. Matchmakers use that on purpose (2b).

### 2b. Matchmaking: why the record is built
- Tune-up: a heavy favorite fight, used to shake off rust or stay busy while waiting for a bigger fight. Dubois and Joyce each took tune-ups before their 2020 plan. [snippet, sportsmole]
- Step-up: each opponent a bit more accomplished or covering one test. Dalton Smith's climb is a good example. [snippet, Boxing News "Art of Matchmaking" 2024]
- Early title talk: Tyson's team planned a title fight early in the 1990s; the 1995 AP piece also describes a "tuneup for championship bout" (Mathis). [snippet, Arizona Wildcat AP 1995]
- Critics: tune-ups on national TV are mismatches; Gatti's Correa non-title fight is cited as a fairer model. [snippet, ib.tv]
- Why promoters do it: protect the 0, build the fan base, give rounds, and let the kid learn before the real test. [mem]
- No source gives a fixed number of fights before a title shot. Prospect pacing in `For the game` is (est.).

## 3. Mental after-effects

### 3a. After a big win: confidence and complacency
| Case | What happened | Lesson | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buster Douglas | Beat Tyson (1990). Gained weight, barely trained for Holyfield; lost R3 | Peak win then no motivation or camp | [snippet, search summary; Ruiz articles] |
| Andy Ruiz Jr. | Beat AJ (June 2019). Bought 5 cars. Rematch: 283 lb, 15 lb heavier than fight 1 (AJ 237). Admitted "I was overweight... should have trained harder". Lost UD | Weight and camp slip after a windfall | [snippet, ESPN, ringtv, SI, Guardian] |
| Lennox Lewis | Filmed Ocean's Eleven before the Rahman fight; had about half Rahman's time in South Africa; KO'd R5 (2001) | Distraction + overconfident favorite | [snippet, ESPN Maxboxing, Globe and Mail] |
| Lewis rematch | Far more focused; KO4 | Motivation is restored by a loss | [snippet, Boxing Scene] |
| Tyson | Lost to Douglas after a long run of easy wins, training slipped [mem] | Streak complacency | [mem] |
| Ruiz quote | Denied complacency, "slicker and faster" | Fighters rarely admit it before the fight | [snippet, Guardian] |

- Pattern: the sharpest drops come right after the biggest upset win or the biggest payday. The slip is in CAMP (weight, hours), not in skill. The skill stays; the shape and the focus fall. (est. from cases)
- Sample is tiny and biased: we remember the ones who failed. Many rich champions keep training. [mem]
- Motivation after getting rich: Wilder, 2023: "I don't know (if I still have the same fire), a lot has calmed me down." Lewis blamed his rust. [snippet, SI, DAZN, worldboxingnews]

### 3b. After a KO loss: gun-shy or "never the same"
- No documented clear gun-shy case turned up in search. Wilder-Parker was blamed on inactivity and slow trigger, not fear. [snippet, SI, worldboxingnews]
- Lewis lost to Rahman (KO5) and came back to KO him in 4. A KO loss did not break him. [snippet, ESPN]
- Fury after Wilder 1: some said the knockdowns could haunt him; he fought on at a higher level. [snippet, worldboxingnews; commentators' speculation]
- Pacquiao after Marquez 4 (2012): "more careful" than before. [snippet, from `mind_learn.md`]
- Other often-cited cases, all [mem], unverified here: Tyson after Buster and Lewis (later fights showed less offense), Keep this list for flavor, not tuning: Tyson, Hatton after Pacquiao (long layoff and a tough comeback).
- Take: there are two outcomes. Most good fighters ADAPT (caution, then fade). A few lose the edge for good, and it usually looks like a slow trigger and a worse chin together. Both are in `For the game`.

## 4. Ring rust

### 4a. The only data found (UFC, not boxing)
- AgentMMA: 5,975 decided UFC fights. The fighter with MORE days off won 48.1% overall (about even). The effect shows only after long layoffs. [snippet, agentmma.com/mma-lab/ufc-ring-rust-win-rate-study]
- A subset of 627 fights: a man out 365+ days won 39.4% when his opponent had fought in the last 180 days. [snippet, same]
- Caveat from the authors: the clock counts inactivity in the UFC, not lost skill; injury and age mix in. [snippet, same]
- So: under ~1 year, no clear rust; over 1 year against an active man, about -10 points of win rate (50 down to ~39). (est. from one MMA study)

### 4b. Boxing cases (anecdotal)
| Case | Layoff | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Wilder vs Parker (Dec 2023) | long gap (exact length not checked) [mem] | Lost UD; Lewis said rust was a massive factor [snippet, worldboxingnews] |
| A returning boxer who "admitted rust" after a year+ off | 12+ months | Narrow win [snippet, Cafu/Aranas, Sunday Times 2026] |
| Leamington ABC comeback | 3 years | Won, but amateur-level story [snippet] |
| Hatton vs Pacquiao 2009, Tyson vs Williams 2004 | 1.5 to 3 years | Both lost; sources not checked [mem] |
| Mayweather 2 years off (2007 to 09 Marquez) | ~21 months | Won all 12 rounds but looked "off" early [mem] |

- Typical look of rust (commentary, [mem]): slow start, timing off, jab short, tired in the middle rounds because sparring cannot copy fight stress. Skill itself returns in 1 to 3 rounds.
- A gap is not rust if the man stayed in camp. Rust needs no live rounds, not just no fights. (est.)

## 5. Wear and tear across a career

### 5a. Chin and knockouts
- MMA evidence (705 UFC fighters with a KO/TKO loss): 22.7% lost their NEXT fight by KO/TKO; prior KO history was the only independent predictor after age, weight, etc. Cause is tangled: defense, power of opponents, and brain damage all play. [snippet, AgentMMA "knockout loss repeat risk"]
- A 2023 paper on UFC data 2010 to 2021 asked "can a fighter develop a glass jaw": results suggested SOME fighters are more susceptible, i.e. individual trait, not a rule. [snippet, Revista de Artes Marciales Asiáticas, 2023]
- Common view: chin is mostly genetic but is affected by total blows taken. A consensus, not a measurement. [snippet, Wikipedia "Chin (combat sports)"]
- Weight cuts may also weaken the chin; an AgentMMA piece asks the question (not confirmed here). [snippet, agentmma weight-cut]
- Boxing cases [mem]: Tyson, Wilder (stopped late), Ricky Hatton after Mayweather and Pacquiao, Hearns, Lennox Lewis (held up), Wladimir (two KOs then no more in 11 years, so chin was not "gone"). Both outcomes exist.
- Wars: no data. Common belief is that a brutal 12-round war ages a boxer more than 3 years of sparring. Examples: Gatti-Ward trilogy, Marquez-Pacquiao 4, Barrera-Morales. [mem]

### 5b. Age
- General science: human peak physical function ~30, declines modestly after; speed and stamina sports favor the young, fine-motor skill sports hold on longer. [snippet, bioRxiv cricket aging paper]
- Cognitive speed: a 2014 Canadian study of 3,305 people (16 to 44) found a ~15% decline every 15 years after 24. Secondhand; not verified. [snippet, via Boxing News]
- Boxing News (Danny Wilson): physiology in the 20s vs 30s may not differ much; injuries and motivation explain more of the drop-off. [snippet, boxingnewsonline "science behind the aging boxer"]
- Hopkins: reflexes dropped near 40 but ring IQ held, kept him at title level to 49. [snippet, boxing writers; also `mind_learn.md` 3a]
- Pacquiao: a decline debate before Thurman at 40, loss of legs and burst. [snippet, boxingscene Rosenthal]
- Order of decline (est., supported by the above): reflex speed and legs/burst first (early-to-mid 30s), then stamina and recovery, then chin / punch resistance (uneven), with IQ and defensive craft last.

### 5c. Punch-resistance myths vs evidence
- Myth: a good chin can be trained like a muscle. Neck strength and tight guard help a bit [mem]; real chin is mostly inherent.
- Myth: a boxer "goes" after one KO. Evidence shows a raised risk (22.7% next-fight KO loss in the UFC sample) but 77.3% did not. [snippet, AgentMMA]
- Fact: repeated head hits raise long-term risk of brain injury, and commissions respond with suspensions (1b). [snippet, state rules]

## 6. Habits that come back, and drilling a fix

- A habit that cost a fight returns when the fighter is tired or hurt. Pacquiao's dropped guard returned "in kamikaze mode" after he said he was careful. [snippet, `mind_learn.md` 2e]
- Wilder had no plan B: after losing, he kept the same plan in the next two fights. [snippet, Sowetan, Defector, SI]
- Canelo's changes (waist, lean-back) stuck because they were drilled and rewarded for years. [snippet, `mind_learn.md` 2a]
- Drill counts: no boxing-specific data found. General motor-learning rules (all [mem], est.): a new motor pattern needs thousands of reps; stable under stress only after being used in sparring; old habit survives for months. Marquez needed 4 months for one counter. [snippet, Philstar]
- Coaching practice: break the fix to one cue (e.g. "hand back to the chin after every jab"), pad work first, slow shadow, then sparring with coach shouting the cue, then fatigue rounds. [mem]
- Pressure test: the fix is "real" only when it holds in the last 3 rounds or when hurt. (est.)

## For the game

All numbers (est.) unless noted. Stored on the fighter sheet between fights.

### Rest and calendar
- After a stoppage loss: forced rest 30 days; after a KO: 60 days; 2 KO losses in a row: 90 days; 3 in a row: 180 days. [src: state rules, 1b]
- Camp 8 to 10 weeks (existing). Minimum gap ~13 weeks. Champion default gap 6 to 9 months; prospect 2 to 3 months; journeyman 2 months. [snippet 1c]

### Debrief (one per fight, before the next camp)
- Take the top 1 to 3 "lessons" from the log: what hurt me most (punch, side), rounds lost, gas-out round, plan missed.
- Each lesson: +50% XP on the matching skill (loss) or +20% (win), applied in camp. Matches 4b of `mind_learn.md`. A good trainer doubles the lesson count (1 to 2 for a poor trainer).

### Confidence (`conf`, 0 to 1, start .5)
| Event | Change | Lasts |
|---|---|---|
| Win vs weaker | +.03 | fades half in 2 fights |
| Win vs equal/stronger | +.08 | 3 fights |
| Big upset win (title) | +.15 | 3 fights, plus complacency roll |
| Loss on points | -.05 | 2 fights |
| Loss by KO | -.15 | 4 fights (see caution) |
| Knocked down but won | +.05 | 1 fight |
- Effect: conf shifts risk-taking and composure by ±10% at the extremes. Above .85: -10% camp quality (complacency). Below .25: slower trigger.

### Complacency roll (after a title win / big upset / big purse)
- Chance of a lazy camp: 25% base, +15% for low `heart`/discipline, +10% per extra belt won in a row, -20% with a strong trainer.
- Lazy camp: weight +4 to 8% bodyweight (Ruiz +15 lb on ~268 = ~6%), conditioning -15%, sparring -1. Lasts one fight. Tuned so a lazy-camp champ loses more often (Ruiz, Douglas).
- A loss ends it: next camp is sharp (Lewis rematch).

### Caution after KO (adds to `mind_learn.md` 4b)
- `caution += .15` after KO loss, fades 50% in 3 fights. Low composure: `gunShy` (trigger delay +10%) for 2 to 4 fights, 15% chance it becomes permanent.
- Real cases are few; most recover (Lewis, Wlad). So default is caution, not ruin.

### Ring rust (days since last fight, with live rounds in camp counted)
| Layoff | Early-round effect | Win rate effect |
|---|---|---|
| < 8 months | none | 0 |
| 8 to 12 months | rounds 1 to 2 timing -5% | -2 pts |
| 12 to 24 months | rounds 1 to 3 timing/accuracy -10%, gas -5% | -8 pts (UFC: 50 down to 39) |
| > 24 months | rounds 1 to 4 -15%, reads slow | -12 pts |
- Scaled by age: x1.3 if over 35. Reduced 50% if the fighter stayed in full camp or had a tune-up.
- Rust fades within 3 rounds. A tune-up fight removes all of it (the real reason for tune-ups).

### Chin wear and war damage
- `chin` is a fixed rolled trait plus `wear`. KO loss: `wear += 3` (of a 0 to 100 chin scale); each stoppage or "hurt badly" (knocked down twice) +1; each 12-round war (damage taken top 10%) +2; a normal win +0.3.
- Wear drops chin by `wear * .5`. After 3 KOs, about -5 to -9 points. Not a cliff.
- Next-fight KO risk after a KO loss: +30% relative (22.7% vs a ~14% base in MMA sample is about this). [src: AgentMMA]
- Age speeds wear x1.5 after 35. Rest 12 months with no hard sparring heals 1 point (est.), never to zero.
- Big individual spread: rolled `chinResilience` 0.5 to 1.5 multiplies wear (some fighters never decline, e.g. Wlad).

### Age curves (per attribute group)
| Group | Peak | Starts to fall | Rate after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reflex, hand speed, legs/burst | 22 to 28 | 31 to 33 | -2 to -3% per yr |
| Stamina, recovery | 24 to 30 | 33 | -1.5 to -2% per yr |
| Power | 25 to 32 | 35 | -1% per yr |
| Chin (age part) | 25 to 30 | 34 | via wear x1.5 |
| IQ, craft, ring generalship | 28 to 36 | 38 to 40 | -0.5% per yr (Hopkins) |
- Experienced fighters partially cover for lost speed by reading earlier: +1 anticipation per 3 years of lost speed, capped. [src: Hopkins]

### What a win vs a loss teaches
- Win: +use XP on what worked (+20% matching lessons), entrench habits (habit strength +1). Beat a style: `know[style] += .15 to .25`. First 8/10/12 round fight: +pacing XP bonus x1.5 for that first time. Beat a stronger man: x1.5 XP.
- Loss: +50 to 100% XP on the exact defense that failed; opponent memory seeded; caution/gunShy as above; confidence drop. Same XP caps still apply.
- One-plan fighters (high `habit`, low IQ): wins entrench; after a loss, `adaptChance` = IQ-based, 0.2 for Wilder-types vs 0.8 for Marquez-types.

### Habits under pressure
- A fixed habit (flagged in debrief) has `fixProgress` 0 to 100. Camp adds ~12 to 18 per week with drilling (6 to 8 weeks to hold under calm). Spar-tested at 70+.
- When hurt or in the last 3 rounds: chance old habit returns = `(1 - fixProgress/100) * 0.7 + stress*.2`. Below 70 it returns most of the time. (est.)
- A trainer who isn't present or a fast turnaround cuts the progress by half.

### Prospects
- Early record: 4-rounders, then 6s (fights 4 to 8), 8s (9 to 15), 10 to 12 (15+). Tune-up and step-up flags: about 1 in 3 fights is a tune-up for a top fighter; a step-up every 3rd fight for a prospect. (est.)
- Win count gives "record bonus" to promotion, not skill. Don't let a padded record affect combat numbers.
