# Batch 1 (Oct 22)

## 1. Andy Ruiz interview (before the Matchroom comeback fight)
Thin for us: a life story, not a fight plan. The few decisions in it:
- **Why he fell off:** after the Joshua win he left his trainer, did the conditioning himself, "thought I knew it all", and six months of nothing went by. The fix is the old trainer back and a boss who says "come to me, I'm not coming to you". Colour for a trainer's job: a fighter who thinks he knows it all stops taking in information (Kenny Porter said the same in `toprank.md` #2).
- **Underestimating a lesser name** is how the hurt happens: he skipped the real camp for the Miller fight, thought "move around, counter, hand speed will be too much", hit Miller on the back of the head, **broke his hand in round 5 and boxed 12 anyway**. In the 9th he told his father "I can't do this" and the answer was "three more rounds", so he kept going.
- **Weight as a speed choice:** 270 down to 255-260 "for the hands and the conditioning".
- **Plan for the next man (a bigger, jab-first heavyweight):** back him up, jabs to the body and face, rights, "don't let him back me up or have that jab in my face". Same shape as the Fury lessons: the short man needs the inside.
- **Sparring 10-12 rounds** "to get it in your head that you can go all those rounds": a belief thing, not a fitness number.
- Have: the cut hand is not in the engine (no injuries mid-fight besides cuts and swelling); gas by round is. New: a hand injury as a swing event, rare, he fights on with half a right.

## 2. Canelo vs Billy Joe Saunders (full fight, Arlington): the boxer wins the middle, then the body wins the eighth
Pre-fight reads (booth): Saunders is a wide-stance southpaw whose jab is over half of what he lands; the ask was "show something besides the jab". Canelo is **more cautious vs a southpaw** (the counter left cross is waiting) and Eddy Reynoso predicted a stoppage in 8-10 (unusual for him). Saunders' own ask: the biggest ring he'd ever had (22 ft), plenty of room to circle. Austin Trout's advice: "in and out, time him as he comes in, but you have to do it for 12".
- **R1-R2:** "pedestrian". Saunders keeps him on the end of the jab, stays off the ropes; Canelo feels him out like he does with everyone, then lands heavier shots from R2.
- **What Canelo does to a man's mind:** he makes fighters **reluctant to throw their biggest punch** (Kovalev barely threw the right) because they know what comes back. Saunders is told to stop sitting in range soaking counters: "throw three or four, then get out", and to be busy for the judges' eyes.
- **Canelo's weapons early:** the **right uppercut** thrown with power again and again and **missing** (4 times by R4), body shots straight down the middle, the right hand to the ribs. The booth: blocked body shots still "dig in", drain energy; Roy Jones: the body shots put fear in him, he doesn't want that same power upstairs.
- **R3-R7, Saunders:** jab, then combos, left uppercut, short hook counter on the inside, a catch-and-counter off a body shot. Hands **down** when he's relaxed ("fights best like that; hands up he's reluctant"), pivots and angles to the right away from the right hand ("front foot battle"), **a good veteran move: bumped Canelo off on the ropes**. Canelo was "loading up, landing little, output down". Cards after 7 were 68-65 Saunders (Mannix) and 68-66 Canelo (Carl): a real fight.
- **R8, the turn:** Canelo cuts off and lands heavier body shots. Saunders starts **biting on foot and hand feints** ("he's worried about the power, so every little fake gets a reaction"), his right eye swells, and when Canelo's right hand lands he **smothers and hugs instead of countering back**. Between rounds the eye is the story ("he's not sure what's happening with his eyes until he gets back to the corner"). The corner stops it at the end of the round; the ref calls it on the corner's advice.
- **Why the stool stop matters:** he was not behind on the cards, nobody knocked him down. It was the eye and the body of work: "when the going got tough he didn't get up from the stool".
- Have: stool RTD exists (worn + behind the cards, coin flip) and the eye stoppage exists (`TKO (eye)`). New: an RTD for a man who is **level or ahead** but compromised by one eye and fed a heavy body, which is not what the current check says (it needs behind on the cards). The feint reaction: a hurt man biting on fakes is the "gun shy" idea (`toprank.md` batch 1 #3) with a visible tell.

## 3. Ryan Garcia vs Devin Haney (full fight, 3 KDs): how the hurt man lived, and how the winner didn't finish him
- **R1:** Garcia's left hook drops him into trouble inside 30 seconds, then again; Haney **holds, backs off, is gun-shy**. Garcia "stays poised", does not rush, **an adrenaline dump** after the big moment, and Haney is back to his jab. The booth: "he's never been in this kind of trouble this early", and Haney's father in the corner after the round: **throw the jab, then get under that hook**; dip, make him miss and fall off balance, then hit him from the side. Also: throw away a right hand to bait the hook, and use foot fakes.
- **The flaw that ran the fight:** Haney dipped to the **same side** every time and ate the chopping right hand ("they better tell him to dip the other way"). Garcia drops his own right hand when he dips back, so the left hand is always open. Haney kept finding that left hand and a body shot.
- **R3-R6, the champion adjusts on the fly:** jab, then pressure, then he becomes the aggressor and Garcia is on the back foot ("not as good on the back foot as the front"). Jab count through 8: **Haney 35, Garcia 6**; power punches through 8: **Garcia 56, Haney 23**. Garcia: spurts, one punch is enough, no volume, a lot of movement that "zaps energy". Hearn's pre-fight line: conditioning would show late.
- **Garcia reaches and gets caught** (that's how Davis dropped him): the booth tells him to get the jab back and walk him down with it.
- **Garcia's defense:** shoulder roll and head dipped almost to the canvas, even turning his back. The ref had to say "don't hit him there". The advice: **take what he gives you** (his left side, the body); a legal shot is a legal shot.
- **R7, KD 1, the chaos round:** a left hook, Haney **holds for dear life**; Garcia **punches on the break and loses a point with no warning**, which hands Haney "precious seconds". Two more knockdown-looking moments were ruled **pushdowns / not knockdowns** (in the corner against the ropes). The booth: "if Haney's in trouble he's got to take a knee", and "a minute is a lifetime". The final 24 seconds: Garcia frustrated by the clinching and by the ref **not letting him fight with one hand free**, not punched out.
- **Pace for the finish:** at the halfway point the veteran voice says "not yet, that moment passed, now catch him slipping again". Setups were a thrown-away right to bait the hook.
- **R10, KD 2:** a rocked Haney again, then lots of clinching: "they're going to have to take a point for holding". **R11, KD 3** (10-8 for the 2nd round in a row): "Haney's skill is in control, but his chin and legs are betraying him". Garcia mocks it (shakes his head), asks his dad to stop it for the show.
- **R12:** the champion who needed a stoppage; a right-hand uppercut sneaks in with 10 seconds left. Cards: **112-112, 114-110, 115-109**, a majority decision to Garcia. Three knockdowns and a deduction and it was still a split-ish fight.
- **Lessons:** (1) the survivor lived on holding, turning, and the next-round recovery, not on one big adjustment; (2) a fresh jab-and-move man gets back into the fight inside two rounds; (3) the puncher who hurt him is the one who gets lazy: spurts, no jab, no pressure; (4) a point deduction for a break shot cost real seconds of recovery.
- Have: stoppage rate, 10-8 rounds, deductions and fouls (`foul()`, `dirtyShot`) exist; the hurt brain has `hurtResp` (shell, hold, run, fire back) and the finisher has `check`/`hunt`/`huntSafe`. New: **a hurt man who holds can be saved by the break rule**: punching on the break costs the hitter a point and gives the hurt man time. Also the **"dip to the same side"** habit as a flaw (the hook finds it), and the pace of a hunter who rushes then stalls.

## 4. Joshua vs Prenga: down twice in R1, back to knock him out in R2
- **R1, the surprise:** the first 20 seconds: Prenga throws a **right over the top, then a right uppercut through the middle** as Joshua closes (he doesn't see it through the gap). Two knockdowns, the ref counts, Joshua "rocking and rolling around the ring", back to the corner after "one of the worst rounds of his career", scored 10-7. Joshua's own words: "I wasn't... he's a strong guy".
- **R2, how the favorite came back:** he's keeping the distance, **holding on when hurt** in R1, then his feet and head are clear; his corner says **get the jab going**. Prenga is "swinging from the hip", "took his foot off the gas", didn't jump all over a hurt man (experience: 21 fights, none at this level). Joshua steps **behind a solid jab, pushing him to the ropes**, throws the right hand: **"if you land it, throw it again"**, a stagger, then a higher right hand to finish. 10th KO in the 2nd round of his career.
- **What the booth said about the miss:** "if you give him an inch the power is still decisive". The winner of the first round **hurt him and let him live**. A fresh hunter without a plan (just swings) is the risk, not a hurt-man tell.
- Have: `hunt` has a "don't rush a man who still has power" check (`BR.fin`). New: the finisher's **experience** gating how well he finishes a man he hurt twice: low experience = swings, loses the thread, comes up short. The hurt man's recovery in R2 (jab again, distance) with the judges seeing a 10-7 round to overcome.

## 5. "It Was An Early Stoppage!" Usyk and Verhoeven react (the argument around a stoppage)
- **The call:** after ten rounds, in the 11th the ref (Mark Lyson) stepped in at **2:59 of the round, one second left** (stopped from a knockdown/damage; caption doesn't spell it). Usyk keeps the unified heavyweight belts.
- **The loser's case:** "I thought it was an early stoppage... the referee knows we're almost at the end of the round... let me go out on my shield, or let the bell go." He said cards felt "pretty equal". His team was upset too.
- **Booth side:** "I still would have had him up going into the 12th, even with the knockdown. One second left." Everyone praised the loser: **a kickboxer in his first big boxing night**, smart, used his physical strength, "great chin", a style Usyk hadn't faced.
- **The winner's side:** it was hard; the finish was **the right uppercut, then the check hook, "bam bam bam"**.
- Have: the ref stops it from damage or on a knockdown rule; `REFS` have dials. New: **a late-round stoppage when the bell is seconds away** is the one the crowd argues with; a ref who is more likely to save a man in the last 5-10 seconds, or less, is a dial of its own. Colour for post-fight commentary ("early stoppage" vs "had to stop it").

## What's new for us (each one an engine check first)
1. **The stool stop on one eye + a heavy body, even when he's level.** Saunders was not losing; the corner stopped it. The current RTD check needs "behind on the cards and worn". Add an eye/body reason and let the corner act when the fight looks like it is about to turn (Porter's towel line: stop it on what could happen).
2. **A punch on the break costs the hitter a point and gives the hurt man time.** The Garcia-Haney round shows a hurt man living through a hold, a ref who does not let the other man fight one-handed, and a deduction with no warning. Ties to `skillr.md` batch 1 "the break is live" and `ring.md` fouls.
3. **The hurt man's order of survival:** hold first, then turn and move, then cover; the champion got back to the jab inside two rounds, and the jab count (35 to 6 through 8) is what kept the cards close. (Helps `hurtResp`.)
4. **The winner who hurt him and didn't finish:** Prenga-R1 and Garcia-R7/10/11 both show the hunter running short on ideas after the big moment. A "post-KD" window (adrenaline dump, poise vs panic) tied to experience/IQ.
5. **A habit that gets found:** Haney dipping to the same side and Garcia's dropped right. A "same-side dip" flaw (the hook finds it) fits `BR.flaw` / the f3 traits pass.
6. **Early stoppage at the bell as a ref dial** (last seconds of a round); colour for the booth.
