# The Mind of a Boxer: coaching during the fight

How the corner reaches a boxer mid-round and between rounds, how much he absorbs, how fast it shows, how long it lasts. Feeds the coach calls ("pick it up", "slow down", "go to the body", "double the jab", "cut the ring", "tie him up", "go get him", "bank it", "steal the round"). The 15 corner calls and their effects are in `mind_pace.md` section 6; trap/adjust timing and the corner-talk tone note are in `mind_traps.md`. Not repeated here.

Source note: the proxy blocked page fetches (boxingnews, titleboxing, ESPN, DAZN all failed DNS), so everything is from search snippets. Tags: [snippet] = from a search result summary, [unverified] = I could not confirm the claim, [mem] = my memory of the fight, (est.) = my own estimate. **There is no published data on how much a corner call changes punch output.** Section 4 is estimates, labeled as such.

## 1. Mid-round shouts

### Can he hear and act on them?

- Coaches say hearing the corner is a trained skill, not a given: in a real fight "everyone will be yelling advice; coaches, fans, friends, your grandma", and the goal is to "filter through the static until you can hear only your corner". Coaches drill it with shadowboxing to cues so the fighter knows the voice and the commands [snippet: Title Boxing, "How to hear your corner"]. Turning up gym music does not do it; sparring in front of a crowd does [snippet: same].
- Too many voices ruins it. Boxing News coach: "only one voice in the corner" [snippet: Boxing News, "the boxing coach's role between rounds"]. Fury vs Usyk 1: SugarHill Steward, Andy Lee and John Fury "competed for airtime between rounds and often provided conflicting instructions"; Fury said afterward the corner believed they were ahead and nobody told him he was down [snippet: SI, Sportskeeda, BoxingScene thread].
- Pacquiao vs Horn: Roach, who "doesn't talk very loud", said he could not get instructions across because Diaz and Buboy were "very vocal and excited" in the corner [snippet: BoxingScene]. ESPN described Roach giving strategy in a tone inaudible past about five feet [snippet: ESPN "Pacquiao seeks many voices"]. Lesson: a calm single voice wins; noise from your own side is the bigger enemy than the crowd.
- Contrast case: Greg Hardy (UFC, empty arena 2020) said he heard his coach clearly and adjusted. It shows crowd noise normally costs something [snippet: TMZ]. No boxer quote found on "could not hear my corner" [snippet: search found none].
- Fighters can ignore it. Canelo, on Reynoso: "he tells me things but I do what comes to me" (paraphrase of La Opinion's headline) [snippet: La Opinion, Sep 2022]. Reynoso's DAZN-audible scolds in GGG 3 ("stop dropping your hands", "do the last three rounds right") came at the breaks, not as shouts [snippet: Mediotiempo, Marca USA]. Game ignores this (fighter always listens) but it is the real reason for a future trust stat.

### What works live vs only between rounds

| Works live (during the round) | Why | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| One or two words, tied to a drilled meaning | Heard in a gap, no processing time | Cus D'Amato numbers: 1 = jab, then hook/uppercut/body numbers, drilled until hearing the number = throwing it, combos of up to 7 punches called at speed [snippet: Title Boxing, Leslie Parke memoir]. Numbers were called by gym mates from the corner in old Tyson fights [snippet: Parke]; Atlas or Rooney shouting numbers in-fight is [unverified]. |
| Intensity/urgency words | Needs no decoding | "Throw more", "let them go", "you've got six minutes to go, son" (Roach, after R10 vs Bradley, a between-rounds line) [snippet] |
| Reminders of a plan already given | Cue only | "Jab!", "Body!", "Hands!" (cue-drill idea, Title Boxing) |

| Only works between rounds | Why |
|---|---|
| Anything with a reason ("he drops his right after the jab, counter it") | Needs a sentence and a calm head |
| A new plan, a stance switch, a scoring update ("you're down, you need this") | Needs the fighter to rethink |
| Two or more linked instructions | Absorb limit (section 2) |

Rule of thumb (est.): live shout = a reminder or a lever on something he already knows. It cannot install a new idea.

### Is coaching during a round legal?

| Where | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Florida (pro) | Seconds **may** verbally coach during a round; no profanity, no "excessive" coaching (commission rep decides); must stay seated during the round | [snippet: FAC 61K1-3.011, amended Dec 2024] |
| Washington (pro) | "Corner second shouting" is listed among fouls | [snippet: WAC boxing rules] |
| New Mexico | Old text barred coaching "during the progress of the round"; that language was struck in the Jan 2024 amendment. One second in the ring between rounds; seconds seated during rounds | [snippet: NM Register XXXV-2] |
| Ohio | Bans loud yelling or profanity by corner | [snippet: OAC 3773-7-17] |
| Pascua Yaqui (tribal) | No aid to the athlete during a round | [snippet] |
| Amateur (AIBA/Olympic) | Not found [unverified]. Study below recorded coach talk **between** rounds only | Halperin et al. |

So pro boxing mostly tolerates seated, reasonable shouting, and every pro corner does it. Practice: short calls from the apron at the stool side, loudest on the ring-apron side nearest the fighter, plus the 10-second clapper (`mind_pace.md` section 3).

## 2. Between rounds: the 60 seconds

- Real usable time is ~45-50 seconds, not 60 (stool, mouthpiece, water, cut work) [snippet: Boxing News coach].
- First ~30 seconds is for breath and calming down; technical talk comes after. Then "clear information, just one or two things because he won't be able to absorb any more". A fighter "only remembers the last thing you tell him". One voice only [snippet: Boxing News, "the boxing coach's role between rounds"]. Matches the note already in `mind_pace.md` section 6.
- A broadcast explainer says the same: a trainer focuses on one or two areas to correct next round [snippet].
- Amateur data (the only real dataset): Halperin, Chapman, Martin, Abbiss, Wulf 2016, *Psychology of Sport and Exercise*. 12 coaches, 2015 Australian Championships, 25 bouts (14 won, 11 lost), 445 feedback statements, **~8 statements per round**. Of statements coded for focus/control/valence (neutral excluded): internal focus 15% vs external 6%, controlling 53% vs autonomy-supportive 6%, positive 29% vs negative 12%. Winning bouts: less internal (12% vs 19%), less controlling (48% vs 58%), more positive (36% vs 18%). Correlation only, no causation, no punch counts [snippet: ECU/UNLV/ACU abstracts].
  - Reading it for the game: coaches **say** ~8 things; the fighter keeps ~1-2 (coach rule). The rest is noise, which is why the order of the last sentence matters.
- Dundee's pattern: calm and positive even when losing [snippet: Arum quote on Dundee as "greatest motivator"]. Fury 2020-24 shows the opposite pattern, many voices.

Turnarounds that went wrong or failed: Fury-Usyk 1 (conflicting corner, no score update, split-decision loss) [snippet]; Canelo-Bivol R12 ("you can't knock him out anymore" is a damage-control line, lost UD) [snippet]; Joshua-Klitschko R6/R7 (caution plan over "finish him") [snippet]; Joshua-Ruiz 1, McCracken "one round at a time" with a concussed Joshua [snippet: Ring].

Pattern (est.): the famous turnarounds are almost all **urgency** plus **one tactical lever** (body, finish, fire up), delivered in the late rounds by a corner that knew the cards. Early-round turnarounds are rarer and quieter.

## 3. Speed and persistence

Nobody has measured this; what follows is from the fights above plus coach rules (est.).

| Stage | What the evidence says | Timing (est.) |
|---|---|---|
| Hearing to action, live shout | A cue he already knows (a punch number) is acted on as soon as the next opening, one to two exchanges. Simple auditory reaction is a fraction of a second; decoding a sentence plus choosing a punch is far slower (no ms figure found for spoken commands [snippet]) | Next exchange: 1-3 s. Full effect by 5-8 s |
| Between rounds, instruction | "Fighters come out with whatever they just got, the first exchange often shows the adjustment" (`mind_pace.md` section 3) | First 20-30 s of the next round (feel-out is where it shows) |
| Leonard R13, Klitschko R7, Loma R10 | The effect landed in the **very next round**, sometimes in the first half minute | Round after the instruction |
| Persistence | Corners say a fighter "only remembers the last thing", so a plan fades unless re-said. Dundee/Steward re-said the push every round | Half-life ~1 round (est.) for a strong corner call; ~15-25 s for a live shout (est.) |
| Drift back to habit | Fury vs Pianeta: Davison's steady "keep doing what you're doing" was easy because it matched habit [snippet: Sportskeeda]. Opposed-to-habit calls decay faster (Canelo reverts to "what comes naturally") | Call that fights his style: half-life ~0.7 round (est.). Call that matches style: ~1.5 rounds (est.) |
| Reinforcement | If the call pays off (a jab lands, a body shot hurts), fighters keep doing it. This is already `TEACH` in the sim | Payoff doubles the stick time |

Tiring and being hurt shorten all of it. A hurt man gets one thing: Dundee's "last round, loosen up" style, McCracken's "one round at a time" with Joshua. Atlas's long speeches to Moorer were between rounds with a fresh brain, not live.

## 4. Effect size (est., no published data)

What exists: coach talk is correlated with winning in amateur boxing (Halperin 2016) but nobody measured punches. No CompuBox study on "round after the corner told him X". The numbers below are my estimates from the pace file's curve multipliers and the turnaround examples.

| Call | Likely change in next round (est.) | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| "Pick it up / let your hands go" | Output +10 to +20% (thrown), accuracy -3 to -5 pts | Behind-on-the-cards champions step on the gas (`mind_pace.md` section 4); Leonard's surge was 25-punch bursts, an extreme. A normal fighter can only lift output ~20% before gas bites |
| "Slow down, pick your shots" | Output -10 to -20%, connect +3 to +5 pts, stamina cost down | Opposite of above, same range |
| "Go to the body" | Body share from ~20% of power to 30-40% (x1.5 to x2) | Loma, Inoue, Ward examples; the payoff is delayed (legs, late) |
| "Double the jab" | Jab share +10 to +15 pts of thrown, more jabs landed 1.3-1.6x | Jab ~50% of thrown already; doubling is a rhythm change, not a volume change |
| "Cut the ring" | Pressure steps up, distance closed 15-25% faster, more clean exchanges on the ropes | Pressure side effect; not measured |
| "Tie him up" | Clinch chance per contact x1.5 to x2 | Survival plan after a hurt (`mind_pace.md` section 6, #14) |
| "Go get him" / finish | Output x1.3-1.6 for the burst, risk up; needs a hurt opponent to pay | `mind_pace.md` section 5 finishing knobs |
| "Bank it / protect the lead" | Output -10%, more jab and move, avoid exchanges | `mind_pace.md` section 4: leading late = ease off |
| "Steal the round" | Final 30 s output +15 to +35% (stealer trait raises it) | `mind_pace.md` section 3 |

Judgement call (est.): a corner talk-in at its best moves **style tendency** by about 20-40% of the gap to the target, not 100%. Live shout moves it half to a third as much. That fits the sim's existing `SHOUTS` (jab x1.6, body x2.3, but gated by quality `q` and fit).

## 5. Game knobs

Fits the existing machinery: `SHOUTS` mods mixed through `mixMods`, `SHOUT_LEN` (14 sim-s, about 42 s of clock at the 3x timescale in the code comment), `TEACH`, `planOf`. Suggested numbers (est., test with `shout.js` and `teach.js`):

| Knob | Suggestion | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Corner instruction strength | 1.0 (reference) | Mixed into the round plan, what `STRATS` / `planOf` already do |
| Live shout strength | **0.35 to 0.5 of a corner instruction** | Only a lever on something he knows; cannot install a plan |
| Same call said live AND at the break | live adds on top, capped at 1.1x the corner value | A reminder, not stacking |
| Delay, corner instruction | 0 s, but the first 15-20 sim-s of the round is feel-out (existing `feel1/feelN`); full effect by ~25 s clock | Matches "first exchange shows the adjustment" |
| Delay, live shout | 1 to 3 s clock to start, ramp to full over 5 s | Next exchange |
| Decay half-life, live shout | 15 to 25 s clock (about the existing 14 sim-s `SHOUT_LEN` = 42 s fade-out; use as the end, not the half-life) | Gone by ~40 s if not reinforced |
| Decay half-life, corner instruction | ~1 round (3 min), so ~50% left next round, ~25% two rounds on | Re-say to refresh. Existing `TEACH.keep .7` per break fits |
| Fights his style | half-life x0.7 (dies faster) | "Bank it" for a pressure fighter; "slow down" for a volume man |
| Matches his style | half-life x1.5 | "Double the jab" for a jabber |
| Instruction cap | **2 live per round, 1-2 at the break; a 3rd cancels the oldest** (or halves the first two) | Coach rule: fighter keeps the last thing |
| Stacking | Linked pairs fine ("jab then body"); contradictory pair (slow down + pick it up) = neither | Also kills "conflicting voices" like Fury |
| Payoff reinforces | A landed shot after the call: half-life x1.5, existing `TEACH.step` | Already built |
| Urgency calls | "Pick it up", "steal the round", "go get him" are urgency only; strength scales with the **score gap and round**: worth more late and behind (x1.3), worth less early and ahead (x0.7) | Dundee/Leonard R13 |

### How hurt and gassed change a live shout (even though he always listens)

Listening is binary (always true). **How much it shows** is the fighter's state:

| State | Live shout effect (x of normal) | Break instruction effect | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh, composed | 1.0 | 1.0 | Baseline |
| Gassed (stamina < ~0.35) | 0.75 on output calls ("pick it up" cannot make him throw more than the tank allows); 1.0 on defensive calls ("hands", "tie him up") | 0.9 | Output calls are capped by gas, not attention |
| Hurt (`shook` / low composure) | 0.4 for tactical calls; 1.0 for survival calls (tie up, move, hands up, "last thing you tell him") | 0.6; survival calls 1.0 | Hurt men fixate on defense; Joshua got "one round at a time" |
| Low Ring IQ | 0.7 on calls that need reading ("cut the ring", "counter") | 0.8 | Existing `teachK` already weights IQ and experience |
| High experience / smart | 1.0, and the call can be adapted ("body" becomes "body when he's open") | 1.0 | `teachK` |
| Crowd noise (optional) | Big arena x0.9 | none | No data; (est.), skip if too fiddly |
| Cut, blood in eyes, pain | x0.85 | x0.9 | Distraction |
| Opponent mid-flurry | Call delayed until the next gap, not lost | n/a | Only one exchange at a time |

Defaults for state weighting: `shoutQ = base * (0.5 + 0.5 * stamina) * (1 - 0.5 * hurt)` for output calls; survival calls ignore hurt. Same shape as the existing `orderPow` gate `q`.

### Cheap sanity targets (est.)

- A single good live shout at its best should shift the tracked stat (jab share, body share, clinch rate) by 10 to 20% of the target, not more; a corner instruction 25 to 40%.
- Output calls must stay inside the audit: "pick it up" then +10 to +20% thrown for one round, followed by -3 to -5% the round after as the gas bill arrives (`mind_pace.md` section 4, missing costs 1.6x).
- A coach who spams (3+ live calls a round) should do worse than one who picks one. Halperin: winners got fewer controlling statements and more positive ones. Matches the existing "1 instruction + 1 positive" note in `mind_traps.md`.
- Fury-Usyk 1 should be reproducible: two contradictory calls on the same round give about zero, not an average.
