# Move Flow: how elite fights actually move, measured

Scope: motion and rhythm on screen (time-motion, kinematics, footwork, exchange shape).
Pacing, strategy and engage logic live in `mind_pace.md`, `mind_engage.md`, `combat_research.md` §5. Not repeated here.

Tags:
- (snip) = number seen only in a search-result snippet or abstract. Full text was blocked by the sandbox proxy (PMC, MDPI, ResearchGate, T&F, Frontiers, arXiv, Wikipedia all refused).
- (est.) = our inference or arithmetic. Not a measured fact.
- No tag = stated the same way in two or more snippets, or in an abstract.

Caveat up front: almost all time-motion science is **amateur / Olympic** (3 x 3 min) or **kickboxing**.
No peer-reviewed time-motion profile of elite **pro** 12-round boxing was found. Pro numbers below are CompuBox only.

## 1. Time-motion: what a round is made of

### 1.1 Elite amateur activity profile (Davis et al.)
- Olympic finals/semis, 29 bouts (Davis 2015): **~1.4 actions/s**. Per minute: **~20 punches, ~2.5 defensive moves, ~47 vertical hip movements (VHM)**. Rounds ran ~200 s with stoppages.
- World Champs after 2013 rules, 50 bouts (Davis 2018): **~1.55 actions/s**. Per minute: **~21 punches, ~3.6 defensive moves, ~56 VHM**. ~62 punches per round (snip).
- "Action" = punch + defensive move + VHM, divided by net activity time (round minus clinch minus referee stops).
- After 2013 the sport went more long-range, straight-punch based, "more defence and more movement around the ring" (abstract).
- Winners in 2018: more straight punches (both hands) in R1, more landed in R3, fewer air punches.

### 1.2 Clinch and stop time
- Elite amateur clinch total per round, 2012 Olympics: **12.6 s, 17.2 s, 28.7 s** for R1-R3 (snip; SD ~11-18 s).
- After 2013 rules: **9.2 s, 16.4 s, 17.1 s** (snip).
- So clinching is ~5-16% of a 180 s round, rising every round (est. from above).
- Activity-to-break ratio: elite ~18:1 to **19.3:1**; novices ~9:1, falling 16:1 -> 8:1 -> 6:1 across rounds.
- Women's 2023 World Champs finals: activity-to-interruption ratio **6.6:1** (snip). Clinch made up most of the interruption time.
- Heavier divisions clinch more (women's 81+ kg mostly clinch; European heavyweights longer clinches than Pan-Am) (snip).

### 1.3 Burst length and gap length (kickboxing is the only measured source)
- Elite kickboxing World Champs (Ouergui 2014): **high-intensity action = 2.2 +/- 1.2 s**. **Low-intensity (bouncing, sliding) = 2.2-2.3 s**. Referee pause 5.4 +/- 4.3 s.
- **Gap between consecutive high-intensity actions ~3.4 s**, filled with bouncing/sliding, not rest.
- Fighting : non-fighting ~1:1 in that study. Pause time rose by round (12.8, 22.3, 24.6 s).
- Elite low-kick: fighting bouts **~1.6 s**, preparatory phases **~2.5 s**, ratio ~1:1.5 (snip).
- Boxing has fewer stoppages than kickboxing. So boxing gaps are probably similar length but less dead time (est.).

### 1.4 What this means for a round (est.)
- 1.4-1.55 actions/s = **something visible every ~0.65-0.7 s**.
- 47-56 VHM/min = **a hip/level change every ~1.1-1.3 s**. This is the single best "never stop-motion" number we have.
- 2.5-3.6 defensive moves/min = one obvious slip/roll/block every **17-24 s** (counted, not every guard tweak).
- Burst ~1.6-2.2 s + gap ~2.5-3.4 s = **one engagement cycle every ~4-6 s** -> **~30-45 cycles per 3 min** (est., kickboxing-based).
- Pro boxing is slower than amateur. Pro averages ~40-60 thrown/round (CompuBox below) vs amateur ~62. Expect **~20-30 exchanges per pro round**, gaps **~4-7 s** (est.).

### 1.5 Missing
- No study gives exchange length, punches per exchange, ring-crossings, or distance covered (m) for boxing. See §8.

## 2. Punch kinematics (mocap)

### 2.1 Durations
- Piorkowski 2011 (10 competition boxers, 8-camera mocap):
  - Straights (jab/cross) delivered faster than hooks. Jab vs lead hook: no significant difference.
  - Straight, ACTION -> CONTACT: **357 +/- 178 ms** (snip, secondary citation in a thesis).
  - Hook, ACTION -> CONTACT, all modes: **477 +/- 203 ms** (snip).
  - Single maximal cross, PROJECTION -> CONTACT: **223 +/- 99 ms** (snip).
  - Reverse hook inside a combination, PROJ -> CONTACT: **125 +/- 34 ms** (snip).
  - "In-synch" combos are faster than single maximal punches or "out-of-synch" combos.
  - Authors: defender "may have more evasion time than previously reported".
- Stanley 2018 (15 amateurs, mocap + force plates, max effort into a bag): jab shortest delivery **405 ms** (SD reported as 0.15 ms, likely a typo). Range across six punches 405-657 ms (snip; 657 from a citing paper).
  - These 400+ ms include wind-up from a static start. They are lab maximal efforts, not fight jabs (est.).
- Elite lead vs rear straight (17 elite boxers, 2026): lead hand **shorter completion time**; rear hand more force, more velocity, **more trunk rotation ROM** (abstract, no numbers retrieved).
- Fist-on-target contact time: **~20 ms jab, ~30 ms cross** (MDPI 2024, 13 advanced boxers) (snip).
- Force peak time on target: **~16.5-17 ms** (Liu 2023) (snip).

### 2.2 Speed
- Lead hook peak fist velocity **11.95 +/- 1.84 m/s** (Stanley 2018). Hooks faster at the fist than straights.
- Single maximal punches ~**9.26 m/s**; the same punches inside combos **~7.5-8.0 m/s** (Piorkowski).
- Lead straight peak **7.31 +/- 0.42 m/s** in boxers (Liu 2023, Vicon).
- Rear straight 6.97 m/s (snip, secondary; treat with caution).
- Jab impact velocity lower than cross (coaches' study, abstract).
- Rear cross has higher acceleration than the lead jab even though the jab "looks" faster (MDPI 2024).

### 2.3 Reaction and anticipation
- Rear straight to a light, competition period **~310 ms**, fatigued **~390 ms** (already in `combat_research.md` §5).
- Jab reactions faster than cross reactions (Loturco 2015, 22 Brazil national team; p<0.001; ms values not retrieved).
- Studies drop responses **<100 ms** as anticipatory, so true reactions are >100 ms.
- Elite vs lower-level defenders: peak effort comes **22 ms earlier**, peak joint moments up to 16.5% lower, more consistent EMG (2026 defensive-movement mocap) (snip).
- Expert boxers read attacks from video cut at **-80 ms / -40 ms** before the action starts (occlusion study) (snip).
- One teen study reports boxer simple RT "23.7 ms". That is physiologically impossible; probably a unit error. Do not use.

### 2.4 Head / torso per punch
- Head moves in both forward-back and side-to-side axes during a rear straight; authors flagged head velocity as notable (Cheraghi 2014, abstract, no numbers).
- Rear straight is leg-driven; stretch-shortening in the legs (same).
- No head-displacement-per-punch numbers found.

## 3. Footwork

- Winners **bounce more and step less** than losers in all three rounds (Dunn 2017, 26 elite national-level boxers). A bounce:step index plus accuracy classified 84.6% of results.
- Bouncing **falls** from R1 to R2 while clinch time and guard drops rise (Dunn 2017).
- Winners "more footwork" than losers in Olympic data (snip).
- Defensive actions using footwork ~**20%**, using trunk ~**20%** (London 2012 welterweight analysis) (snip).
- Forward double-step cadence in student boxers **~182-183 steps/min** at baseline (conference paper, weak) (snip).
- VHM (47-56/min) is the closest measured proxy for "on the toes" bounce rhythm.
- **Not found:** step length, lateral vs forward vs back share, pivots per round, stance width change, % time on toes.

## 4. Exchanges

- Winners land more (33% vs 23%) and throw fewer air punches (17% vs 27%). Total punches, defence and interaction time were **similar** for winners and losers (Dunn 2017).
- Winners use more **block-and-counter** combinations; defensive moves "must initiate a counter-attack" (Davis 2013 / Beneke 2013, novices).
- Winners use *fewer* defensive moves overall (Davis 2015). Elite defence = fewer but purposeful moves (est.).
- Jabbr DeepStrike tracks "pressure" as **% of round** (cornering, throwing first) and ~50 metrics/boxer, trained on 7,000 judged rounds. No exchange counts published.
- **Not found:** who initiates (lead/counter share), counter delay in ms, how exchanges end, ring-position shift per exchange.
- Working estimate (est.): counter lands ~0.3-0.5 s after the lead punch starts = one reaction (>=100-310 ms) + one short projection (~125-220 ms).

## 5. Classic fights: rhythm and numbers

Numbers are CompuBox via news snippets (snip). Rhythm notes are our reading of the fights (est.).

### Hagler-Hearns R1 (1985)
- Combined **165 thrown in 180 s**, **106 landed**, 95 power landed. Hagler 50/82, Hearns 56/83. **Hagler threw zero jabs.**
- Middleweight average then ~25 landed/round; this round was ~4x.
- Rhythm (est.): no gaps. Continuous pocket war, ~0.9 punches/s each. Camera barely pans. The outlier, not the template.

### Canelo-GGG 1 (2017)
- GGG **218/703** (31%; ~59 thrown/rd). Canelo **169/505** (33%; ~42/rd). Power: GGG 110/342, Canelo 114/272 (42%).
- GGG out-landed Canelo in 10 of 12 rounds.
- Rhythm (est.): GGG walks forward behind the jab; Canelo answers in short 2-3 punch counters then rolls off the ropes. Many short resets in the middle of the ring.

### Loma-Rigondeaux (2017, 6 rds)
- Loma **55/339** (16%; 56.5 thrown/rd). Rigo **15/178** (8%; 29.7/rd). Rigo jabs 10/114.
- Landed by round: Loma 2, 12, 7, 16, 7, 11. Rigo 2, 3, 2, 3, 2, 3.
- Rhythm (est.): lots of motion, very few clean connections. Loma circles and angles; Rigo holds and resets. Proof that a fight can look busy with low landed numbers.

### Crawford-Spence (2023)
- Crawford **185/369** (50%). Jabs **87/206** (42%). Power **98 landed**; power thrown = 369 - 206 = 163 -> ~60% (est.; one source misprints 263).
- Spence down in R2 and twice in R7.
- Rhythm (est.): Crawford's jab to the body/head controls range; exchanges short; he wins them and steps out.

### Usyk-Fury 1 (2024)
- Landed **Usyk 170, Fury 157**; accuracy ~42% vs ~32%. After R3 Fury led jabs 25-14, Usyk led power 21-12.
- R9: Usyk ~18 landed at 53%, Fury ~11 at 39% (snip, unlabeled table). Fury in trouble in R9.
- Usyk landed 36 power in R10-12 vs Fury 15.
- Rematch: Usyk 179/423 (42.3%), Fury 144/509 (28.3%).
- Rhythm (est.): Usyk constant lateral stepping to his left, feints, short 2-3 punch bursts, then out.

### Inoue-Donaire 1 (2019)
- Landed **Inoue 227, Donaire 141**; power 116-99 (snip). Inoue 82-34 landed in R10-12 (snip).
- Donaire cut Inoue in R2. Inoue rallied late.
- Rhythm (est.): both counter-punchers; long feint/jab gaps, then sudden single big shots.

### Mayweather-Canelo (2013)
- Accuracy Mayweather **46%** vs Canelo **22%**; Mayweather landed ~100 more jabs (fan summary, snip). Thrown totals not verified.
- Rhythm (est.): Mayweather on the back foot, pull-counters, lead-hand jabs, then a step or pivot off the line every few seconds.

### Takeaways
- Elite thrown per man per round: **~30 (Rigo) to ~59 (GGG)**, peaks 80+ (Hagler-Hearns R1).
- Elite connect: **16-50%**; most title fights land in **30-42%**.
- Low-output elite fights still **move all the time**; output and motion are separate dials.

## 6. Engine / render translation (60 Hz, 1 frame = 16.7 ms)

### 6.1 Round rhythm targets
- **Something visible every 0.65-0.7 s** (punch, defence, feint, level change). From 1.4-1.55 actions/s.
- **Hip/level change every 1.1-1.3 s.** Hard cap: hips/torso never static >1.3 s (~78 frames) outside a clinch (est. from 47-56 VHM/min).
- **Exchange (burst) length 1.5-2.5 s.** From kickboxing 1.6-2.2 s.
- **Gap between exchanges 2.5-4 s amateur pace, 4-7 s pro pace** (est.). Fill the gap with bounce/slide/feint, never idle.
- **~20-30 exchanges/round** per pair at pro pace (est.). With 53 thrown/man, that is ~2-2.5 punches per man per exchange (est.).
- **Clinch 5-15% of round**, rising each round (9-29 s/round amateur). Heavyweights more.
- **Clear defensive move** (slip/roll/block that reads on camera) **~3/min** per fighter.
- **Bounce > step** for the better fighter; bounce rate drops from R2 on and with fatigue.

### 6.2 Our numbers: verdict
- **53 thrown/man/round: right.** Inside the elite band (30-59) and near pro averages (~59 at 140 lb). Keep, but spread by style: 30 (Rigo) to 60+ (GGG).
- **29% connect: right.** Elite title fights 30-42%; 16% shows a cagey style. Let style move it 15-50%.
- **Jab load .04 + snap .11 = 0.15 s to contact; total 0.29 s.**
  - Lab "action->contact" straights ~357 ms and Stanley 405 ms are maximal, from a static start. Fight jabs are flicks. Our 0.15 s is fast but plausible for a non-telegraphed jab (est.).
  - Speed check: reach ~0.5-0.6 m in 0.11 s = ~5 m/s average -> peak ~7.5-9.5 m/s with a bell velocity curve (est.). Matches the measured 7.3 m/s lead straight and 7.5-9.3 m/s range. **Good.**
- **Cross .08 + .13 = 0.21 s to contact; total 0.38 s.**
  - Matches single maximal cross PROJ->CONTACT **223 ms**. **Good.**
- **Hooks (0.22-0.25 s to contact) are slightly fast** vs hook action->contact ~477 ms (~1.34x straights). Our hook/jab ratio is fine; absolute single power hooks could load longer (0.12-0.18 s) when thrown alone. In combos keep them fast: combo reverse hook PROJ->CONTACT is **125 ms**, our snap 0.14 matches.
- **Combo punches should be faster and lighter** than singles: speed ~7.5-8 m/s vs ~9.3 m/s single (~15-20% less) (Piorkowski). Scale snap x0.85 and damage x0.85 inside an "in-synch" combo (est.).
- **Contact = 1-2 frames** (20-30 ms measured). Hit-stop beyond that is a stylistic choice, not realism.
- **Retraction: no data found.** Our ret > snap (0.14 vs 0.11) is the usual guess. Keep the hand moving back immediately; no hold at full extension (est.; extension hold not measured).
- **Reaction floor 100 ms, typical 300 ms, fatigued 390 ms.** No AI defence should start <6 frames after the punch's first visible frame unless it is a read (anticipation).
- **Counter delay ~0.3-0.5 s** after the lead punch starts (est.).

### 6.3 Movement targets (mostly est.)
- Range -> pocket entry: one step + punch in the same 0.15-0.25 s (we already ease the step over load+snap; good).
- Direction change: tie to the 1.1-1.3 s hip rhythm; lateral step or pivot at least every 2-3 VHM (est.).
- Step cadence cap ~180/min (3 steps/s) during bursts of footwork (weak source).
- Fatigue: fewer bounces, more flat-footed steps, more clinch, longer gaps (all measured as directions, not magnitudes).
