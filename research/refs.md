# Real referees, 2024-2026 (research for ref reputations)

Two research passes, Oct 20 2026, for Ed's refs-with-reputations idea (`research/corners_video.md`, officials section; the game already has 4 refs in `REFS`). Real names stay out of the game: Ed makes fictional refs from these profiles. Evidence tags and dial scales are explained in each part.

**Takeaways:** no ref has a *documented* house bias (keep bias as a game-flavour knob); split fouls into warn-often vs deduct-threshold; stoppage speed needs an erratic setting, not just early/late; a big-fight leniency for hurt stars (Nelson, Pabon); refs run late more often than early (Bernick 2020: doctors would have stopped 37% of 30 fights sooner, ~220 s).

## Part 1: US boxing referees (active 2024-2026): dial research for Last Bell

Researched Oct 2026. Summaries are written in my own words. Quotes are kept to short phrases.

### How to read this

- **Evidence tags.** [DOC] means the event is on the record: a commission sheet, a fight report, or the referee's own statement. [PRESS] means it is a journalist's or analyst's take. [FAN] means it comes from forums, social media or a fighter's complaint.
- **Highlight bias is strong here.** A referee works hundreds of bouts, and the internet remembers 2 or 3 of them. One infamous call is *not* a tendency. If a dial below is based on only one incident, it says so ("n=1").
- **Dial scale.** Each dial gets a rough 0-10 suggestion. 5 is the league-average US referee.
  - **Clinch:** 0 = breaks every clinch at once, 10 = lets them work inside.
  - **Fouls:** 0 = only warns, 10 = deducts quickly.
  - **Stop:** 0 = stops late, 10 = stops early.
  - **KD calls:** high = rules borderline drops as knockdowns, low = calls them slips or pushes.
  - **Bias:** 5 = neutral, higher = favours the A-side or champion.
- **Base rate for bias.** No US referee has a *documented* house-fighter bias. Every bias claim below is inference from single moments. My recommendation is to keep the bias dial near 5 for real refs and use it as a game-flavour knob.

### Active referees

### 1. Thomas Taylor (California-based; works NV and CA constantly)
- **Status:** Very active and currently the top US big-fight referee. Nevada sheets list him all through 2025 and 2026.
- **Notable fights:** Davis-Garcia (Apr 2023), Canelo-Munguia (May 2024), Benavidez-Morrell (Feb 2025), Inoue-Cardenas (May 2025), Pacquiao-Barrios (Jul 2025), **Canelo-Crawford (Sep 2025)**, Fundora-Thurman (Mar 2026), Cruz-Bravo (2026), Garcia-Benn (2026).
- **Dials:**
  - **Clinch (5).** No documented clinch tendency either way. In Cruz-Bravo he took a point for excessive holding early in round 2 [DOC/PRESS], so he will act on a chronic holder.
  - **Fouls (7).** In Cruz-Bravo he deducted twice for holding and a throw-down, warned the other man too, and threatened DQ, but did not deduct for low blows [PRESS]. In Benavidez-Morrell the NV sheet records a point off for a punch after the bell in R11 [DOC]. He has been praised as firm but "not needlessly intrusive."
  - **Stop (6-7, trending earlier recently).** He has had three disputed stoppages in a short window: Collazo-Vayson 2025 (the corner asked, he deferred), **Fundora-Thurman Mar 2026** (stopped at 1:17 of R6 with Thurman upright and far behind on the cards), and **Garcia-Benn 2026** (R2, right after a knockdown, Benn on the ropes). The Ring noted in March 2026 that he did *not* have an early-stopper reputation before that. The cluster is recent, so treat it as an emerging pattern, not an established one.
  - **KD calls (5).** Nothing notable.
  - **Bias (5).** In each disputed stoppage the stopped man was well behind or hurt. The complaints are fighter and fan complaints [FAN].
- **Famous calls:** Fundora-Thurman (2026), where Thurman publicly said Taylor should not work main events again [FAN, fighter]. Garcia-Benn (2026), widely called early [PRESS/FAN].

### 2. Harvey Dock (New Jersey-based; works NJ, NY, NV, WBC fights)
- **Status:** Active. He worked many Nevada cards in 2025 as a visiting referee.
- **Notable fights:** **Crawford-Spence (Jul 2023)**, stopped in R9. **Fundora-Tszyu I (Mar 2024)**. **Haney-Garcia (Apr 2024, Brooklyn)**. Plant-Resendiz (May 2025). Dychko-Franklin on the Canelo-Crawford card (Sep 2025), where he deducted a point from Dychko in R8 [DOC]. He was also named for Fundora-Tszyu II (2025).
- **Dials:**
  - **Clinch (3, breaks fast and is hands-on).** In Haney-Garcia, critics said his frequent breaks and separations killed Garcia's momentum and gave Haney rest. They also said he did not punish Haney's constant holding [PRESS/FAN]. This rests mostly on that one fight, so it is n=1-ish.
  - **Fouls (6, uneven).** In Haney-Garcia he took a point from Garcia for hitting on the break without a prior warning, which many called heavy-handed. He did not take one from Haney for holding [PRESS]. In Fundora-Tszyu he did not penalise the elbow/head clash that opened a bad cut on Tszyu; Jim Lampley criticised that [PRESS]. Not clearly strict or lenient, so call it "inconsistent."
  - **Stop (5).** Crawford-Spence R9 was widely seen as fair, and he let a heavily bleeding Tszyu go 12 after the doctor cleared him. Neither suggests early or late.
  - **KD calls (4).** In Haney-Garcia he ruled two of Haney's extra trips to the canvas in R7 as slips; reporters called them borderline but defensible [PRESS].
  - **Bias (5).** The WBC president publicly defended him after Haney-Garcia [DOC].
- **Famous call:** Haney-Garcia 2024: a point off Garcia, Haney's holding left unpunished, and the slip rulings. Garcia said Dock could have stopped the fight.

### 3. Tony Weeks (Nevada)
- **Status:** Active despite heavy calls for his retirement after Jan 2024. One report said he faced termination, but I found no confirmed NSAC action. BoxRec shows him working Las Vegas (T-Mobile, Virgin) and Buenos Aires in Aug 2026, and he refereed in the Philippines in Sep 2026. He also judges MMA. He is about 69-70.
- **Notable fights:** **Corrales-Castillo I (2005)**, Jack-Browne (2019), **Romero-Barroso (May 2023)**, **Ortiz-Lawson (Jan 2024)**.
- **Dials:**
  - **Clinch (5).** No documented tendency.
  - **Fouls (4).** No strong record either way.
  - **Stop (bimodal, 7 recently).** His two recent infamous calls were early. In Romero-Barroso he stopped it in R9 after a push and a grazing shot, with Barroso up on all three cards; 3Kings named it Robbery of the Year. In Ortiz-Lawson he stopped it in R1 with Lawson not down, cut or visibly rocked [PRESS]. Older history leans the other way: he let Jack-Browne go 12 with a gushing cut, and he let Corrales continue in 2005, which is a famous "let them fight" moment. So he is erratic rather than consistently early.
  - **KD calls (6).** In Barroso he scored a push as a knockdown [PRESS], n=1.
  - **Bias (5-6).** Romero was the PBC house fighter, and fans read bias into it [FAN]. Nothing is documented.
- **Famous calls:** Romero-Barroso (2023). Ortiz-Lawson (2024): he posted, then deleted, a claim that Lawson had an aneurysm on a scan. Golden Boy and the NSAC said Lawson was fully cleared [DOC].

  - **KD calls (7).** Agbeko-Mares, n=1.
  - **Bias (5).** Nevada keeps giving him Canelo's biggest fights. That says the commission trusts him, not that he is biased.

### 5. Mark Nelson (Minnesota; WBO/WBA/IBF world-title referee, often abroad)
- **Status:** Active. He worked four Nevada cards in 2025 (Mar, May, Jul, Sep), including Cruz-Salcido (WBC interim). He has refereed 100+ world title fights.
- **Notable fights:** **Pacquiao-Horn (2017, Brisbane)**, Rigondeaux fights, **Usyk-Fury I (May 2024, Riyadh)**.
- **Dials:**
  - **Clinch (5).** No data.
  - **Fouls (4).** No data.
  - **Stop (2, gives the benefit of the doubt).** This is his clearest pattern, seen in two huge fights:
    - Pacquiao-Horn 2017 R9: he warned the battered Horn in the corner that he would stop it, then let it continue. Critics called that coaching, and Horn won the decision [PRESS, partisan Philippine outlets].
    - Usyk-Fury I 2024 R9: he gave the badly hurt Fury a count when Haye and others said it should have been stopped. Chisora called it "the slowest count". Defenders (Matt Macklin) said the undisputed heavyweight title deserved the benefit [PRESS/FAN].
    - That is n=2 and consistent: in mega-fights he leans late.
  - **KD calls (7).** He ruled a knockdown on Fury when the ropes held him up and Fury touched down, and that decided the cards [PRESS].
  - **Bias (5-6 toward "big name gets a chance").** Both times the hurt fighter was the bigger draw or the local one. That is an inference from n=2, not a documented pattern.
- **Famous calls:** Pacquiao-Horn (2017), Usyk-Fury I (2024).

### 9. Laurence Cole (Texas)
- **Status:** Active. BoxRec shows Dallas and Irving cards from May to Aug 2026, but he gets fewer top-level assignments than in his 2010s peak. His father Dickie Cole ran Texas combat sports for 21 years until 2014. Critics have long flagged conflict-of-interest issues, including his insurance business selling event policies to promoters [PRESS opinion].
- **Notable fights:** Marquez-Jaca (2006), **Salido-Lomachenko (Mar 2014, San Antonio)**, **Figueroa-Burns (2015)**, Broner-Maidana (2013), **Spence-Ugas (Apr 2022, Arlington)**. He also worked Canelo (Alamodome) and Fundora (AT&T Stadium) bouts.
- **Dials:** He is the most documented "character" referee in this list.
  - **Clinch (2, very hands-on).** In Figueroa-Burns he repeatedly warned Burns and physically yanked him apart in clinches; one review called it "the Laurence Cole Show" [PRESS opinion].
  - **Fouls (2, warns but won't deduct).** In Salido-Lomachenko, Salido fouled low and held throughout, and Cole did not deduct. Afterwards he admitted he probably should have taken points from both fighters [DOC, his own words]. Bob Arum blasted him.
  - **Stop (3, can run late).** The BWAA put him on its officials "watch list" after he let a fighter (Velasco) take about 35 more seconds after signalling he was done [PRESS]. In Spence-Ugas he stopped the fight for a doctor check and defended it as safety-first, which was early in that case. So he is erratic.
  - **KD calls (5).** No data.
  - **Bias (7, perceived house/Texas lean).** In Marquez-Jaca he told Marquez mid-fight that he was ahead on the cards and was suspended 6 months and fined [DOC]. The house-fighter lean is a long-running [FAN/PRESS] narrative (Figueroa was the Texas A-side vs Burns), and the conflict-of-interest claims are critics' opinions. It is the best real-world template for a "house ref" dial, but it is not proven bias.
- **Famous calls:** Marquez-Jaca (2006, suspension). Salido-Lomachenko (2014, fouls unpunished).

**Design notes for the game:**
1. The stoppage dial should have an "erratic" (variance) setting as well as its mean. Weeks and Cole both have infamous early *and* late calls.
2. A "big-fight leniency" modifier fits the Nelson pattern: give a hurt star an extra count in a title fight.
3. A fouls dial alone misses Cole and Dock. "Warns a lot but rarely deducts" and "deducts without warning" are different behaviours, so warning frequency and deduction threshold need to be separate dials.
4. Real refs should probably use fictional names in-game, given that the bias claims are unproven.

## Part 2: Non-US referees (UK, Riyadh, Europe, Japan, Australia): research notes for Last Bell ref dials

Compiled Oct 2026. All notes are paraphrased. Tags used:
- **[DOC]**: documented in a fight report or official ruling.
- **[PATTERN?]**: one or two incidents. Treat it as a tendency, not a proven style.
- **[FAN]**: forum, fan or pundit opinion.

**Highlight bias warning.** Referees become "known" for their one or two viral calls. Hundreds of routine fights never get written up. For every ref below, the evidence is a handful of controversial nights, not a measured rate. The dial values are my own inference from those nights, so treat them as game-design flavour rather than fact. On the scales below, 1 means low or slow or lenient and 5 means high or fast or strict.

### 1. Howard Foster (UK, BBBofC)
- **Status:** Active. English, one of the BBBofC's top "Star Class" refs. He also judges big cards: he was a judge on Dubois vs Joshua in 2024.

### How often does a referee change the outcome? (thin evidence)
- **No solid boxing dataset exists** on deductions per fight or on how often a ref's call flips a result. I found nothing peer-reviewed for pro boxing.
- **Stoppage timing.** Bernick et al. (2020 medRxiv preprint, later in *The Physician and Sportsmedicine*) had a panel review 30 PBC boxing and 30 UFC fights.
  - Physician raters felt 37% of the boxing fights (11 of 30) should have been stopped sooner. For boxing, the excess averaged roughly 220 seconds, with a range of 6-835 s.
  - The fighter concussed first lost about 98% of the time.
  - This means refs more often run late than early, which is the opposite of the "early stoppage" outrage seen in highlights.
  - Small sample, US cards only.
  - Sources: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.04.28.20073221.full.pdf, https://combatsportslaw.com/2020/12/02/study-first-fighter-to-be-concussed-loses-98-of-the-time/
- **Amateur referee-stopped contests.** Bianco et al. (BJSM 2013) studied 29,357 Olympic bouts from 1952-2011. Ref stoppages rose from about 9.7% to 13.1% after headguards came in (1984). They fell to about 5.9% when bouts were shortened. So the rules frame matters as much as the individual ref. Source: https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201703002094/RIS
- **Deductions benchmark (MMA, not boxing).** An AgentMMA audit found point deductions in about 1.6% of UFC bouts since 2010 (120 of 7,491). That makes deductions rare events, and boxing is plausibly in the same low-single-digit-% range. This is unverified and non-peer-reviewed. Source: https://agentmma.com/mma-lab/ufc-point-deduction-rate
- **Anecdotal outcome-flippers in this list:**
  - Foster: Groves, Parker (both leading on the cards when stopped).
  - Lyson: Verhoeven (level or leading).
  - Alexander: Warrington (leading).
  - McDonnell: Donovan DQ (leading).
  - Pabon: Dubois low-blow ruling.
  - Lyson: Edwards vs Martinez (overturned to NC).

  The pattern in highlights is that fighters stopped late in a fight they were winning get remembered. Fights stopped correctly do not. That is the highlight bias at work, not a rate.
