# The Mind of a Boxer: when to engage, when not to

Scope: the decision layer (engage, wait, reset, hold), not style DNA (see fighters_a.md / fighters_b.md).
Sourcing note: quotes below came from web search result text; most source pages could not be opened from this container (egress blocked), so wording is as reported by the outlet listed. [unverified] = paraphrase or analyst consensus, not a checked quote. Source list at the bottom.

## 1. Core principles

- **The goal is a trade you win, not activity.** Mayweather: "I'm a boxer who believes that the object of the sport is to hit and not get hit." [S1] Whitaker (via Lou Duva): "the most beautiful feeling in the world, to hit the other guy and not get hit." [S2]
- **Throw when he cannot hit back, not when you can hit him.** Elite engagement is timed to the opponent's weak moments (mid-punch, feet moving, recovering), not to own readiness. D'Amato's system aimed for positions "where you can hit the fighter, and they cannot hit you" [S3, paraphrase in source].
- **Each range has an owner.** Long fighter owns outside (jab, half-step back). Short fighter owns inside. Mid range is where both can hit, so the smart fighter passes through it fast or makes it costly. Steward on fighting a long jabber: "You have to fight Wladimir's arms before you can fight Wladimir." [S4] Ward on Kovalev: "Sergey has no inside game, so I focused on my midrange and my inside game, and that made all the difference." [S5]
- **Risk is a budget, not a mood.** Shakur, after taking more than usual vs Zepeda: "I wanna get back on my boxing, make sure I don't take a lotta punishment in there." [S6] Shakur's logic on KO-dependent opponents: "If the only thing you can do to win is knock me out, you've lost." [S6]
- **Make him pay vs don't get hit are two ends of one dial.** Pressure camp (Porter, Canelo under Reynoso) accepts some shots to own position. Reynoso's whole plan vs Bivol: "Cut off the ring and hit on the counter." [S7] Kenny Porter on Shawn: "He's going to be there in front of Errol for three minutes a round for all 12 rounds." [S8]
- **Find the tell, then build the trap around it.** Roach after Pacquiao KO'd Hatton: "Hatton pumps his hands before he throws a punch. He's a sucker for a right hook. That's what we worked on in camp." [S9]
- **Angles beat straight lines.** Roach/Neri plan vs Hatton: "when Hatton comes forward, Manny needs to throw that jab then move to the side." Roach: "It's all about footwork." [S9]
- **Patience is a weapon when ahead or longer.** Hopkins on losing to Kovalev: "When I tried to pull him into my fight he took a step back and started to jab." and "It was smart that he stayed patient." [S10]

## 2. Trigger cues (cue -> what the elite does -> known for it)

- **Opponent just threw (recovery window)** -> counter on his return, or step off and fire as his hand comes back. -> Mayweather pull counter, Canelo, Shakur. Mayweather leans in and drops the lead to bait, then pulls back and fires the right [S11, analyst description].
- **Opponent's feet set / planted to throw big** -> leave range or slip-and-counter; do not stand in front. -> Shakur, Bivol [unverified, analyst consensus].
- **Opponent's feet moving (mid-step, crossing, retreating straight back)** -> attack now; he cannot punch with power while stepping. Straight-back retreat is the classic moment for a pressure fighter to unload. -> Inoue, Crawford finishing [unverified].
- **Pre-punch tell (pumping hands, dipping, loading shoulder)** -> pre-empt or counter the specific punch. -> Pacquiao/Roach vs Hatton [S9].
- **Dropped hand after a punch / lazy jab return** -> counter over the top on that side (right hand over lazy jab, hook inside a wide right). -> Crawford, Inoue [unverified].
- **Square stance / feet in line** -> straight shots down the middle, he cannot pivot out cleanly. -> general coaching [unverified].
- **On the ropes or in a corner** -> pressure fighter commits to combos and body work; counter-puncher on ropes sets traps (shoulder roll, catch and return). -> Canelo, Porter attack; Mayweather defends off ropes [unverified].
- **Opponent resets / walks to center** -> pressure fighter cuts the angle with lateral steps before he gets there; boxer uses the reset to restart the jab. -> Reynoso's "cut off the ring" [S7].
- **Opponent leads with the same pattern twice** -> on the third time, counter the known punch. Lomachenko is described as not needing "many rounds to get a bead on what the fighter in front of him is thinking" [S12, writer's line].
- **Opponent hurt or tired (slow hands, holding)** -> step up volume, aim at body to keep him down, stay in front of his exits.
- **Southpaw vs orthodox lead-foot fight** -> whoever gets the lead foot outside gets the straight back hand line. McIntyre's plan vs Canelo: "take away the right hand, your left hand will travel a shorter distance than his right hand... your left hand goes straight down the pipe." and "keep turning him, keep turning him." [S13]
- **Opponent counters too eagerly** -> feint to draw his counter, then counter the counter. Drawing leads / baiting is the central tool of Locche and Benitez style counterpunchers [S11].

## 3. Disengage / reset rules

- **After landing a clean shot, leave.** Elite boxers exit on an angle after the last punch instead of admiring it. Kovalev's method vs Hopkins per Hopkins: "when he got hit with some of my shots he would step back." [S10]
- **When the other man gets inside and you are the tall one, tie up.** Steward to the Klitschkos: "If they called holding, Muhammad Ali never would have won a fight.. Short guys have the advantage on the inside so tie them up... The referee separates you and you're back outside." [S14, forum-reported, treat as unverified wording]
- **When hurt: hold, move, survive the round.** Corner wisdom; clinch until the head clears [unverified].
- **When on the ropes and the opponent is fresh: pivot out first, punch second.** Out-boxers reset to center rather than trade on the ropes [unverified].
- **When the distance is not "felt" yet: do not move forward.** Lomachenko on Teofimo: "I couldn't move forward at the very beginning. I couldn't feel the distance at the very beginning." [S12]
- **After taking a counter you did not expect: stop that lead.** Inoue: "in the second round it kind of got a little loose. And then from then thereafter I made sure I didn't take that punch again." [S15]
- **Half-step back to see more.** Steward's instruction for Klitschko: dominate behind the jab and take a half-step back until comfortable enough to open up [S4, paraphrase in source].

## 4. Reading phase (how long, what they look for)

- **Typical length: 1 to 4 rounds.** Crawford vs Canelo: "The first round went by, the second round went by ... and then I thought, 'I've got him.'" [S16] vs Porter he said he had Porter figured out in round 1 [S16, paraphrased]. Mayweather vs McGregor: team chose to let McGregor come out fast, then go after him after a few rounds; turned in round 4 [S17]. On Maidana I: "After the fourth, he lost every round." [S17] Usyk vs Joshua I: shifted plan from round 4 [S18, analyst].
- **What they collect:** his distance (how far his jab reaches), his favorite lead, his reaction to a feint (blinks, shells, counters?), which way he moves when pressured, how he resets, his tells before big shots, how he breathes after a combo.
- **How they collect it:** low output jabs and feints, touches to the body, lots of stance/angle changes (Crawford switch-hitting), giving him the lead on purpose.
- **Cost:** the reading fighter often drops early rounds. Crawford is widely called a slow starter for this reason [S16]. Elite readers accept that cost because the later rounds pay back with interest.
- **Exceptions:** Inoue and big punchers go early when the read is fast or the opponent is clearly outclassed; Shakur reads behind a high jab so he loses fewer early rounds [unverified].

## 5. Score / situation logic

- **Banking a lead:** when clearly ahead late, elite boxers drop risk: more jab, more movement, more clinch, fewer exchanges. Shakur's "If your best shot is to knock me out, you lost." [S6]
- **Needing a KO:** corners flip the instruction. Reynoso to Canelo before round 12 vs Bivol: "Está más pesado que tú, ya no lo puedes noquear" ("He's heavier than you, you can't knock him out anymore"), as reported from Matchroom footage [S19]. Read: even the corner knew the KO was gone; the plan had been to make the last rounds count.
- **Hopkins' late push:** BoxingScene headline from Hopkins: he wanted a knockdown in the 12th, not survival [S20, headline only].
- **Close fight late:** take the last 30 seconds of each round (judges' recency), finish rounds with a flurry [unverified, common corner advice].
- **Hurt early, ahead on cards:** survive, rebuild, the lead buys patience.
- **Behind and the opponent is fading:** pressure, body, cut the ring; the tired man cannot reset to center.

## 6. Engine translation (rules for decide() / react() / footwork())

Generic state assumed: `d` (distance), `myRange` (STYLES.range), `oppAfterPunch` (frames since opp's punch ended), `oppFeetSet`, `oppMoving` (+back, side), `oppSquare`, `onRopes(me/opp)`, `inCorner`, `oppHurt`, `meHurt`, `readLevel` (0..1 per opponent), `oppTell[punchType]`, `roundScoreDiff` (my cards minus his), `round`, `roundsLeft`, `roundTimeLeft`, `gas`.

**Reading phase (new: readLevel)**
```
readLevel grows each time opp throws in view: += 0.02 * ringIQ * (style.counter + 0.5)
reading = readLevel < 0.35 && round <= 3 && !oppHurt
if reading: decide weights -> jab x1.3, feint x1.5, combo x0.6, move x1.2 (lower output)
counter-punchers / Crawford-like: reading lasts longer (threshold 0.5); swarmers skip it (threshold 0.1)
```

**Trigger cues in decide()**
```
if oppAfterPunch < 0.35s && d < myRange + 15:          // recovery window
    combo/counter weight x (1.5 + style.counter)        // "make him pay"
if oppMoving == 'back' && !onRopes(opp) && d < myRange + 25:
    lunge-in combo weight x1.4 for cutK > 0.6 styles
if oppFeetSet && d < myRange && style.ret > 0.8:
    move/out weight x1.5                                 // don't stand in front of a set man
if onRopes(opp) || inCorner(opp):
    combo x(1 + style.aggr*0.5), body share +20%, clinch x0.5
if oppSquare: straight punches (jab, cross) weight x1.2
```

**react() using the read**
```
counterChance *= 1 + 0.6 * readLevel                     // the more you've seen it, the better you time it
if oppTell[type] seen >= 3 times: block/slip success +15% vs that punch, counter chance +25%
if opp counters often (oppCounterRate > 0.3): my feint weight x1.5, then counter-the-counter
```

**Disengage / reset (footwork + decide)**
```
after my combo lands: if style.ret > 0.7 -> exit on an angle (circ dir) instead of standing; else stay
if d < MIN_D + 10 && myReach > oppReach + 5 && style.clinch >= 0.8: clinch weight x2  // Steward rule
if meHurt: clinch x3, move x2, combo x0.3 until recovered
if onRopes(me) && !meHurt: pivot out first (circ toward open side), punch weight x0.5 for 0.6s
   except counter styles with high defense: stay and counter (shoulder-roll trap), counter x1.3
if taken clean counter on lead X twice this round: lead X weight x0.5 for rest of round  // Inoue rule
```

**Ring generalship (footwork)**
```
cutter (cutK > 0.6): when opp circles, step laterally to his circling side, not straight at him
boxer (circ > 0.6): drift back toward center when within 25 of ropes; reset after each exchange
```

**Score logic (decide, per round start and last 30s)**
```
if roundsLeft <= 2 && roundScoreDiff >= +2 rounds: risk down -> combo x0.7, clinch x1.3, jab x1.2
if roundsLeft <= 2 && roundScoreDiff <= -2: risk up -> combo x1.5, power share up, guard lower
if roundTimeLeft < 30s && roundClose: output x1.3 (steal the round)
if oppHurt && !meHurt: aggr x1.6, keep him off center (cutK x1.3)
```

**Corner tie-in:** shouts can map to these levers: "Make him pay" (counter window), "Cut the ring" (cutK), "Tie him up" (clinch), "Bank it" (lead protection), "Go get him" (KO mode).
