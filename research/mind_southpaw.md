# Southpaw edge: how much does it hurt a man who has never faced one?

Research for the Last Bell `know` (familiarity) knob. Date of pull: 2026-10-14.
WebFetch was blocked (DNS) in this session, so every figure comes from search snippets, not full text: all rows are `[snippet]` unless noted. Re-check exact numbers before quoting them as fact.

Short answer: the real-world southpaw edge is small. Our 52-53% at normal familiarity is at the top of what the data supports, and 43% for a fighter who has never seen one is bigger than any published win-rate number. No study measures "never faced a southpaw" directly, so the 43% has to be argued from the perception work, not from results.

## 1. Pro boxing

- Sorokowski, Sabiniewicz, Wacewicz (2014), Anthropological Review 77(3). Top 20 active male pros in each of 17 divisions, n=340, 25% southpaw. Stance did not predict overall win %. Matchup effect: both stances won more against orthodox opponents than against southpaws. Figure caption (ResearchGate, not the paper): victory ratio 0.89 (SD 0.11) vs orthodox, 0.85 (SD 0.21) vs southpaw. That is about a 4 point cost of facing a southpaw, for top-20 pros, whichever stance you use. [snippet]
  https://czasopisma.uni.lodz.pl/ar/article/view/11285 , figure: https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Own-stance-opponents-stance-and-the-percentage-of-wins-in-professional-boxers_fig1_286024810
- Richardson and Gilman (2019), Scientific Reports 9:15402. 13,800 pros (10,445 male boxers, 1,314 female boxers, 2,100 MMA). Southpaws overrepresented: 17.0% of male boxers, 12.5% of female boxers, 18.7% of MMA (population about 10%). Authors report robust evidence of greater fighting success for lefties, small effect, same for men and women. A search summary gave win rates of 52.4% (male boxers) and 53.5% (MMA); I could not confirm this in a second source, treat as (est.). [snippet]
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6817864/
- Share of top-20 pros who are southpaw (25%) is above the 17% of all pros, which is above about 10% of the population. Rises with rank. Selection bias warning: southpaws who reach the top have learned to win despite few lefty sparring partners.
- Gursoy (2009), BJSM 43:142: 22 Turkish amateur/semi-pro boxers, lefties had more wins (p<0.01). Tiny, one club, ignore except as history. [snippet] https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/43/2/142
- Recent data (2020-2026): none found for boxing win rates by stance matchup. No BoxRec-based study turned up.
- Unsourced web claims of a 5-20% southpaw win-rate edge trace to no study. Ignore.

## 2. MMA

- Pollet, Stulp, Groothuis (2013), Animal Behaviour 86:839. 205-210 UFC bouts (UFC 118-148), 245 fighters, 20.4% left-handed. Southpaw vs orthodox head to head: 34-34 (50%). [snippet via AgentMMA secondary] https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003347213003485
- Baker and Schorer (2013), PLoS ONE 8:e79793. 1,468 MMA fighters, 17.4% southpaw. Career win %: 64.0 southpaw vs 62.6 orthodox, not significant. Southpaws had more fights. [snippet] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3834302/
- Pollet and Riegman (2014), Frontiers in Psychology: UFC hall of famers, little sign of a handedness effect. [snippet] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4012197/
- Recent (2020-2026): no peer-reviewed matchup study found. Medium/betting posts only (e.g. one cites 53% southpaw, unsourced; another says southpaws had higher win ratios in 7 of 10 years, roughly 2009-2019). Treat as blog-grade. https://agentmma.com/mma-lab/ufc-southpaw-vs-orthodox-advantage

## 3. Other sports, frequency dependence

- Rarer lefties, bigger edge is the standard explanation (negative frequency dependence), tested mostly by share-of-top-ranks, not by head to head win %.
- Table tennis, men: top 200 about 24% left, top 50 about 30%, top 10 about 45% (summary of Loffing-style data, 2007-2016 rankings; population about 10%). [snippet]
- Fencing: top 200 2007-2016 was 16.9-22.8% left (women), 20.9-25.8% (men) in epee/foil; French men's foil top 4 about 50% (1979-1998). [snippet]
- Tennis: lefties about 15% of pros vs 11% of population (Fagan, Haugh, Cooper 2019, JQAS; their modeled match-win edge was not in the snippet). Break-point analysis: about 2 points per-point edge (Tennis Abstract, blog). Loffing et al. 2012 (PLoS ONE): lefty edge moderate and shrinking over time for men, nearly absent for women. [snippet]
- Recent, 2025: Royal Society Open Science 12(9):250303 (DOI 10.1098/rsos.250303), elite fencing and racket sports 2004-2023: lefty excess shows up in fencing and table tennis, rises with rank. For men it appears in epee, foil, table tennis; NOT in sabre, tennis, badminton (tennis and badminton: no tier overrepresented). So the edge is real only where the sport is fast, close-range, and lefties are rare. Boxing fits that profile. [snippet] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12457039/
- Recent, 2025-26: elite women's epee, 2017-2019 Worlds (PubMed 42283593): lefty win rate up to 80% in the early elimination rounds through the semis, 50% in finals (matching righties). Small sample; shows the edge vanishing at the very top. [snippet] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42283593/
- Recent, 2020: junior cricket (PMC7424046): left-hand batters had higher batting aggregates, small effect sizes, males only. [snippet]
- Recent, 2024: handball 7-meter study (PMC11555897) asks if lefties score more; result not in snippet. Not needed.

## 4. Experience and familiarity

- Loffing, Hagemann, Strauss, MacMahon (2015-16 and earlier): left-handed actions are harder to anticipate for everyone. Novice handball players trained on lefty throws improved on lefty throws only (Loffing et al. 2012), so the deficit is trainable and side-specific. [snippet] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3492260 and PMC4664728
- Volleyball (Loffing et al. 2015, Hum Mov Sci): lefty attacks harder to read for both skilled players and novices; the gap shows up at a different moment (skilled: 480 ms before contact, novices: 240 ms). Skilled players are better overall, but still lose accuracy vs lefties. [snippet] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25689236/
- Hagemann (2009), tennis: prediction error lower vs righties; the handedness gap was largest in EXPERT players. Not "experts are immune". Exposure helps, expertise by itself does not. [snippet]
- Combat sports expert-vs-novice meta-analysis (27 studies, about 2022, PMC9650920): large expert advantage in anticipation accuracy and reaction time, but nothing split by opponent handedness. Gives a size for "experience matters", not for southpaws. [snippet] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9650920/
- Debut or early-career pros or amateurs vs southpaws: NO data found at any year. The only supporting claim (a trainer blog: edge shrinks with experience) is unverified.

## 5. Does it fade within a fight?

- No study. Anecdotal only. A search for trainer quotes found none credible saying the edge fades after the first rounds. Found instead: general tactical explanation (orthodox jab crosses the body line, lead-foot battle decides angles) and fan-forum opinion that the southpaw edge persists. Tactics sites say lead-foot position is the whole fight (anecdotal): https://warriorpunch.com/southpaw-vs-orthodox/ , https://expertboxing.com/southpaw-guide-to-beating-orthodox-fighters
- Perception evidence implies fade is possible (side-specific training works in minutes of video, Loffing 2012) but it is not tested in a live exchange. Anything on in-fight fade is (est.).

## Take for the sim

- Normal familiarity: real-world edge looks like 2-4 points, not 5. Pro boxing victory ratio drops 4 points against southpaws for either stance (89 to 85), and Richardson/Gilman put southpaw win rate near 52-53 (est.). Our 52-53% is the top of the believable range. Aim for 51-53%.
- Never faced one: no result data. Perception work says the deficit is real and trainable but nothing supports a swing of 7+ points. Reasonable target for a fully unfamiliar equal pro is about 45-47% (est.), which means the southpaw wins 53-55%. Our 43% is steeper than anything published; I would soften it, not drop it, because "a first-timer" is exactly the case no study covers.
- Expertise alone does not erase it (Hagemann: experts still show the gap), exposure does. So tie the knob to count of southpaw fights seen, not to the level or rating. Do not make a veteran immune just for being a veteran.
- Fading mid-fight: no evidence either way. If you add it, keep it mild and slow (est.: unfamiliarity penalty decays roughly a third by round 6 of 12), and cut the penalty with experience gained in the fight, not with a timer. Perception training works fast in lab video, so a small decay is plausible.
- Selection caveat: published southpaw edges come from fighters who survived to the top. A random southpaw in the sim should have no built-in edge beyond the matchup effect.
