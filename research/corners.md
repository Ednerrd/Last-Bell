## Patterns

### Notes per break (27 breaks with words)
- 1 note: 17 (63%). 2 notes: 6 (22%). 3 notes: 3 (11%). 4: 1. Mean about 1.6. None above 4.
- The 3-4 note breaks were all Reynoso (a scolder) and Steward in a late panic. Both gave short items, not sentences.
- Biased sample: the quotable ones are short. Atlas ran about 12 sentences but only 2 instructions. A speech is not a plan.
- Amateur data in mind_coach says about 8 statements per round. Pro mic'd breaks: far fewer, because the first 20-30 s is breath and cut work.

### First words
- Fighter hurt, shaken or just knocked down (8 breaks): 6 open with state or calm ("Breathe", "How do you feel? Are you sure?", "Sit down, relax", "one round at a time", "keep that right up"). 2 open with a line about the damage ("it's nothing, it's a cut").
- Fighter fine (about 15 breaks): almost none open with calm. They open with the point: "You're blowing it", "Don't let him grow", "Don't just stand there".
- Late in the fight (R9+) the first words are the clock: "three rounds left", "last round", "six minutes to go".

### What kind of message (primary one per break)
- Motivation/urgency: 10 (37%). Damage/state/survival: 8 (30%). Tactics: 8 (30%). Score information: 1 (4%).
- The 8 damage/state breaks are all hurt, cut or blind men. The 10 motivation breaks are mostly R7 or later, or a fighter who had gone passive.
- Pure tactics happen early and in the middle (Davison, BoMac, Bivol's corner), when the card is not in doubt.

### What corners say to a hurt fighter (8 cases)
- Check first: a question ("how do you feel? are you sure?"), not a statement. Charles, Reynoso.
- Then one basic safe thing: hands up (Steward), control the distance (Charles), breathe (Steward), run (Dundee), one round at a time (McCracken).
- No new tactic, no "roll the dice" (Davison with Joshua is the case people hold up as the mistake).
- Cut or swollen eye: tell him it's nothing (Reynoso) or give a bad adjustment (Futch's stand upright). Both can fail.
- Hurt fighters need the corner to have a plan for the next break too. McCracken's plan was "see where he is" and he was stopped 4 rounds later.

### What corners say to a winner
- Ahead and fresh: keep it steady, one defensive reminder (Reynoso: "stop dropping your hands"), the clock. Roach: "six minutes to go."
- Ahead and the other man tired: finish (Banks, Davison/Itauma, Roach R12). This is where the fails sit: the finish call burned Itauma's tank, and Pacquiao-Bradley shows a winner who drifted.
- A winner who is told "you're up" and believes it gives rounds away (Fury, Pacquiao R11).

### What corners say to a loser late
- Say the score, once, with a lever: Dundee ("you're blowing it", so honestly behind), Diaz ("you need the last round"), BoMac ("it's close, I'm not sure"), Atlas ("you're lying to yourself").
- When the KO is not on, say so: Reynoso "ya no lo puedes noquear", then "two punches and move the waist". He asked for rounds, not a miracle.
- When the corner does not know the score, it says nothing (Kenny Porter: "round by round"). Porter said that was unusual for his dad.

### Reading the other man (what trainers say they saw)
- Tired: head down on the stool (Steward on Hearns), open mouth and stooped head (writer on Itauma, R6-R7), "he gassed out" (Hrgovic's plan was to wait for it).
- Hurt: "He's still hurt" (Banks), "This guy is finished" (Atlas), "Go look at his balance. He laughed, he still was shaky" (BoMac), glazy eyes (McCracken, about his own man).
- Bluffing: BoMac says Spence was more hurt than he let on; Crawford said it was a flash knockdown. Two experts, same moment, split. Even a fighter in the ring did not agree with the corner.
- Habits: "he throws the double jab only when you're close to the ropes", "his legs are too far apart, he reaches", "he's not as fast as you" (BoMac). Slow = counter, do not chase.
- Corners can see more than the fighter and still overread: Banks saw hurt, Wladimir saw a man whose hands still worked, Vitali's caution won the argument.

### When a corner stops it
- Documented stops: Futch (can't see, 14 rounds, said once and calmly), Breland (R7, head coach disagreed, fighter wanted to go on his shield), Kenny Porter (after the second knockdown of R10, his man a point down on two cards).
- Late or no stop: Garcia/Margarito (fighter vetoed, ~4 rounds late per Roach), Davison/Itauma (hesitated, feinted the towel, ref beat him), McCracken/Joshua (read the damage, sent him out).
- Banks: "A lot of fathers who are trainers either stop the fight too early or too late." The relation to the fighter changes the threshold.
- Triggers that show up: swollen shut eye plus can't see the punches; repeated knockdowns in one round; a fighter who is "lurching", can't stand still; a corner saying out loud "it's bad".
- Stops come at the break or by towel inside the round; the fighter's own "no" delays them, and the corner often caves.

### Voices
- One voice in most of the 26 corners above. Two or three voices showed up in Fury-Usyk I, Joshua-Klitschko, Wilder-Fury II and Tyson-Douglas. In each the side lost, or the louder voice was the cautious or wrong one.
- The tie-breaker was family or the loudest. Vitali (brother) beat Banks (head trainer).
- Calm stayed calm: Lomachenko corner "unflappable" at R5-R7 in Usyk-Fury I (DAZN).

### Corner score reads
- Fury corner believed ahead, was behind: lost a close fight.
- Roach scored 10-2 and told Pacquiao he had control: lost 2 of 3 cards.
- BoMac and Diaz said "it's close / you need this round": both won their fights.
- Honest, specific, "we may be behind" outperformed comfort in these cases.

### Does the fighter do it?
- Often only in part. Canelo: "I do what comes to me." Wladimir did not follow Banks. Ali ran (Dundee: one move) and it worked. Moorer fought (long speech) and it worked.
- Clear wins came from one lever + the right moment: Dundee 13th, Futch's no, BoMac's counter, Steward's breathing.

## For Last Bell

Numbers are starting values to tune in the sim, not trainer quotes. Evidence: S = a sourced case above, D = my design reading.

### (a) AI corner: between-round plan and live shouts

1. **Notes per break.** Default 1. Allow 2 when the fighter is clean and the card needs a change. 3 only for a "scolder" corner personality. Never 4. Weights from the sample: 1 note 60%, 2 notes 25%, 3 notes 12%, 4 3%. Follow-through drops ~15% per extra note past 2. [S]
2. **Order.** Break text = (state line if hurt/cut/gassed) then (one tactic) then (optional clock/score line). Hurt fighter: state line first. Fine fighter: tactic first. [S]
3. **Hurt fighter (shook or just down).** Skip the plan. Pick from: breathe, hands up, keep distance, one round at a time. Also a tiny chance (10%) the corner asks "how you feel?" and the fighter's answer feeds a stop check. Never "roll the dice" on a hurt man unless the cards demand it; if it does, flag it as a gamble (success lower, KO risk higher). [S: Charles, Steward, McCracken, Davison]
4. **Winning and fresh.** One defensive reminder plus the clock ("hands up, three rounds"). No tactic change. [S: Reynoso]
5. **Winning, other man tired.** Call "finish" only if the other man is flagged hurt AND own gas > ~35%. If own gas is low, call "stay on the jab, rest, make him come". The Itauma failure: finish call at own gas ~ empty. [S, D]
6. **Behind late (R9+).** Say the score as it is, one lever, one clock line. If the KO is not realistic (other man not hurt), say "win the round", not "knock him out". [S: Reynoso/Bivol, Diaz, BoMac, Atlas]
7. **Score misread.** The corner's card is the true card plus noise: ±1 round typical, ±2 sometimes. Bias toward optimism for a corner whose man has been dominant early (Fury-Usyk I). A misread "we're ahead" when behind -> the corner gives a bank-it call and the fighter coasts: a real engine hit (the fighter's own `pace` goes down). Honest corner gets a small plus. Never show the player the true card when his own corner misreads it. [S]
8. **Lying on purpose.** I found no confirmed boxing case of a corner telling a winner he's losing. Atlas's "you're lying to yourself" is a push, not a lie. If this is built, make it rare (e.g. 5% of breaks for an Atlas-type), small bonus (+3-5% work rate), and a cost: the fighter later trusts the corner a bit less. [D, flagged unconfirmed]
9. **Two or three voices (second voice).** Give some corners an assistant or family member. Chance 25% per break the second voice contradicts the first; if so follow-through x0.5 for the round and the loudest/closest relative wins the tie (Vitali over Banks). [S: Fury-Usyk I, Joshua-Klitschko]
10. **Stopping it (corner stop).** Per break: stop if (eye shut AND can't land for 2 rounds) or (2+ knockdowns this round and he's still down) or (visible defenseless: can't stand still, stumbling). Delay: a corner is on average ~1 round late. Family corner: x1.5 later; headstrong fighter (veto): stop chance x0.5; hurt but in the lead: x0.5 (Wilder, Margarito). A towel inside the round is the same logic, but fires the moment a second knockdown lands (Kenny Porter). [S: Futch, Breland, K. Porter, Garcia]
11. **Hesitation window.** A stop call can take up to 5-10 s of round time; a hit lands in that gap (Itauma). [S]
12. **Live shouts.** I found almost no verbatim in-round shouts; the mic'd material is mostly the break. Keep live shouts to reminders: 1-2 words about a thing already ordered ("hands!", "jab!", "work!", "move!"). Jay Bright's "Don't just stand there and look at him, you've gotta work!" is the long end. [S, D]
13. **Do not try something new.** An order the fighter has not drilled lowers follow-through. Davison's "roll the dice" was a move AJ had in his bag; unknown moves are worse. [D, coach_c rule 18]
14. **Cut/swelling.** Tell him it's nothing when it isn't (Reynoso). A bad cut order: "stay off the ropes, move". Don't prescribe "stand upright" (Futch, Manila). [S]

### (b) Corner-talk lines (example lines in a trainer's voice; mine, not quotes)

- Hurt: "Breathe. How you feel? Hands up. Move your head. One round at a time."

- Behind late: "It's close. You need this round. Step on it. Combos, not one shot."
- Behind, no KO: "You can't knock him out. Win the round. Two punches and move."

### (c) How the corner reads the other man

15. **Tired.** Sign list: head down on the stool or at the bell, open mouth, stooped head, output down >25% on his own round average. Corner says "he's gassed" after ~2 signs, and only a round after he actually is. [S: Steward, writer on Itauma, D for the numbers]
16. **Hurt.** Signs: wide legs, shaky balance on getting up, slow glove return, glazy eyes (late block, no slip), clinching. Two of three signs -> the corner says "he's hurt". The read lags one round. [S: BoMac, McCracken, Banks, D]
17. **Bluffing.** The corner can be wrong in either direction. Give the corner's read an accuracy by its level (Pro 75%, Elite 90%, Novice 50%) and a fixed 25% disagreement between corner and fighter after a knockdown (Crawford "flash", BoMac "done"). A hurt-bluff by the other man fools a low-level corner more. [S, D]
18. **Habits.** After 2-3 sightings the corner names a habit by round: "stepping in after the jab", "reaching, legs too wide", "slow feet". Next break gets a counter order. [S: BoMac]
19. **Overreading.** Banks saw hurt, the fighter saw a man still firing; Vitali's caution won. A corner that calls "he's hurt" should be wrong ~20% of the time at Pro. [S, D]
20. **Cut/eye on the other man.** The corner sees it and calls it ("work the cut", "go to the left eye"). Roach said Margarito's corner should have stopped it; in R11 Pacquiao himself turned to the ref asking for the stop (Gulf News live blog, search snippet [B]). Connect to the existing `cutmanLine()` and `target()`. [S]
