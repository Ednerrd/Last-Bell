# The Mind of a Boxer: combinations (which, when, why)

Numbers: 1 jab, 2 cross, 3 lead hook, 4 rear hook, 5 lead uppercut, 6 rear uppercut, b = body, `|` = a beat (touch or feint, then the real one). For a southpaw the 1 is the right jab and the 2 is the straight left.
Fighter DNA (stance, rhythm, knobs) lives in `fighters_a.md` / `fighters_b.md`. This file is about the combo grammar and the counter logic. "(unverified)" = analyst claim or common gym teaching I could not tie to a primary source. "(est.)" = my number.

---

## 1. Combo grammar (the rules every good combo follows)

1. **Alternate hands.** Each punch loads the next: the cross rotates the hips so the lead hook is already cocked, the hook rotates back so the cross is cocked. 1-2, 2-3, 3-2, 2-3-2 all flow. Same-hand doubles (1-1, 3-3, 3b-3) are the deliberate exception: they break the rhythm the opponent expects.
2. **Change levels.** Body then head, or head then body. A guard can't cover both. Teddy Atlas teaches a body jab first ("knock knock"), watch how he reacts and how long the reaction takes, then the right hand upstairs ([Dynamic Striking / Atlas](https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/start-low-and-end-high-with-creative-combinations-from-teddy-atlas)). Loma "targets one area, forcing the opponent to shift his guard, then switches" (same source).
3. **Change lines.** Straight down the middle, then a curved punch around the guard (1-2 then 3), or a hook to draw the gloves wide, then a straight or uppercut through the middle (3-2, 3-6).
4. **End on the lead hand (usually the 3).** The lead hook lets you snap back to stance fastest, so it is "regularly taught as a way to protect the boxer throwing" and it lands a lot of the knockouts because it comes after the opponent stops watching ([FightCamp](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/training/3-boxing-combos-inspired-by-champion-boxers/)). Ending on a rear hand leaves you square and in range unless you step or roll out.
5. **The first punch is the key, not the weapon.** The jab blinds, measures and makes him react. The power is the 2nd to 4th punch. Usyk "regularly tapped Joshua's gloves with throwaway shots to set up his more damaging attacks" ([Boxraw](https://boxraw.com/blogs/blog/usyk-vs-joshua-2-fight-breakdown)).
6. **Punch in pairs, flurry in bunches only with a reason.** Most exchanges are 1 to 3 punches (unverified gym norm). Long flurries (4 to 8) are for a hurt man, a man on the ropes, or a new angle where he can't punch back (Loma). Roach drilled Pacquiao in "bunches of four, five, six, followed by a heavy knockout punch" ([Scotsman via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/the-scotsman/20150429/282441347640446)).
7. **Combos get stereotyped as men get bigger.** A pro study (8 bouts per division, 2 to 4 punch combos, 9 punch types) found that just 12 combo types covered 43% of combos at bantamweight, 58% at middleweight and 64% at heavyweight. A lead hand landed more often inside a combo than alone (48.8% vs 45.4%) ([A temporal analysis of combinations in professional boxing](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dbv/Record/4027122?lng=en)). Lighter men = more variety.
8. **Accuracy beats volume.** In elite amateur bouts winners and losers threw about the same total, but winners landed 33% vs 23% and missed into air less (17% vs 27%) ([Dunn et al. 2017](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8w0yx/human-behaviours-associated-with-dominance-in-elite-amateur-boxing-bouts-a-comparison-of-winners-and-losers-under-the-ten-point-must-system)). Elite amateurs run ~20 punches a minute (Davis et al., [record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4034158)). Pro norm: ~50 to 60 thrown per round (fighters_a.md). I found no public CompuBox number for "share of 3+ punch combos" (unverified).

## 3. Counter library (his action -> the answer)

| Jab (you slip outside) | 2 over the top, or slip and 3 | You are outside his rear hand | Classic slip-counter ([Evolve](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/the-art-of-slipping-punches-in-boxing/)) |
| Cross (you slip inside/outside) | 3 to the head or 3b | Hook comes from the side you slipped to | Classic; engine already does this |
| Rushing in, head first | Check hook (3 while pivoting off the line), then 2 | He runs onto it and you are gone | Mayweather vs Hatton ([Wylie via TSS](https://tss.ib.tv/boxing/?p=17206)); Bam vs Sor Rungvisai |
| Ducking / coming in low | 6 or 5 up the middle | Head is coming down onto it | Canelo vs Smith and GGG ([ESPN](https://www.espn.in/boxing/story/_/id/17575307/canelo-alvarez-defeats-liam-smith-ninth-round-knockout-att-stadium)), Nakatani retreating uppercut |
| Lunging rear straight (southpaw vs southpaw, or closed stance) | Counter jab, then 2 | Jab is shorter and lines up with his left | Crawford vs Spence: counter jab "pivotal", planned by McIntyre ([Bloody Elbow via search](https://bloodyelbow.com/2023/07/30/terence-crawford-vs-spence-breakdown)) |
| Body jab, then drawing back | Chase with 2-1 | He is off balance going back | Crawford's first KD of Spence (same source) |
| Wide hook | 2 straight down the middle (inside line) | Straight beats curved: shorter path | Classic (unverified) |
| Throws to your body | Stiff 1, then 6 | He has to dip in to reach | Bam vs Sor Rungvisai ([Queensberry](https://queensberry-rules.com/?p=15984)) |

## 4. Southpaw vs orthodox playbook (open stance)

- **Win the lead foot battle.** Both want the lead foot outside. The man outside has the straight line for his rear hand and makes the other guy move toward that power ([ExpertBoxing](https://expertboxing.com/20-southpaw-punch-combos-and-counters); [Evolve University](https://evolve-university.com/blog/southpaw-guide-for-fighting-orthodox-boxers/)).
- **The 2 is the main weapon, the jab is a tool to tangle.** Jabs meet each other up top, so the rear straight down the middle is the scoring punch. Roach told Pacquiao the straight left had "the better angle" vs Mayweather (Interaksyon).
- **Jab to the body works better.** The head jab gets parried; the body jab is hard to see from that angle ([ExpertBoxing](https://expertboxing.com/20-southpaw-punch-combos-and-counters)). "Sneak in two lefts: one to the body, then right back to the head" (same).
- **The liver is in front.** In open stance the orthodox man's liver faces the southpaw's rear hand (left body straight / left uppercut to body). Bam's body KO of Estrada and Shakur's body left are this.
- **Lead hook comes from outside** (southpaw right hook catches the orthodox man as he steps to his left). Nakatani and Bam 3s.
- **Counter favourite:** slip outside his jab, straight 2 over it. Shakur's left as a "pull counter, split counter, lead straight, body shot" ([BoxingScene community](https://www.boxingscene.com/community/posts/696846), unverified forum).
- **Closed stance (same stance) is different:** the jab is king again and the counter jab wins (Crawford vs Spence).

## 5. Set-up chains (show it early, cash it late)

1. **Body early, head late.** Shakur: "by consistently landing his jab to the body, Stevenson laid the groundwork for the knockout left" ([BoxingScene](https://www.boxingscene.com/community/posts/696846), unverified forum). Inoue vs Fulton: body jab all fight, Fulton started dropping his stance to absorb it, then Inoue swept upstairs for the KO ([Bloody Elbow via search](https://bloodyelbow.com/2023/07/26/naoya-inoue-vs-stephen-fulton-breakdown)).
2. **Same opener, new ending.** Show 1-2 for rounds, he learns to block the 2. Then 1-2-3 (hook around the block) or 1|2 (the jab is a feint, the 2 lands into the parry).
3. **Teach a rhythm, then break it.** Usyk taps on a beat, then fires off-beat (fighters_a). Engine already has a `|` beat.
4. **Make him react, then hit the reaction.** Atlas: throw the body jab single first and time the reaction. Lomachenko keeps a feinting rear hand in front of the eye while stepping outside ([Vice](https://sports.vice.com/en/article/bmqmk8/the-pivots-and-precision-of-vasyl-lomachenko)).
5. **Bait the counter, then counter the counter.** Mayweather drops the lead hand and leans in to invite the jab, then pull-counters it (Evolve).
6. **Late in the fight the body work pays.** Canelo's body hook "thud" KO of Smith in 9 and Bam's of Estrada in 7 both came after rounds of body investment.

## 6. Engine translation (vs `const COMBOS`, index.html ~343)

**What the engine already does well.** 39 combos in trainer numbers, range tags, style weights, guard holes (`GUARD_HOLES`), open stance boost (`OPEN_W`), a learned read (`Rd`), "don't repeat the last combo", level swap after a block, hook after a slip, uppercut after a duck (`nextInCombo`). That matches rule 1 to 3 and the counter logic above.

Static menu (my calc, before range filter): mean length 1.9 (counter) to 2.2 (swarmer). Singles 16 to 32%. 3+ punch combos 16 to 34%. Combos with a body punch 27 to 40%. Feels right (est.).

**Gaps found**
- `pickCounter()` ignores WHAT he threw and HOW you avoided it. A pulled jab, a slipped cross and a ducked hook all give the same random menu.
- No "set-up memory": body punches landed early don't raise the value of head shots later (only `softBody` raises body weight when he is tired).
- No counter jab. No check hook with a pivot. No catch-and-shoot after a block (block counter uses the same `pickCounter`).
- No 4b (rear hook to the body) or 5b/6b (uppercut to the body). Needs new `PUNCH` types and art, so low priority.

**Weight changes per style** (est., sim before trusting)
- outboxer: `1-2-3-2` .4 -> .25; `1b` .9 -> 1.1 (the jab to the body is an outboxer weapon).
- counter: `1-2` 1.6 -> 1.3, put the difference into the counter table below (counter men win off counters, not leads).
- swarmer: `3b-3` 2.0 is high vs `1-2-3` 1.2; fine for pressure, but add `1-2-3b` +.3 so he still has a way in from mid range.
- slugger: `2-3` 2.0 ok. `4` .5 is the only rear hook: keep it rare (wide, slow).
- STYLE_COMBO `angle`: add `body: 1.3` (Loma ends angle attacks to the body). `pboxer`/`jabgrab`: add `1-1-1` boost through `jab` already.
